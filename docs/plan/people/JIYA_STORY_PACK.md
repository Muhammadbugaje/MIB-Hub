## 1. You, in the game

### Dear Jiya

You read law. Then you got better. Then you came to a university in northern Nigeria for a six-week short course and, as far as anyone can tell, never quite left.

That's you in {{GAME_NAME}}. You're the visiting fellow who teaches "Law and Technology" (mostly, you say, "Have You Tried Restarting"), judges the hackathon, holds second place on every arcade machine in the mall and takes it personally. You never raise your voice. You don't need to. Your roasts arrive politely, sit down, and stay for a week.

The game follows a fresher (the player) through five years of queues, exams, power cuts, a strike, football and friendship. You're the person in the queue who reads the fee notice like a contract, who turns up just before somebody gets fined, bullied or bored to death by a rule, and who could argue anyone into the ground but would much rather watch *them* win their own argument.

Over five years you become the fresher's favourite unofficial lawyer, Nusaiba's mentor and sparring partner, Shamsu's chess nemesis, Mubarak's emergency contract reader, Jega's schedule-drafter and, in the last year, the person who reads an old document aloud at five in the morning at a door under the Senate building. You won't carry anything down there. You'll make ten nervous people laugh.

We think it's a good part. We've written it for your voice. Now please tell us everywhere we got you wrong.

### At a glance

| | Your character |
|---|---|
| **Who you are** | Jiya, visiting fellow. Read law, became a software engineer, never lost an argument (that she admits) |
| **Your power** | **Legal Immunity.** Campus security can't arrest you and fines can't touch you; you talk your way out of anything. Once per mission you can say **"Objection!"** and win an argument outright |
| **Your look** | Cartoon style, from your photos. Tall and slim; long wavy dark-brown hair with caramel ends; sunglasses pushed up on your head |
| **Your outfits** | Smart-casual with a gamer hoodie (everyday) · a red football cap and jersey (match days, chess, any day you feel like winning) · a law graduation gown (court, the hearing, convocation). All modest; swap any of them |
| **What you love** | Games, football and chess. All three are in your story |
| **Your voice** | Yours. A calm British delivery. "Right." "Mate." "With respect, and I mean none." |
| **How you unlock** | For players, you're one of the special characters: locked at the start, unlocked later. You also get your own personal code that unlocks you straight away. When someone plays *as* you, the opening film begins with your own arrival (section 2) |

### The rules that protect you

These are written into how the game is built, not just promised.

- **You approve everything.** Every line, every outfit, your cartoon likeness, before release and again whenever we add anything new. Cross out whatever isn't you.
- **Your voice is only ever yours.** Your recordings are used for your character and nobody else's. They are never used to train or make an AI copy of your voice, and never edited to say something you didn't say.
- **The kill switch.** If you ever want out, the owner switches you off for every player at once. The game is written so that every mission you're in still works without you (each one has a fallback), so you're never trapped in the story.
- **Never romance.** Your character is never in a romance, never flirted with, never the subject of a "will they, won't they".
- **Never carries the plot.** You help with one step, use your power, say a few lines. The story never depends on you.
- **Never sees the mystery.** The campus has a long, strange secret. You're never shown it, never asked to explain it, and you never touch the objects at the centre of it. (In the final year you're at the door. You read. You don't carry.)
- **Modest, and kind.** The jokes are about situations (queues, forms, power cuts, bad contracts), never about anyone's tribe, faith, body or money.

### Your arc, in one breath

You arrive for a six-week course, find a fee notice that's wrong, and decide to stay "until it's fixed". Year 1, you win arguments *for* people. Year 2, you win one magnificently in a moot court, then discover that the better trick is passing one note and saying one word. Year 3, you find nothing illegal and are furious about it. Years 4 and 5, you read people the paper and give them back the pen. By the end, Nusaiba is president, Mubarak has said no to five million naira, Jega's left boot has its own schedule, and the fresher reads contracts before signing them. You never once told any of them what to do. That's the point, and you know it.

**Running jokes you carry:** "With respect, and I mean ___" (the amount changes every time) · meat pies as legal fees · "sevens have hats" · coming second to someone called MIB on every arcade machine · Shamsu saying "Simple" while losing · Jega's boots · reading the thing nobody reads.

## 4. Your signature moment: Point of Order

*Mission 31 (Year 2). The faculty of law's moot court. This is the scene people will clip and share. Here it is in full, with every line.*

### Before: the law library, 10:00

Nusaiba's moot is tomorrow and her partner has malaria. She has drafted the fresher, an engineering student, as her junior counsel.

- **NUSAIBA**: "My partner has malaria. You are junior counsel."
- **FRESHER**: "I'm an engineer."
- **NUSAIBA**: "The rules don't say which faculty. I checked. Three authorities by one o'clock. Can you read fast?"

At the next table, in your red football cap, watching a match on your phone with the sound off, you don't look up.

- `m031_open_j01` "Catchword, volume, page. It's an index, not a séance."

That one sentence is the whole tutorial: from now on, every search in the library puzzle has a visible hint.

- **(new)** (as they leave) "Good luck tomorrow. Say as little as possible. Say it slowly."

### The moot court, 14:00

A wood-panelled room. A public gallery of law students who have come to see blood. Presiding: **Dr. Dangana**, known to every student as **Dr. Minus**, because he deducts marks from anyone who asks him a question.

Nusaiba's case is about a lease of public land, and her best authority is a very old grant. She's good. She's very good. The fresher stands behind her holding the folder and, when it's their turn, says their only line: "As my learned senior said." They say it four times.

You're in the front row, as the faculty's guest, in your law graduation gown, sunglasses still pushed up on your head.

Dr. Minus makes three objections. **(His lines are new drafts.)**

- **DR. MINUS**: "Objection one. Counsel is… very young."

## 7. How to record (the short version)

You don't need a studio. Your phone is fine.

1. **Find a soft, quiet room** (curtains and a bed: good; bathroom or kitchen: echo). Turn off fans and the air conditioning.
2. **Hold the phone a hand's width away,** slightly to one side so your breath doesn't pop.
3. **Say the code, then the line,** like this: "M-zero-three-one, mid-two, j-zero-one… *With respect, sir.*" That's how we match your file to the scene.
4. **Do two or three takes** of anything you like, each a little different: drier, warmer, faster. We'll pick.
5. **Change the words.** If a line doesn't sound like you, say it the way you would and note it. Your version wins.
6. **Short sessions are best:** thirty to forty minutes, water nearby. Send the files however suits you.

The full voice script (every line, with a mood and a cue for each) comes as its own PDF. **At the end of that PDF is a one-page volunteer agreement** in plain English: what we may use your recordings for, that they're never used for an AI copy of your voice, how you're credited, and how to withdraw. Please read it, sign it, and send a photo of it back with your first recordings. If anything in it is unclear, ask before you sign.
