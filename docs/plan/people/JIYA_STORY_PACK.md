## 1. Dear Jiya

We are making a game about five years of university life in northern Nigeria, and we would love you to be in it: as yourself, with your own voice, your own humour and a superpower that could only be yours.

This pack shows you exactly what we have in mind: who you would be, every mission you would appear in, the lines we have drafted for you, and what we would ask of you. Nothing goes into the game until you have read it and said yes, and you can change or refuse any line.

## 2. What the game is (one page, no spoilers)

You arrive as a fresher at a big fictional federal university. On your first morning you chase a runaway goat through the food court; at sunset there is a note under your door that says somebody is watching.

Over five in-game years you go to lectures and sit exams, survive hostel life and "sapa" (being broke), cook garri when NEPA takes the light, play football, fall in and out of love, start hustles, and slowly uncover a mystery that runs under the whole campus: why the lights keep failing, who the quiet man in the old lab coat is, and why the clock tower has been stopped at 6:06 for forty years.

It is an open world with a real calendar: weekdays feel like school, weekends like freedom, exam weeks like panic. The tone is warm and very funny. Nobody dies, nothing is crude, and we laugh *with* people, never at them. The look is a fun, expressive cartoon style.

There is one story, the same for every player. But players can unlock **special characters**, real people who appear on campus as friends and can also be played. You would be one of the first.

## 3. Who you are in the game

**Jiya: the lawyer who became an engineer and never lost an argument.**

- **Your role on campus.** A visiting fellow who teaches the short course "Law and Technology", judges the hackathon, and is unbeatable in the arcade. You read the rule nobody else read.
- **Personality.** Calm, quick and very funny. You never raise your voice. The roast arrives politely and stays for a week. Underneath, you are on the student's side, and you enjoy it most when they get there themselves.
- **Your look (cartoon style, from your photos).** Tall and slim; long wavy dark-brown hair with caramel ends; sunglasses pushed up on your head. Three outfits to start: smart-casual with a gamer hoodie; a red football cap and jersey (match days); a law graduation gown (court scenes). All modest, all yours to approve or swap.
- **What you love.** Games, football and chess. All three are in your missions.
- **Your voice.** Yours. A calm British delivery. We write the lines; you say them your way.
- **Your superpower: Legal Immunity.** Campus security and fines cannot touch you. You talk your way out of any arrest; offences cost nothing. Once per mission you can say **"Objection!"** and win an argument outright.

## 4. How the player meets you

In the second mission of the game, the fresher is in a registration queue that curls round the Senate building. You are two places ahead with a coffee, reading the fee notice as if it were a contract. You find ₦300 of "sundry charges" that nobody has to pay, and you introduce yourself:

> "I'm Jiya. I did law. Then I got better."

From then on you turn up when someone is about to be fined, bullied or bored by a rule.

## 5. Your signature moment: the courtroom roast

**Mission 31, "Point of Order" (Year 2), the Faculty of Law's moot court.**
A student called Nusaiba is arguing a moot about a lease of public land in front of a lecturer nicknamed "Dr. Minus", who deducts marks when students ask questions. You are there as the faculty's guest, in your law gown, sunglasses still on your head. He makes three objections to her argument and asks, unwisely, whether the visiting fellow has anything to add.

You stand. In about ninety seconds, without raising your voice, you take the three objections apart in order, thank him for each, and sit down. The public gallery applauds. He deducts two marks from himself.

Draft lines (all changeable):
- "With respect, sir. And I'm choosing that word carefully."
- "Your first objection is that she's young. That isn't an objection. That's a birthday."
- "Your second is that the authority is old. So is the land, sir. That's rather the point."
- "Your third I've written down as 'tone'. I'll come back to it when I've found the statute on tone."
- "I've no further points. I had more, but they seemed unkind."
- (to Nusaiba, sitting down) "You were winning anyway. I just like the acoustics."

## 7. Your own missions, in full

These four are yours. The player can do them with you even before unlocking you.

**J1 · Terms and Conditions** (Year 2 onward; the Law steps, then the security post).
The fresher has been fined ₦2,000 for "loitering with intent to queue". You, passing with a coffee, read the campus by-laws on your phone in forty seconds and walk them to the security post. It plays as a dialogue duel: the player hands you the right clause at the right moment.
- "Right. Which regulation?"
- "With respect, officer, and I mean a great deal of it: that by-law was repealed in 1993. I can wait while you check."
- "You paid a fine without reading it? Mate. You're the reason terms and conditions exist."
*Reward for the player:* a free "Lawyer's Card"; fines are cheaper for good.

**J2 · Rage Quit** (Year 3 onward, weekends; the mall arcade).
You hold second place on every machine and take it personally. Best of five across three games. You commentate your own play like a cricket match and the player's like a wildlife documentary.
- "And here we see the fresher, approaching the joystick as one approaches a tax return."
- "That's not a bug, mate. That's your personality."
- (losing a round) "Objection. …Overruled. Fine. Well played. Again."
- (of the mysterious top score, "MIB") "Whoever that is, I'd like a word. And a rematch."
*Reward:* a gamer hoodie and an arcade perk.

**J3 · Objection!** (Year 4; the hackathon hall).
You judge the hackathon's ethics round and pick the player's team as the defendant. Honest answers get taken apart kindly; bluffs get taken apart slowly. Then you fix one line of their code without being asked.
- "I've read your privacy policy. It's a poem. It's a lovely poem. It is not a policy."
- "I did law so I could argue for a living, and software so I could be right for a living. Sit down, you're doing fine."
*Reward:* "Objection!" as a one-use item; your friendship.

**J4 · Checkmate, Professor** (Year 2 onward, weekends; the staff club veranda).
Shamsu, the campus genius, has never lost at chess here and says so, "technically". You play him in your red football cap while watching a match on your phone. The player is your second: tea, calling his moves, and two chess puzzles you set them between turns. You win in nineteen moves. He asks for a rematch. Twelve.
- "It's your move, Professor. It's been your move since kick-off."
- Shamsu: "Simple." You: "It isn't, mate. That's why you're losing."
- "Good game. Truly. Now explain to me, at length, what went wrong. I've got ninety minutes plus stoppage time."
*Reward:* a chess set for the player's room.

## 8. More sample lines (drafts; cross out anything that isn't you)

- "Right."
- "With respect, and I mean none."
- "Sorry, quick question. Is this a levy, a toll or a donation?"
- "So what we have here is a large man standing near money. Which is not a legal category."
- "I've read the terms and conditions. You haven't. Nobody has. That's my entire career."
- "He stopped for the goat. That's an emergency stop. Tick the box, there's a good man."
- "'Left, emotionally.' I'm having that. That's going in a contract."
- "I can't object to that thing. It hasn't said anything. Rude."
- "It says 'a guest'. It doesn't say 'a good one'. No offence, mate."
- When the player does well: "There you are. Told you. Well. I didn't tell you. I thought it, loudly."

If any of these sound like someone doing an impression of you, tell us and we will rewrite them with you.

## 9. Your unlock

You get a **personal code**. Enter it and you play the whole game as yourself: your look, your voice on the player's lines, your power, and your own reactions in shared scenes. The code is yours alone: it is checked on our server, it can be changed if it ever leaks, and nobody else can use it.

Later, only if you agree, other players could unlock your character through an event or a purchase, with a share of the revenue going to you. That is a separate decision and a separate agreement; saying yes to this pack does not commit you to it.

**Your voice is yours.** Your recordings are used only for your character. They are never used for any other character, never used to train or generate a synthetic voice, and never altered to say things you did not record.

## 10. What we would need from you

1. **Your yes** to the character as described, or your changes.
2. **Line approval.** We send your script (about 200–300 short player lines plus the scenes above); you mark what to keep, change or cut.
3. **Reference photos** for an "inspired-by" cartoon likeness: one clear front view, one side view, and a few outfits. The result is a stylised cartoon that people will recognise as you, not a photo copy. You approve it before anyone sees it. The photos stay private and are deleted on request.
4. **Voice recording, later.** On your phone is fine; we send a simple guide. Two or three short sessions.
5. **A short written agreement** (below), signed by both of us.

## 11. Consent and permissions: your rights, in plain words

- **You approve** your character's look, every outfit and every line before release, and again whenever we add new material.
- **You can change** anything at any time: a line, an outfit, the way a scene treats you.
- **You can withdraw.** If you ask, we switch your character off for all players (the game is built so this can be done at once) and remove it from future versions within an agreed time.
- **Nothing embarrassing.** Your character is never used in romance, never made the butt of a joke about who you are, and never placed in content you have not seen.
- **Where it appears:** in the game, and in its trailers and screenshots, only with your approval of each use.
- **Your data:** photos and raw recordings are kept private, out of the public game files, and deleted on request.
- **Money:** nothing is being sold at launch. If your character is ever sold, you receive an agreed share, in writing, first.
- **This page is a summary, not the contract.** The full agreement will say all of this precisely, and we would encourage you to read it as only you would.
