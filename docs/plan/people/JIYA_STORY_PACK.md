# Dear Jiya

I'm Muhammad. Everybody calls me M.I.

I'm making a video game, and I wrote you into it before I asked you. I know. Legally, that's a bold opening. Please read to the end before you object.

**Here is why I'm making it.**

University in northern Nigeria is the funniest, hardest, warmest five years a person can have, and nobody has ever made a game about it.

You arrive with one bag and your mother's instructions. You queue for four hours to get a form that lets you join another queue. Your roommate eats your food and becomes your brother. The power goes off in the middle of your exam revision, and two hundred students shout "Up NEPA!" at the sky as if the sky is listening. The lecturers go on strike for eight months and you learn who you are when nobody is making you get up. And at the end you sign each other's white shirts and cry like you weren't counting the days.

I wanted to keep all of it. The queues, the blackouts, the suya, the friends. So I'm building it into a game, mostly on an old laptop that is always out of storage, with my friends and family voicing the people in it.

**Here is why I'm writing to you.**

The campus needed one person who actually reads the notice on the wall. Someone who walks up to a man collecting an illegal "fresher tax" and asks, very politely, whether it is a levy, a toll or a donation. Someone who did law, then software, who plays chess with a football match on her phone, and who has never once raised her voice because she has never once needed to.

That's not a character I could invent. That's you. So she has your name, your red cap and your sense of humour, and I'd love her to have your voice.

It's unpaid. I'd rather say that now than on page twenty. It's a volunteer thing, built by friends, and you can walk away whenever you like. What I can promise is that every line is yours to change, and that your character is the one everybody is going to quote.

Read the story. Meet your character. If you get to the end and think "I want to be part of this", then I've done my job.

With respect, and I mean all of it,

**M.I.**

# What is this game?

**The short answer:** it's a game you play in your web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university.

**The honest answer:** please don't expect GTA 6.

There are no helicopters. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in the game is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back seat. The closest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

It's a browser game. You open a link and you're in. Cartoon characters, a small campus you can walk, cycle and drive around, and a great many people who want to talk to you.

Think of it as **a school-life simulator**. You play a brand-new student (we call them the Fresher) and you live the whole thing, first day to graduation day.

- **You register.** This takes four offices, four queues and four passport photographs. It is the tutorial. It is also a warning.
- **You go to lectures.** Sometimes the right ones.
- **You go broke.** There's a word for it, *sapa*, and it arrives in week three.
- **You eat.** Suya, jollof, and a cassava cereal called garri that every student swears they will never eat again after graduation.
- **You make friends.** A footballer, a genius, a hustler, a future president, a doctor, a roommate who steals your food.
- **You sit exams by torchlight** because the power has gone again.
- **You play football, drive a keke, run a stall, plan a heist with cereal cups, vote, graduate.**

The campus runs on a calendar. Lecture days are busy. Market day is chaos. During exams everybody looks haunted. During the strike the place is so quiet you can hear a goat thinking.

The university is made up. The town is made up. I haven't even settled on the names yet, so if you have a good one, I'm listening. The working title of the game is *Freshers School Life*.

And underneath all the jokes there's a mystery. That's next.

# The whole story

*Spoilers for everything. You're cast. You get to know.*

### The hook

It's the Fresher's first day. They chase a runaway goat through the food court. They catch it on a suya table, in front of everyone.

That night, a note slides under their door: **"The Maker is watching. Welcome."**

### The problem

The lights keep going off. Students blame the power company. But a quiet old man in a lab coat, who lives in the Engineering Garden and looks after one strange humming coil, calls it something else: **Entropy**. Everything runs down unless somebody puts energy in.

And in the dark, small grey shapes drift towards anyone sitting alone. They never hurt anybody. People are just found very still, cold and tired, and get better with food, light and friends. (It's that kind of game. Nobody dies. The cure for the monster is company.)

The old man is **the Maker**. Forty years ago, a professor and six engineering students tried to build a ring of six coils to keep the campus lit for good. It failed. Five students went home. He stayed, to keep the last coil alive. He never graduated. The clock tower stopped that evening at **6:06** and has not moved since.

### The five years

**Year 1: Arriving.** The Fresher finds friends, loses a football final, and discovers that when enough people stand together with torches, the dark backs off. On the last night, half the school marches down the boulevard with phones, torches and a generator in a wheelbarrow, and the first coil lights.

**Year 2: Money.** A shiny new mall opens. Every time the campus goes dark, the mall gets brighter. A rich man called Chief Megawatt is quietly drinking the school's electricity. The friends plan a heist on a bedroom floor using cereal cups as buildings. It works. Then there's a formal hearing, and somebody has told on them.

**Year 3: The strike.** The lecturers walk out "indefinitely". The campus empties. This is the year about who you are when nobody makes you get up. The roommate stops telling jokes and has to be gently pulled back. The footballer wrecks his knee. And thirty-one primary school children sing an old coil awake in the forest.

**Year 4: Fame.** A video of the Fresher goes viral. Then copies of people start appearing at night, doing what the real ones do, slightly wrong. There is a student election, a fake video, and a recount won by eleven votes.

**Year 5: Leaving.** Final-year projects. Job offers. The last coil. And a tall blank figure called Chancellor Null, who offers everyone the same thing: a letter that says "Stay". No endings. No goodbyes. Everyone here for ever. It sounds kind. It isn't.

### The twists

**The goat.** Somebody let it out on purpose. It was Shamsu, the class genius, who was giving it a lecture about littering at the time.

**The note.** The Maker never wrote it. An old hostel porter has slipped one note under one fresher's door every year for forty years. His instruction was: pick the one who runs *towards* the trouble. He picked the one who chased the goat.

**The snitch.** In Year 2 somebody tells the authorities about the Fresher. Everyone suspects everyone. A year later the truth comes out, and it's Shamsu again. A lecturer asked him straight, and Shamsu cannot lie. "I have tried. I practised in a mirror." He has felt sick about it for a year. There's no villain in it, which is why it hurts.

**The padlocks.** During the strike, somebody padlocks the lecture halls so they look abandoned. The padlocks were bought from Mubarak, the campus hustler, who had no idea what they were for. He finds his own receipt.

**The big one.** The ring never failed because of bad engineering. It failed because six engineers tried to build it alone. The professor's real plan needed all ten faculties: ten brass seals, one given to each dean, to be added "when your people are ready". Law. Medicine. Arts. Agriculture. All of them. Nobody ever knocked on a single door.

So the whole game turns into this: go and knock on doors.

**The letter.** Chancellor Null's letter says "Stay" in the Fresher's own handwriting. They tear it up. A second, much older letter falls out. The same word. The Maker's handwriting. Forty years ago, he said yes.

### The ending

Ten friends from ten faculties carry ten seals down a tunnel at five in the morning. The door opens. The Fresher rings a bell. The ring closes, and the clock tower moves off 6:06 and strikes for the first time in forty years. The whole campus hears it.

The Maker walks out of the garden, a free man, and gets his degree forty years late.

And in the very last scene, a polite young stranger steps off a bus at the gate and asks the way to the Senate building. He has turned up in every year of the game, always exactly when he was needed, and almost nobody else can see him. Who he is, I'm not telling. Not even you. Players are going to argue about him for years, and I want you arguing too.

**What the game is really saying,** and it never says it out loud: light is something people do together.

# Meet Jiya

**Read law. Got better. Came for six weeks. Still here.**

Jiya arrives at the university as a visiting fellow, to teach a short course called "Law and Technology". (She says it's mostly "Have You Tried Restarting".) The course lasts six weeks.

On day two she finds a mistake in the fee notice. She decides to stay until it's fixed.

That was five years ago.

### How she talks

Calm. British. Unhurried. She has never raised her voice in her life, because she has never needed to.

Her roasts don't hit you. They arrive politely, take a seat, and stay for a week.

Her favourite words:

- **"Right."** The sound of her deciding to get involved.
- **"Mate."** For friends, freshers and people about to lose an argument.
- **"With respect, and I mean..."** Then an amount. The amount changes every time. To a nervous security guard it's "a great deal". To a rich man with a nasty contract it's "some". To the Fresher, on a bad day, it's "none".
- **"Objection."** Used rarely. Works every time.
- **"That's not a bug, mate, that's your personality."**
- **"I've read the terms and conditions. You haven't."**

### What makes her funny

She treats everything on campus like a legal document, because nobody else will.

A fine for walking on a path? "That's what paths are for." Two students given the same bed? "That's not an allocation, that's a custody dispute." A one-page contract with nothing on the back? "As contracts go, it's a haiku."

She's never cruel. The joke is always the rule, the form, the queue, the contract. Never the person. She will dismantle your argument and then check whether you've eaten.

### Her three loves

**Games.** She is the second-best player on every machine in the mall arcade. Second. On every single one. First place always belongs to three letters: **MIB**. She has never met MIB. It keeps her awake.

(Between us: that's me. I wrote myself into my own game as the one person you can't beat. I'm sorry. I'm not sorry.)

**Football.** A red cap comes out on match days, and with it the only version of Jiya that shouts. "That was offside! I have a law degree and working eyes!"

**Chess.** She plays on the staff club veranda while watching football on her phone, and wins anyway. During the strike she teaches the hostel porters to play. They get good. They start beating her. "I've made monsters."

### Her quirks

- She initials every page of everything, including a T-shirt.
- She accepts legal fees in meat pies.
- She is from London and it is forty degrees. "I'm a London product. Help."
- She believes handwritten sevens should have a little hat, and she will die on that hill.
- She makes you say what you want in one sentence, without the word "vibes".
- She says "I was never here" and then turns up exactly when it matters.
- She keeps coming second to MIB and has decided that is rude.

### Her people

**The Fresher.** Her favourite defendant. Her "most promising disaster". She meets them in a queue, saves them from a fine, and never really stops.

**Nusaiba.** The law student who will end up student president. Clever, brave, always holding a folder. Jiya sees herself at nineteen and refuses to go easy on her. Together they are, in the words of the script, "a small terrifying committee".

**Shamsu.** The class genius. Starts every answer with "Simple." It never is. He has never lost a chess game on campus, until Jiya, in nineteen moves, then twelve.

**Mubarak.** The hustler. Signs things without reading them. Texts her "legal emergency, premium". She has told him he's not a crook: "You're an optimist with a stall. It's treatable."

**Jega.** The footballer, who must put his left boot on first or lose. In Year 1 she watches him put the right one on first, and lose. She does not forget it.

**Dr. Minus.** A law lecturer who takes marks off for asking questions. Her favourite opponent. You'll see why.

### Her arc

This is the part I'm proudest of.

**In Year 1 she wins arguments for people.** She swoops in, the fine vanishes, the guard walks away confused. It's brilliant to watch.

**In Year 2 she wins the biggest one of all,** in a courtroom, in her gown, in ninety seconds. And then, quietly, she starts doing something harder. At a hearing a few months later she passes one note and says one word, and lets Nusaiba win it herself.

**In Year 3 she finds nothing illegal,** and it annoys her for months. Her whole contribution to an angry petition is one deleted adjective. "Facts are ruder."

**In Years 4 and 5 she stops telling anyone what to do.** She reads people the contract, slowly, and hands back the pen. "I'm not telling you what to do, mate. I'm telling you what it says. Different job."

By the end, a hustler has turned down five million naira, a footballer has his lucky boot written into a contract, a law student is president, and the Fresher reads things before signing them.

She never told any of them what to choose. That's the whole point of her, and she knows it.

Then, on the last night, at five in the morning, at a door under the campus, somebody needs an old legal document read aloud. Five years of reading things nobody reads turn out to have been a rehearsal.

She came for six weeks. Her line in the final year is: "I came for a short course. Five years of you lot. Look at me."

### Her superpower

Players can unlock Jiya and play the whole game *as* her. She comes with a power: **Legal Immunity.** Campus security cannot arrest her and fines slide off her. And once per mission she can say **"Objection!"** and win an argument outright.

### How she looks

A cartoon version of you, and you approve it before anyone sees it. Tall and slim, long wavy dark hair with caramel ends, sunglasses pushed up on her head. Three outfits: a gamer hoodie for every day, the red cap and jersey for match days, and a law gown for court.

### My promises to you

- **You approve everything.** Every line, every outfit, the cartoon face. Cross out anything that isn't you.
- **Your voice is only ever Jiya's.** Never used for another character, never used to make an AI copy.
- **Never a romance.** Nobody flirts with your character and she flirts with nobody. She is seeing a chessboard.
- **An off switch.** If you ever change your mind, I can remove your character for every player at once. Every mission is built to still work without her.

# Your missions

You are in **35 story missions** across the five years, plus an entrance and **four missions of your own**. Some are big scenes. Some are one perfect line shouted out of a taxi window.

Here is each one: what happens, and what changes because you are there.

## Your entrance

A taxi from the airport. You are in the back on a work call, approving somebody's code with one thumb, while the driver argues with his own map. You settle it, with respect.

You step out at the university gate in sunglasses and the red cap, look up at the clock tower stuck at 6:06, and say: "Right. That's going to bother me."

It bothers you for five years.

## Year 1: the Fresher who paid without reading

### 1. Queue Theory

*Year 1, registration week.*

**What happens.** Registration. Four offices, four queues, one file. The queue curls round the Senate building like a question mark.

**What you do.** You're two places ahead with a coffee, reading the fee notice like a contract. If the Fresher got fined for taking a shortcut, you walk them back to the security man and the fine disappears. If not, you cross 300 naira of "sundry charges" off their bill. Either way, this is where you meet.

### 2. First Lecture, Wrong Theatre

*Year 1, first semester.*

**What happens.** The Fresher sits through ten confident minutes of the wrong lecture (Law, not Engineering) and gets asked to define a tort.

**What you do.** You're the guest speaker at the back, hearing a first-year call a tort "a kind of cake". One line as the Fresher flees.

### 3. Fresher Tax

*Year 1, first semester.*

**What happens.** Big Sly, an enormous student with a small heart, charges every fresher 200 naira to cross "his" basketball court. He gives hand-drawn receipts.

**What you do.** You stand up from the bench and ask him, with real professional interest, whether this is a levy, a toll or a donation. The tax drops to zero. Big Sly is not sure what happened. Neither is the guard you send away.

### 4. Test Week, One Lamp

*Year 1, first semester.*

**What happens.** The first tests, revised for under a single lamp because the power is out.

**What you do.** You're invigilating. You can see the Fresher finishing that sentence.

### 5. Keke Grand Prix

*Year 1, second semester.*

**What happens.** The Fresher's driving test, in a three-wheeled keke taxi. In the back: Lula and Faty, the funniest married couple on campus, arguing about directions. ("Turn left." "He means right." "I mean left, *emotionally*.")

**What you do.** You're squashed between them. You only wanted a lift. When the examiner tries to fail the Fresher for "carrying an unauthorised debate", you lean out of the window at eight miles an hour and argue the pass. The licence fee is waived.

### 6. The Vice-Chancellor's Cup

*Year 1, second semester.*

**What happens.** The big football final. The Fresher plays as a guest on Jega's team. It goes to penalties. Jega, showing off for a child in the crowd, puts his right boot on first. He hits the bar. They lose, and he shakes every single hand.

**What you do.** You and Nusaiba found the obscure rule that lets the Fresher play at all, and you shut the referee down at kick-off. Then you watch the boots go on in the wrong order and can do nothing. Remember this. It comes back in four years.

### 7. Everybody

*Year 1, second semester.*

**What happens.** To light the first coil, the Fresher needs people standing round it on convocation night. So they go and ask everyone they've met all year.

**What you do.** You are the hardest yes. You make them explain it in one sentence. You offer to draft a waiver. You say yes anyway, "mostly to see what it is".

### 8. Exam Fever

*Year 1, exam weeks.*

**What happens.** Exams, and a night-time emergency at the power substation. A security patrol catches the Fresher somewhere they shouldn't be.

**What you do.** You happen to be walking past. "They're with me. I'm conducting a site visit." The patrol leaves. You don't ask.

### 9. Convocation Night

*Year 1, the last night.*

**What happens.** The Year 1 finale. Every light on campus goes out. The dark stands up on the Senate steps wearing a cape. The whole school comes down the boulevard with torches.

**What you do.** You promised to come, and you do. You hold the garden gate against a line of security men by reading them their own events programme. Then you see what's in the garden, and for once you have no objection.

## Year 2: a small terrifying committee

### 10. Stalite

*Year 2, the first day back.*

**What happens.** A "stalite" is what a fresher becomes after a year. Stale. The Fresher comes back to find a new first-year asleep in their bed, with his name on it in chalk.

**What you do.** You read the hall rules through the porter's window, and the room is sorted out in one visit. That has never happened before in the history of the university.

### 11. The Mall Never Blinks

*Year 2, first semester.*

**What happens.** A week of blackouts. From the hostel roof the Fresher sees the whole campus dark and the new mall blazing like a ship.

**What you do.** One text message, which makes it clear you've noticed too.

### 12. Meter Reader

*Year 2, first semester.*

**What happens.** The Fresher is following a suspicious van at night, on a bicycle with no light. A mall guard catches them lurking.

**What you do.** You're leaving the arcade, furious, having come second on three machines. You get rid of the guard in four sentences. One of them involves bird-watching.

### 13. Point of Order

*Year 2, first semester.* **This is your big scene.**

**What happens.** Nusaiba has a moot court, a practice trial. Her partner has malaria, so the Fresher is her junior, and has one line: "As my learned senior said." They say it four times. The judge is Dr. Minus. He makes three objections to Nusaiba: she is young, her legal source is old, and he dislikes her tone.

Then he makes his mistake. He turns to the gallery and asks whether "our visiting fellow has anything to add".

**What you do.** You stand up. Law gown. Sunglasses pushed up on your head. And you take his three objections apart, in order, in ninety seconds, without raising your voice.

"One: she's young. That isn't an objection. It's a birthday."

The gallery applauds. Dr. Minus takes two marks off *himself*. You sit down and whisper to Nusaiba that she was winning anyway; you just like the acoustics.

This is the scene people will clip and share.

### 14. Faculty Week: Engineering

*Year 2, first semester.*

**What happens.** A quiz, a football match, an exhibition, and Shamsu's first robot, which serves a drink directly onto the Dean.

**What you do.** You're the quiz master in your gamer hoodie. A wrong answer costs a point and a remark. A right one earns "Good." Then you tell them not to frame it.

### 15. Test Week Twist

*Year 2, first semester.*

**What happens.** Somebody has told a lecturer where the Fresher was at night. They get an official warning, and everyone starts suspecting everyone.

**What you do.** You find them on the stairs, read the letter, and get the tripled fines struck out. You never ask what they did. And you tell them, gently, that finding out who told is their job, not yours.

### 16. On Air

*Year 2, first semester.*

**What happens.** The campus radio wants to hint, live on air, that somebody is stealing the school's light. As a riddle. At midnight.

**What you do.** You read the script first and cross out two words, which is the difference between a riddle and a lawsuit. Your fee is one meat pie.

### 17. Change-Over

*Year 2, second semester.*

**What happens.** The Fresher catches the man who's been switching the power. He did it for his daughter's school fees. Report him tonight, or let him own up in the morning?

**What you do.** On the phone, you explain exactly what each choice means. Then you refuse, kindly, to choose. "It's your call, mate. I only do the footnotes." This is the moment your character becomes who she is.

### 18. The Plan

*Year 2, second semester.*

**What happens.** A heist is being planned on a bedroom floor, with garri cups for buildings.

**What you do.** You walk in "by accident" and stop them before they tell you anything. Then you answer some purely hypothetical questions about what is and isn't trespass. You were never there.

### 19. Mubarak's Crash

*Year 2, second semester.*

**What happens.** Mubarak's cheap power banks start swelling. Forty customers want refunds. He disappears to the bus park at midnight with a bag.

**What you do.** You read the one-page contract that sank him and find the single useful sentence in it. He gets 20,000 naira back that week. You also tell him he isn't a crook, which he needed to hear more than the money.

### 20. The Hearing

*Year 2, second semester.*

**What happens.** A formal Senate hearing about the heist. Nusaiba speaks for the Fresher. Chief Megawatt has brought a real lawyer.

**What you do.** You sit right behind Nusaiba and do almost nothing. One note. Then, when the lawyer calls the students burglars, one word: "Objection." It's upheld. The word is struck from the record. You're pleased with yourself for a week.

### 21. Two Papers, One Day

*Year 2, exam week.*

**What happens.** Shamsu's robotics contest lands on the same morning as an exam. The robot crosses campus strapped to a motorbike, saying "Simple" at every bump.

**What you do.** You're a judge. You give the robot its only perfect mark, for the salute.

### 22. Sign-Out, Small

*Year 2, after exams.*

**What happens.** The graduating students sign each other's white shirts.

**What you do.** You sign one in tiny perfect handwriting, with a legal disclaimer.

## Year 3: the strike

### 23. Indefinite

*Year 3, the first day of the strike.*

**What happens.** The lecturers strike. The student congress is pure shouting. Then the goodbyes at the bus park, one friend at a time.

**What you do.** Your own course has just been cancelled. You go to congress anyway, read out the rule that says a written motion beats shouting, and Nusaiba's motion passes while nobody is paying attention. At the bus park you tell the Fresher you're staying. The arcade doesn't strike.

Through the strike you run a chess club on the veranda. Four o'clock. Bring tea. Bring hope.

### 24. Padlock Receipts

*Year 3, when lectures resume.*

**What happens.** Nusaiba finds out who padlocked the halls, and why. She is furious and wants to write a petition that says so.

**What you do.** You read all the paperwork in the time it takes to drink a coffee and discover, to your disgust, that nothing illegal happened. Your one edit to her petition is deleting the word "disgraceful". It's the best edit in it. And when she decides to run for president, you say: about time.

## Year 4: the side of the paragraph

### 25. Manifesto

*Year 4, first semester.*

**What happens.** Nusaiba runs for student president on a budget of 4,000 naira. Her rival, Muktar, has a banner the size of a building and a sound system.

**What you do.** You ask whether anyone has read the election rules. Nobody has. You do. Posters have a maximum size. Muktar's banner comes down. You insist you are not on Nusaiba's side, only on the side of the paragraph.

### 26. Proof of Work

*Year 4, the hackathon weekend.*

**What happens.** A 48-hour hackathon. The Fresher's team builds a tool to prove a viral video is fake.

**What you do.** You judge the ethics round and choose the Fresher's team as your defendant. You cross-examine their app. Their privacy policy is "a lovely poem". Then, without being asked, you fix one line of their code.

### 27. Election Day

*Year 4, second semester.*

**What happens.** Polling day ends in a recount.

**What you do.** Both candidates ask you to observe, because neither could find a rule against it. You watch every ballot. The whole election ends up turning on one badly written seven, and your ruling on it. Nusaiba wins by eleven. You shake her hand very firmly, which for you is a hug.

## Year 5: read it, then hand back the pen

### 28. The Missing Script

*Year 5, first semester.*

**What happens.** Nusaiba's exam paper vanishes and she's marked "absent". She is president now. She could make one phone call. She won't.

**What you do.** You won't let her either. "You're the president, not a cheat code." You draft the complaint with her, step by step, the slow way. She gets her mark, 81, and immediately argues it up to 83. You get something in your eye. Dust.

### 29. Megawatt's Offer

*Year 5, first semester.*

**What happens.** Chief Megawatt offers Mubarak five million naira for his shop.

**What you do.** Mubarak texts you: "legal emergency, premium". You come up from the arcade and read him Clause 9, slowly. You tell the Chief his contract is beautifully drafted, "like a mousetrap". And you give Mubarak no advice at all. He says no by himself.

### 30. Contract

*Year 5, second semester.*

**What happens.** A professional academy wants Jega to sign today and leave before his final exams.

**What you do.** You read him the contract on the office step. Then Jega demands one condition, and he is completely serious: left boot first, in writing. So you draft it. "Schedule A: The Player shall don the left boot first." Four years after you watched him put on the wrong boot, the boot gets its clause.

### 31. The Ten Seals

*Year 5, five o'clock in the morning.*

**What happens.** The last night. Ten friends, one from each faculty, carry ten seals down a tunnel under the Senate to an old door.

**What you do.** You carry nothing. You're not on the list; you checked. Your job is to keep ten terrified people laughing in a tunnel with no fire exits. And at the door, when an old legal document must be read aloud, you read it. Every word. With enormous enjoyment.

"A right of way, in perpetuity, for the carrying of light."

### 32. Chancellor Null

*Year 5, the same night.*

**What happens.** In a blank room, the Fresher wins their friends back by calling their names.

**What you do.** You answer. Legally.

### 33. Gown, 34. Convocation, 35. Sign-Out

*Year 5, the end.*

**What happens.** Photographs. Graduation. White shirts and marker pens.

**What you do.** One line in each. You pose for a photo, having checked for a waiver. You watch from the stand in your gown, almost proud. And you sign the Fresher's shirt, and initial every page.

## Your own four missions

These unlock as the player gets to know you. You are the star of each.

**Terms and Conditions.** The Fresher is fined 2,000 naira for "loitering with intent to queue". You read the entire campus rulebook on your phone in forty seconds, march them to the security post, and point out that the rule was repealed in 1993.

**Rage Quit.** The arcade. Best of five, you against the player. You commentate your own play like a cricket match and theirs like a nature documentary. If they win a round, you are delighted and furious.

**Objection!** The hackathon ethics round, extended. The player's app on trial, you as judge.

**Checkmate, Professor.** Shamsu has never lost at chess and says so, "technically". You beat him in nineteen moves while watching football on your phone. He asks for a rematch. Twelve.

# Scripts

Nothing below but your lines.

### Quick guide

**What you've got.** **390 lines**, about **3,980 words**. That's 146 story lines and 244 common lines. Read straight through, it's about **45 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few missions a day is perfect.

**Change any word you like.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound like you, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Jiya talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use the voice recorder app, on its best quality setting. Put it in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Under a blanket works too. Big empty rooms echo.
- **Switch off** fans, air conditioning and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Recording

- **One recording per mission.** Start recording, read that mission's lines in order, stop. Then the next one.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut.
- **No need to say the line numbers.** Just read them in order. If you skip one, say its number before you read it.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **The small orange words above each line are how to say it,** and often who you are talking to. Do not read them out.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**
- **Shouty football lines last,** and drink water.
