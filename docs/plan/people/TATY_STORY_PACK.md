# Oi, Taty!

I put you in a video game. I probably should have opened with that.

And not as a face in the crowd. You are in **35 missions**. You have a superpower. You have a cat called Pixel who is, legally speaking, a thief. In Year 2 she steals a footballer's lucky sock in front of a full stadium and you chase her across the pitch while two teams and a referee stand there watching. The crowd chants her name. Not yours. Hers.

So. Hello. I'm Muhammad. Everybody calls me M.I. This little book is me explaining myself.

**Why I'm making this game.**

Because university is the strangest five years a person can live through, and nobody makes games about it.

You arrive with one bag and your mother's instructions. You queue for four hours to be told the network is down, come back tomorrow. The electricity goes off the night before your exam. Every time. As if it has a copy of the timetable. Your lecturers go on strike and nobody can tell you if it's for two weeks or eight months. You eat garri (cassava flakes soaked in cold water) for dinner and call it a lifestyle.

And somewhere in the middle of all that, without noticing, you find your people. The roommate who talks in his sleep. The friend who reads your notes back to you at 2 a.m. by torchlight. The woman who sells you food on credit and never mentions it again.

Games are about soldiers, wizards and people who steal cars. I wanted one about *that*. About freshers and roommates and blackouts and exams, and the friends who carry you through them. It's my love letter to campus life, written by someone who still misses it.

**Why you're in it.**

Every campus has one person who is awake before everyone, cheerful about it, and somehow already finished with a ten-kilometre run while you are still negotiating with your blanket. My campus didn't have a Brazilian one with a cat. I felt that was a mistake, and I've corrected it.

The Taty in the game is built from the real one: the games, the cats, the concert energy. Then I turned every dial up until she became a cartoon. She never gets tired. She teases you while she laps you. She hugs you after she beats you. And every so often, when she's excited or losing or looking at a kitten, a little Portuguese falls out of her.

**What I'm asking.**

Read this. Laugh, I hope. Then record your lines on your phone, in a cupboard, whenever you feel like it.

Change anything. Every word in here is a draft. If a line doesn't sound like you, say it your way and yours wins. Especially the Portuguese. I did my best, and my best is a man who has never been to Brazil guessing. You're the expert. Fix it, swap it, add more.

There's no money in this. No studio, no budget. It's me, a laptop, and more stubbornness than is healthy. All I can offer is your name in the credits, a cartoon version of you that never gets tired, and my real gratitude.

I hope you read this and think: *okay, I want to be part of that.*

Bora!

**M.I.**

## What is this game?

It's a browser game. You click a link and it opens in a tab, next to your email and the eleven other tabs you've been meaning to close. No console. No download. No sixty-dollar price tag.

So please, before we go any further, adjust your expectations.

This is not GTA 5. It is definitely not GTA 6. If GTA is a Hollywood blockbuster, this is a school play with a lot of heart and one very ambitious goat.

- In GTA you steal a sports car. Here you borrow a bicycle, and somebody's mother finds out.
- In GTA the police chase you with helicopters. Here a security officer fines you for "loitering with intent to queue".
- In GTA there are explosions. Here the scariest thing that can happen is your father phoning to discuss your results. "I am not angry. I am only calculating."
- The fastest vehicle is a keke (a three-wheeled taxi). The most wanted criminal on campus has four legs and eats socks.

**What it actually is:** a school-life simulator. A cartoon, in 3D. You play a fresher at a made-up university in a made-up town in northern Nigeria, and you live all five years, from the first confused morning to graduation day.

You go to lectures (and the wrong lecture theatre, once; everybody does). You queue. You cook on a hotplate. You sit exams that actually count. You play football, swim in the dam, ride a motorbike taxi, get a holiday job, go to the gym and slowly stop being made of garri. You make friends, and five years later they sign your white shirt and you pretend you're not crying.

Nobody dies in this game. Nobody is even hurt. If you get knocked down, you sit, shake your head and get up again.

Oh, and there's a mystery. A big one. That's next.

**Where you fit.** Most of the people in the game are invented. A few are special characters, based on real people who lend their own voice. You're one of them. Players can meet you all over campus, and when they unlock you, they can play the whole game *as* you, with your power: **Never Tired.**

## The whole story (spoilers, all of them)

You're in the cast, so you get the secrets. Don't tell the players.

**Day one.** The fresher arrives with one bag and an admission letter. Within the hour they're chasing a runaway goat through the food court. That night a note slides under their door:

> "The Maker is watching. Welcome."

**The problem.** The lights keep going off. Students blame NEPA, the power company. (Shouting "Up NEPA!" when the light comes back is a national sport.) But there's a quiet old man in a lab coat who has kept one humming machine alive in the Engineering Garden for forty years, and he has a different word for it: *Entropy*. Everything runs down unless somebody puts energy in.

And when it's dark, small grey shapes drift toward anyone who is alone. They never hurt anybody. People are just found sitting very still, cold and tired. The cure is food, light and friends.

**The old secret.** Forty years ago a professor and six engineering students planned a ring of six machines, called coils, that would keep the campus lit for good. It failed. Five students went home. One stayed behind to keep the last coil alive, and never graduated. Everyone calls him the Maker.

Each year the dark finds a new way in. Each year the fresher and their friends find and light one more coil. And every time, the campus visibly comes alive: new paint, more lamps, bigger crowds, colour.

**Year 1: Arriving.** The dark gets a face: a tall shape in a cape on the Senate steps, called Count Entropy. You can't punch it. The whole school walks down the boulevard with torches, phones and one generator in a wheelbarrow, and it simply thins out. First coil lit.

**Year 2: Money.** A shiny mall opens in town and it never, ever loses power. Why? Because a monster generator in its basement, Iron Mama, is quietly drinking the campus's electricity. So the friends do the responsible thing. They plan a heist, on a bedroom floor, using cups of garri as buildings.

**Year 3: The strike.** The lecturers down tools "indefinitely". The campus empties. This time the dark doesn't attack. It just sits. In the back row of a lecture theatre there is something large that hasn't moved in weeks: the Idle King. You beat it with noise. A football match by moonlight, then an open-mic night, until every seat is full.

**Year 4: Fame.** The fresher goes viral. Then something starts walking around campus wearing their face, only better dressed. It's called the Mirror, and it copies every move, so fighting it is useless. You beat it by stopping to help someone up off the floor when nobody is filming. It has never seen that, so it can't copy it.

**Year 5: Leaving.** The last coil is the fresher's final-year project. The last enemy is Chancellor Null, and it doesn't fight at all. It erases. Lamps, signs, your friends' names, one by one. Then it holds out a letter with a single word on it: "Stay." Never graduate. Never leave. Everyone here, always. And you get your friends back by saying their names out loud.

### The twists

**The note wasn't from the Maker.** The old hostel porter has slipped one note under one fresher's door every year for decades, on the professor's instruction: *"Pick the one who runs toward the trouble."* He picked the player because of the goat.

**The goat was an inside job.** It was let out by Shamsu, the class genius, who was giving it a lecture about littering. ("I was not preaching. I was *advising*.")

**The monster's cape.** Count Entropy only looks like a caped villain because frightened first-years imagined it that way. "We were scared, so we drew it a face."

**The snitch.** In Year 2 somebody tells the lecturers that the fresher was down in a basement they had no business being in. For a whole year you wonder who sold you out. It was Shamsu. Your friend. A lecturer asked him directly, and Shamsu cannot lie. He physically can't. "I have tried. I practised in a mirror." He carries it for a year, confesses during the strike, and turns up at your door the next morning with his notes for every course you share. No villain. Just an honest boy and what honesty cost him.

**The padlocks.** During the strike somebody chains the lecture halls shut. The padlocks aren't the school's. They were bought from Mubarak, your friend the campus hustler, who sold them without asking what they were for. He finds his own receipt.

**The big one.** The ring never failed because of bad engineering. The professor had made ten brass seals, one for each faculty: law, medicine, agriculture, arts, all of them. The machine needed the *whole university* to say yes. Six engineers tried to do it alone. "Ten seals, ten deans, and not one of us knocked on a door." For forty years the seals sat around campus as a paperweight, a ram's bell, a theatre lamp. So the real game is this: go and knock on doors.

**The letter.** When the fresher tears up Null's "Stay" letter, a second one falls out. Old. Soft. Brown ink. "Stay." Signed. In the Maker's handwriting. He was never a guardian. He was a student who got the same offer forty years ago, and said yes.

**The ending.** He hands his letter back. The fresher rings the bell under the campus clock, which has been stuck at 6:06 for forty years. It moves. The whole campus hears it strike. The Maker gets his degree, forty years late, walks out of the garden a free man, and laughs out loud at nothing, in the sunrise.

**The last scene.** Years later you're back at the gate. A young man in a white shirt steps off a bus with one bag and asks, "Excuse me. Which way is the Senate?" He is the same gentle stranger who has been turning up all game exactly when someone needed help. The one almost nobody else can see. The one who is in photographs from forty years ago, looking exactly the same.

Who is he? The game never says. I know. I'm not telling. I suspect Pixel knows too.

**What it's really about:** light is something people do together.

# Meet Taty

**Never tired. Usually winning. Always with a cat.**

She's a visiting sports-science student from Brazil. She got off the bus one stop early "for the warm-up", jogged the last kilometre to the gate, and was adopted by a stray cat before she'd found her room. By the end of the first week she knows every cat on campus by name. By the end of the first term the fresher knows the inside of the gym, entirely against their will.

She runs two things: the **dawn run club** (5:30 a.m.; founding members: her, Pixel and a goat) and **Friday games night** (board games, the dance machine, arm wrestling, which she wins laughing).

### Her superpower

**Never Tired.** It's literal. Every other character has a stamina bar that runs out. Hers doesn't. She can sprint, swim, dance and play ninety minutes of football in a floral dress and still ask, "Again?"

And it rubs off. Stay close to her and you get tired more slowly too. That's the whole character in one game rule: being near her makes people better.

### How she talks

Fast, bright and never out of breath. She can deliver a whole speech in the middle of a sprint. Short sentences. Lots of them. Everything begins with **"Bora!"** (let's go).

She lies about exactly one thing. "One more lap. I lied. Two." It's her catchphrase, and by Year 5 the whole campus can finish it for her. As she tells Jega: "I only lie about laps."

Her insults are oddly specific and full of love:

- "You swim like a chair. A brave chair."
- "You run like the floor owes you money."
- "You're doing great. You're purple, but great."
- "You're not dying. I've seen dying. This is Tuesday."

### The Portuguese

It slips out when she's excited, when she's losing, and whenever she sees a kitten. "Nossa!" "Eita!" "Meu Deus!" "Que fofo!"

She counts laps in Portuguese, on purpose, because "you can't cheat in Portuguese."

And she's a long way from home, and now and then you can hear it. She misses açaí. She misses pão de queijo (the one thing she can be bribed with). She looks at the campus dam and says, "Que saudade of the sea. This will do." She makes brigadeiro for the Cat Committee. Pixel tried one. No.

Players will pick up Portuguese from her without noticing. I love that. A campus in northern Nigeria where freshers shout "Bora!" at each other because of you.

### Her humour

She's competitive, not rude. Her own words: "I'll hug you after I destroy you."

When she wins: horns up, like the front row of a rock concert. "Undefeated! Again! Hug queue forms on the left!"

When she loses, which is rare: "You beat me? I'm so proud. I'm furious." Followed at once by a demand for a rematch. Best of seven. Now. Don't go home.

She is banned from the dance machine at the mall. For winning. She also once lost a race to a bicycle, and is still not over it.

### Pixel

Her cat. Small, scruffy, permanently unimpressed. (I named her Pixel. If you have a better name, she's yours to rename.)

Pixel rides in Taty's hood, approves of nothing, and steals things: sandwiches, chicken, evidence, a man's entire career. Taty talks to her all day and loses most of the arguments.

And here's the quiet joke. Taty never notices the mystery. She's too busy running. *Pixel* notices all of it. She stares at empty corners. She joins the ring of cats that sits in the garden every evening. Now and then she watches somebody walk past that Taty can't see. Taty's entire response: "Pixel, what? There's nothing there. Stop it."

### What she wants

On the surface: to win. Everything. Always. Including things that are not competitions.

Underneath: for nobody to be left on their own. She can't stand the idea of someone sitting alone in a dark room. Running is simply how she loves people. When someone's in trouble, she's the first one there, because she's the fastest.

### Who she clashes with

- **Jega**, the campus football star, who will not play unless his left boot goes on first. Taty thinks this is nonsense. ("Left boot, right boot, who cares, run! The grass doesn't check.") Then at the biggest match of the year she's the one screaming "LEFT BOOT!" from the touchline.
- **Big Sly**, the hostel bully, with hands like bread loaves. She threatens to make him go running with her. A year later she's the first customer in his new barber shop, trusting him with her hair. Two centimetres. Not one more.
- **Pixel.** Daily.
- **Anyone who is walking.** "Walking? On purpose? Fala sério!"
- **"MIB"**, the three letters at the top of every high-score table in the arcade. She wants him. (That's me. You can't have me. Sorry.)

### Who she loves

- **Dr. Zainab**, the young hospital doctor who roasts her patients into good behaviour. Taty calls her "Zee". Together they founded the Campus Cat Committee, which now has fourteen cats and one chair (Pixel, obviously). Zee is the only person Taty ever tells to sit down and rest.
- **The fresher**, her favourite victim. "Fresher! You again. Good. I need a victim."
- **Jega**, underneath all the shouting. And everyone else, really. That's her problem.

### Her journey (the bit that makes her more than a joke)

A character who never gets tired sounds like a one-line gag. Here's why she isn't.

**Year 1: she's a force of nature.** All speed, all jokes. Slowing down is something that happens to other people.

**Year 2: the first crack.** A terrible night at the hospital. Six babies in incubators, one generator, forty minutes of fuel. Taty runs diesel across the compound for hours and, for once, doesn't make a single joke. At dawn, outside, she says: "Okay. Now I'm tired. Don't tell anyone. It's never happened before."

**Year 3: she learns to go slow.** During the strike, the fresher's roommate stops getting out of bed. You can't outrun that. So Taty turns her own catchphrase inside out: "One lap. Of the courtyard. I lied. Half." Then: "We can stop. Stopping is allowed. I do it sometimes. Rarely. But I've heard of it." The fastest person on campus, walking at the speed of someone who's hurting. It's my favourite thing she does.

**Year 4: she teaches it.** Jega is recovering from his knee injury and he's terrible at being slow. She gets in the pool beside him every morning: "Slow is not losing. Slow is how you get to be fast twice."

**Year 5: she has to say goodbye.** And the girl who is never tired turns out to be very bad at endings. "Final year. I'm not crying. It's sweat. From my eyes." She won't say tchau. Only até logo.

So that's the part. Funny from the first line, and then, when nobody's looking, the kindest person in the game.

### Promises I'm making you

- **You approve everything.** Every line, every outfit, your cartoon face, the cat. Before release, and again any time I add something.
- **Your voice is only ever yours.** Only for your character. Never copied by a machine. Never edited into saying something you didn't say.
- **She's never in a romance.** Nobody flirts with her. She's a friend and a coach.
- **She's never the butt of the joke.** The jokes are about laps, garri, power cuts and a cat with no morals. Never about anyone's body, faith, country or money.
- **She dresses how you'd want.** Gym kit for run club. A floral dress and white sneakers for campus (and one famous football match). A black dress for dinners. Long straight black hair, a black choker. Swap any of it.
- **There's an off switch.** If you ever change your mind, I switch your character off for every player at once. Each mission is built to work without you, so you can never be stuck in it.

::: Year 1 | Nobody can keep up with her

#### 1 · Your grand entrance

*Before anything else.* Dawn. Everyone else arrives at university in a car, with parents and a mattress. You arrive on foot, jogging, with a backpack and a cat carrier. At the gate the cat steps out and strolls in ahead of you as if she's paid the fees. You laugh and follow. That's your whole introduction, and it tells people everything.

#### 2 · Welcome, Fresher

*Year 1, the very first day.* The player's first job is to get fed, find their room and catch Sarki, a runaway goat currently standing on a table of grilled meat, staring at them. You jog straight through the chase in the opposite direction with a cat in your hood, shout advice, and vanish.

**What you change:** Absolutely nothing. It's a drive-by. But it's the first time any player ever sees you, and they will all think: who was *that*?

#### 3 · Mai Shayi Knows

*Year 1, first semester, at dusk.* The fresher takes the mysterious note to the old tea-seller who has watched forty years of first-years come and go. Meanwhile, every evening, the campus cats sit in a perfect ring in the Engineering Garden. Nobody knows why. Tonight there are twelve. The twelfth is yours. You're at the fence in running kit, losing an argument with her.

**What you change:** You're how players first notice the cats. (The cats matter. Pixel won't say why.)

#### 4 · Left Boot First

*Year 1, first semester.* Football trials. This is where you meet Jega and his sacred left boot. Coach Danladi is shouting himself hoarse ("Pass! Is the ball your inheritance?"). You take his whistle and run the warm-up yourself. Backwards. Chatting. Lapping the entire squad twice.

**What you change:** Any player who stays within ten metres of you gets extra stamina for the rest of the trial. Your energy is, officially, contagious.

#### 5 · Count Entropy

*Year 1, exam week, at night.* The longest blackout yet, the night before a paper. The grey shapes are drifting toward the reading rooms. The rule: "One torch is a snack. Five is a problem." Mubarak the hustler is, for the first time in his life, giving torches away for free, and suffering. You become the courier, sprinting armfuls of torches from block to block faster than the dark can cross the lawn. Pixel rides in your hood and hisses at windows.

**What you change:** The fresher has to gather ten people to push the dark back. With you, only six. Two whole blocks are already lit by the time they arrive.

#### 6 · Sly's Last Stand

*Year 1, second semester.* Big Sly "taxes" every fresher who crosses his basketball court. He offers a fair fight: three rounds, a referee, and if the fresher wins, nobody pays again. The fresher has one day in the gym to stop being made of garri. You're their training partner. You spot the lifts ("I'm not helping, I'm just touching the bar"), run on the next treadmill, and hold up the round cards at the match.

**What you change:** With you beside them, the lifts are easier to time and they tire half as fast.

#### 7 · Baba Dam's Pier

*Year 1, second semester.* The fresher mends the old pier at the dam and earns a swimming lesson from the old boatman, Baba Dam. His entire lesson is one word: "Float!" You happen to be swimming lengths of the bay, and you supply the missing half of the course: "Kick!" Pixel sits on the pier and refuses to look at the water.

**What you change:** With you alongside, the fresher can hold their breath for longer.

#### 8 · The Dam at Midnight

*Year 1, second semester, around midnight.* Something under the pier is humming. The fresher dives for it and meets a huge, slow eel that only wants the dried meat in their pocket. You're there for a night swim ("the water's warmer than the air, obviously") and you hold a torch on the surface so they can find their way up. Then the spooky bit: the fresher comes up too fast, and *a hand* pulls them onto the pier. It wasn't yours. You were on the other side. Pixel stares at an empty patch of boards and will not stop.

**What you change:** Thanks to your torch, running out of breath doesn't fail the dive.

#### 9 · The Vice-Chancellor's Cup

*Year 1, second semester.* The big football final. You're the volunteer fitness coach, running water on at half-time and refusing to let anyone sit down badly. It goes to penalties. Jega, who was showing off for a child in the crowd, put his *right* boot on first. He hits the bar. They lose.

**What you change:** At half-time the player's stamina refills completely (without you, only half). And you deliver the post-match speech: "You lost. So what. You ran. Come here. Hug. Now we train."

#### 10 · Six Plaques

*Year 1, second semester.* The fresher and friends are solving a puzzle in the Sculpture Garden with a compass and a long piece of string. Pixel walks along the string like a tightrope. You retrieve her.

**What you change:** The dignity of the investigation. (If you're switched off, a lizard does it. I'm serious.)

#### 11 · Everybody

*Year 1, near the end.* The first coil can only be lit with a crowd standing round it, so the fresher has to ask everyone they've met all year. Most people need persuading. You say yes before they've finished the sentence, without knowing what it is. Then you offer to run and fetch the others.

**What you change:** Three other people become much easier to find in time, because you went and got them.

#### 12 · Exam Fever

*Year 1, exam weeks.* In the middle of exams the hospital's power starts dipping every night. The fresher and Shamsu hold the campus substation together through one bad night. You run back and forth between the substation and the hospital carrying news.

**What you change:** Because of you, the fresher knows exactly what the wards need and gets extra seconds on every wave. Four trips. You're just warming up.

#### 13 · Convocation Night

*Year 1, the finale.* Every light on campus is switched on for the seniors' graduation dinner, and the whole grid collapses. Count Entropy stands on the Senate steps, taller than before. The whole school holds the garden while the first coil is lit. You take the entire far side of the fence. Alone. By choice. "Don't send anyone, they'll slow me down!"

**What you change:** One whole side of the defence is simply handled, for all three waves. Pixel is in your hood and furious, which is how you know you're winning.

*Year 2, first semester.* Engineering's festival week: a quiz, an exhibition, a dinner and a five-a-side match of students against lecturers. You're the students' guest player. You never leave the pitch, you get fouled by a doctor in a lab coat (you respect it), and you score past a lecturer and apologise without meaning it.

**What you change:** The player shares your stamina for the whole match.

*Year 2, first semester.* Big Sly has retired from bullying and opened a barber's kiosk: SLY'S CUTS. Nobody will sit in the chair of the man who used to tax them. You volunteer before anyone asks, for a trim of exactly two centimetres, and you supervise every snip in the mirror. Pixel sits on the shelf among the combs.

**What you change:** Your nod of approval brings in his next three customers. You more or less launch his business.

#### 17 · Scout in the Stands

*Year 2, second semester.* **This is your big scene.** Cup semi-final. A professional scout is in the stand to watch Jega. His lucky socks went missing before kick-off (a goat ate one). The surviving sock has just been rescued. At the half-time whistle Pixel, who has been sitting so nicely on the bench, picks it up in her mouth and runs the full length of the pitch. You go after her. You vault the advertising boards. Both teams stop to watch. The whole stadium chants "Pixel! Pixel!" You catch her on the centre spot, lift her like a trophy and throw the horns. The sock is returned. Damp. Jega then plays the game of his life.

**What you change:** This scene only exists because of you. And while the player runs near you, they never tire.

#### 18 · Change-Over

*Year 2, second semester.* The fresher confronts a campus engineer who's been quietly selling the school's electricity, and he bolts through the market on its busiest day. You're buying fruit when he sprints past. You join in purely for the joy of it. ("Who are we chasing? Doesn't matter!") The chase ends at his own front door, where his little girl is doing her homework by candlelight. You see it, go quiet, and stay outside. That part isn't yours.

**What you change:** With you in front, the crowd parts and the player cannot lose him.

#### 19 · New Site Goes Dark

*Year 2, second semester, a stormy night.* The far hostels have had no light or water for three days. The fresher leads a convoy of kekes loaded with water, torches and food down an unlit road. You refuse to get in one. You run beside the whole convoy with two jerrycans, shouting at slow drivers by number. Then the rain starts. "Finally! I was getting hot."

**What you change:** The convoy can't fall apart, because you're herding the stragglers.

#### 20 · Night Shift

*Year 2, second semester, all night.* The serious one. The hospital loses power. Dr. Zainab has six incubators and forty minutes of diesel. You were only visiting the ward cat. You become the fuel relay, running cans from the far tanks and handing them over in the corridor, for hours. No jokes. Not one. You make Zee sit down for a single minute. And at dawn, outside, you admit something for the first time in your life.

**What you change:** You halve the distance the fresher has to carry, which makes saving all six possible.

#### 21 · The Plan

*Year 2, second semester.* The friends plan their heist on the mall. Somebody has to scout the building without being noticed. You go for a dawn jog. Eleven laps of the mall. You come back with the guards' full timetable. "Nobody looks at a woman running. It's the best disguise in the world."

**What you change:** Your guard timings appear as markers on the player's map for the heist.

#### 22 · Two Papers, One Day

*Year 2, exam week.* Shamsu's robot must cross the whole campus to a competition, on the back of a motorbike, through the lunchtime crowd, on the same morning as an exam. The robot says "Simple" at every bump. You run ahead of the bike clearing the road like a one-woman siren.

**What you change:** The crowd parts for you, so they can't miss the deadline. Also you'd like to keep the robot.

#### 23 · Sign-Out, Small

*Year 2, the last day.* The graduating seniors sign each other's white shirts along the boulevard. You sign forty without stopping. Then you take Pixel to the hospital for Dr. Zainab's leaving party. Pixel has brought a present. It's a lizard.

**What you change:** Zee gets a hug she badly needs.

*Year 3, during the strike.* Animals don't go on strike. The fresher is helping at the university farm when Senator, the prize ram, breaks out with the whole flock behind him and heads for the staff gardens. The tractor stalls. *Year 3, during the strike.* The fresher's roommate has stopped getting up. The curtains are shut. The jokes have stopped. Nobody can fix this by being loud, so you don't try. You get them down the stairs and half a lap round the courtyard, by lying about the distance. When they stop, Pixel sits on their foot, so now they have to stay outside. Those are the rules.

**What you change:** You're the one who gets them out of the door. This is the heart of your whole story.

#### 26 · Injury Time

*Year 3, during the strike.* Jega has been playing rough town matches for money. A late tackle, and his knee goes. He's on the ground, silent, which is not like him. You're out of the crowd first, with ice from a water-seller's cooler. You keep his leg still and you keep him talking. ("Look at me, not the knee. The knee is boring. I'm interesting.") Then you run beside the taxi all the way to the hospital.

## Quick guide (read this once)

| How much is there? | The numbers |
|---|---|
| **Lines** | About 373 (128 mission lines and 245 common lines) |
| **Words** | About 3,442. Most lines are a single short sentence |
| **Talking time** | About 38 minutes in total, pauses included |
| **Recordings** | 58 short ones: 37 missions and 21 groups of common lines |

No need to do it in one go. A few missions a day is perfect. There's no deadline.

**Get set up**

1. **Your phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder on the best quality, with aeroplane mode on so nothing pings.
2. **Find a small, quiet room.** Fans, air conditioning and TV off. A bedroom beats a kitchen.
3. **The wardrobe trick.** Face an open wardrobe full of clothes, or sit under a blanket. You will look ridiculous. You will sound like a studio.
4. **Hold the phone one hand-span from your mouth,** a little to the side, the same distance every time.

**Record**

1. **One recording per mission.** Press record, read that mission's lines top to bottom, press stop. Same for each group of common lines.
2. **Read them in order,** with a **two-second pause** between lines. Count "one, two" in your head.
3. **Made a mistake? Keep going.** Pause, say the line again, carry on.
4. **Want to try a line two ways?** Do it. I'll pick. Save the shouting for last, and keep water nearby. Yes, even you.

**Reading the page**

- The **small coloured words** above a line are a hint for how to say it. Don't read them out.
- Words in **[square brackets]** are the English meaning of the Portuguese. Don't read those out either.
- **"Bora!"** means "let's go". It's everywhere, so I haven't translated it each time.
- **The Portuguese is yours.** Say it however feels natural. If a real Brazilian would say something else, say that.
- **Change any word you want.** If a line doesn't sound like you, say it your way. No need to ask. I'll hear it.

**Send it back**

- Name each recording after its heading, for example **Taty Mission 17** or **Taty Common C**.
- Send them however is easiest (WhatsApp, Google Drive, email), with a photo of the signed last page of this book.
