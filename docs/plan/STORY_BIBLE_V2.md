## 1. Premise and themes

### 1.1 The story in one page
You arrive at {{UNI_NAME}} in {{TOWN}} with one bag, an admission letter and your mother's instructions. On your first day you chase a runaway goat through the food court, and at sunset there is a note under your door: **"The Maker is watching. Welcome."**

The light keeps going off. Students call it NEPA. A quiet man in an old lab coat, who keeps one humming coil alive in the Engineering Garden, calls it **Entropy**: everything runs down unless somebody puts energy in. In the dark, small grey shapes drift toward people who are alone. Nobody is ever hurt by them. People are found sitting very still, cold and tired, and they recover with food, light and friends.

Long ago a professor and six engineering students planned a **ring of six coils** to keep the school's light steady. They built alone, the parts did not fit, five went home, and one stayed to keep the single working coil alive. He never graduated. He is **the Maker**. What nobody remembers is the professor's last instruction: the ring was never meant to be an engineering project. It needed the consent and the craft of **every faculty**: ten brass seals, one handed to each dean, "to be added when your people are ready". Nobody ever was.

Each year Entropy finds a new way in (blackouts, stolen power, an empty campus, false images, the fear of endings), and each year you and your friends find and light one more coil, and win one or two more seals. Each coil lit makes the campus visibly richer: paint, lamps, crowds, colour. In the fifth year the sixth coil is your final-year project, ten faculties carry ten seals to the door under the stopped Clock Tower, the Maker hands back a letter he once accepted, the ring closes, the clock starts, and he walks out of the garden a free man.

The last mission is a gentle stranger at the gate, asking you the way to the Senate.

**The message, never preached: light is something people do together.**

### 1.3 What is true about the mystery (writers must not contradict this)
1. **Prof. Adaeze Nwachukwu** and six students designed the ring. The first laboratory stood in what is now forest (the **Old Site**). It failed, the school moved, and only the Garden coil was kept running.
2. **The Maker** is the sixth student. He never graduated. He believes that if he leaves, the light goes for good.
3. **Entropy is not a person.** It has no voice and no backstory. It takes the shape of whatever is running down. **Shades** are pieces of it: not ghosts, not the dead. Light scatters them; noise, company and movement weaken them.
4. **The Ten Seals.** The professor cast ten brass seals and gave one to each dean. Over forty years each seal became that faculty's forgotten treasure or its legend (a gavel block, a ram's bell, a theatre lamp). Each seal is won in a main mission that needs that faculty's real skill. All ten sit in ten sockets on the door of the sixth site.
5. **The Clock Tower** has been stopped at **6:06** for as long as anyone remembers. Everyone has a theory. It starts when the ring closes.

### 1.6 Tone guide
- **Warm, fast and very funny.** A funny line in the first 30 seconds of every mission. The joke is always the situation (sapa, garri, NEPA, queues, "network", hostel wahala), never a person's tribe, faith, body or bank balance. We laugh **with** people.
- **Slang bank:** `docs/HUMOUR.md` carries over whole ("Faaa!", "Sapa has entered the chat", "It's giving garri", "Breaking news: he read"). "Fa" is used as the northern emphasis particle: "I'm hungry fa."
- **The dark is quiet, not horror.** No gore, no jump scares, no deaths. Knocked down means sit, shake your head and get up.
- **Modest.** Romance is walks, food, study dates, holding hands at the dinner. Opposite-sex only. The camera never lingers.
- **Faith is ordinary and respected.** People pray, fast and go to service as part of the day. Pastor Sam and Ustaz Auwal are best friends. Nothing religious is ever a punchline.
- **Languages.** English first, with Hausa, Pidgin, Yoruba and Igbo phrases, always subtitled, always checked by a native speaker before recording. Hausa is written in plain letters here; the reviewer adds the proper hooked letters.
- **The player is a light talker.** {{PLAYER_NAME}} speaks in short lines (1–8 words) and choices. Friends carry the long talk. Total player lines: about 260. Sample: "I'm fine." / "Who's the Maker?" / "How much?" / "We move." / "I'll carry it." / "Sorry, Doctor." / "…That goat is looking at me."
- **Every scene must pass:** would a student from a northern campus say "that is exactly how it is"?

### 1.8 The mystery engine (owner's direction: full of mysteries, connected, never fully explained)
**Five rules for every writer:**
1. **Layered questions.** Each year answers one question and opens two. The player always holds at least three open questions (listed below).
2. **Cliffhangers.** Every year ends on one, and at least one mission in three ends on a small one: a line, an object or a face that does not fit yet.
3. **Clues that only make sense later.** Nothing is underlined. A clue is a detail in the world, shown once, that the player may or may not notice.
4. **Connection.** Every mission has a **"Connects to"** line: the earlier missions it calls back to (an object, a consequence, a person you helped coming back).
5. **Deliberate gaps.** Some answers are never given. The truth is written down once, in §13 **[OWNER ONLY]**, so the clues stay consistent. Players never see it.

### 1.9 Muhammad: the gentle mystery
**What the player experiences.** A soft-spoken young man in a plain white shirt who appears exactly when you need him most and is gone when you turn round. Hardly anyone else notices him. When you mention him, friends look confused ("Who? There was nobody there fa."), and some people talk straight past him, mid-sentence, as if a chair were empty. A few people always see him: small children, cats, Kaka, Wednesday, Lily, Baba Porter, and people standing at a beginning or an ending.

**Rules for writing him.**
1. He never lies, never explains, and never stays for the end of a scene.
2. He helps with small things only: a lift, a pen, a warning, a hand up, the answer to question 4. He never fights and never fixes the big problem.
3. He knows things he should not (your name before you say it; which stair is broken; what the exam will ask), and apologises for knowing.
4. Every sighting plants exactly one clue. The full list is the tracker in §10.6.
5. He is never confirmed to be anything.

**Three theories the evidence supports (the game supports all three, and confirms none).**
| Theory | What players will say | Evidence for | Evidence against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. He is the Maker,** somehow: his younger self, or the life he would have had | "Same height, same hands, and they never speak to each other" | The Maker nods to him and looks away. The old photos. The second name in the coat. He knows the garden's every stone. He arrives as a fresher only after the Maker is free | He is younger and darker than the Maker; the Maker is in some of the same old photos, beside him |
| **B. He is the light's face.** Freshers gave the dark a face (the Count). Somebody, once, gave the kindness a face too | "The Count is what we feared. Muhammad is what we hoped" | He appears when someone is alone and needs help, exactly as shades appear when someone is alone. Cats and children see him. He is strongest near lit coils. No registry file | He eats groundnuts. He gets rained on. He was tired once (M105) |
| **C. He is the seventh student,** erased. The scratched-out name on the seventh plaque is his | "He took a letter. He stayed. He was forgotten" | Null erases names. The registry has a file cover with nothing in it. Seven stools in the dream (M105), one always empty. Post for Room 606 | Null's old letter is in the *Maker's* handwriting, not his |

A fourth, playful theory the game allows: **he has not arrived yet.** Everything you saw was a fresher's first year, seen early (the coil bent a day in M80; why not more?).

## 13. [OWNER ONLY] The truth

**This section is never shown to players, voice actors or community creators. It exists so that every clue points the same way. It should be cut from any copy of this document that leaves your hands.** Everything here is a proposal: it is the one explanation that fits all the clues planted in sections 1–10.

### 13.1 What the ring really needs
The professor's design (page forty-one) has seven parts: six coils and a **keeper**. The keeper is not a guard. It is a person who closes the ring by *leaving*: a finalist who rings the bell on their way out, so that every five years somebody new has to care. "Must be someone who will leave." The ten seals are the ten faculties' consent. The bell under the clock is the fourth note.

That is what Baba Porter's notes are for. Each year he picks a fresher who "runs toward the trouble": a candidate keeper, five years ahead of time. For forty years none of them got far enough. Wednesday was one (her note is in the tin, with a tick): in her third year she got caught in a day the coil would not let go of, and has been living the same Wednesdays since. Kelechi's wish in M80 shook her loose; that is why she sees a Thursday at the end of it.

### 13.2 What happened forty years ago
The ring failed at 6:06 one evening. The dark that came that night offered the sixth student a letter: "Stay." He signed it. He believed he was saving the light. What the letter took was his **name** and his **leaving**: his file emptied itself (the empty file cover in the Registry labelled "Muhammad" is *his*), the people who loved him forgot they had, and the life he would have gone on to live was cut loose from him.

He had a little sister, **Aisha**, who came to the dam with him to feed the fish, and whose thumb is in the bronze disc and the kiln brick, because she "helped". The small bare footprints under the clock are hers, from the last day the round room was open. After the letter she forgot she had a brother. She grew up in {{TOWN}}, and grew old; she is Lily and Lyzzy's grandmother, which is why the twins know something ("She says she had a brother once. She thinks."), and why his niece Asiya (his brother's daughter) has never heard of a sister: the letter cut the thread from both ends. She is treated at the teaching hospital from time to time. On those days he sits in the garden outside her window and holds a light up. Those are **his absences**. He carved the statue. It turns toward wherever she is. The bench in the Sculpture Garden was hers; he will not sit on it. The sweets are his. "Ai—" is her name. At convocation she comes, not knowing why, and sits at the back. In the post-game, sometimes, she comes out and sits beside him. We never show whether she remembers.

### 13.3 Who Muhammad is
**Muhammad is the life the Maker gave up:** the young man who would have graduated, walked out of the gate and gone on. The letter separated them. He is not a ghost, not a shade, and not quite a person: he is unlived years, walking, and he is as kind as the Maker would have been if he had not had to be a guardian. The name unpicked from the coat (eight needle holes) is **Muhammad**. It is the Maker's own name, the one the letter took.

This is why:
- he looks the same in every old photograph, and does not hold in new ones;
- the Registry has his file cover and nothing in it;
- most people cannot notice him: to anyone settled into the campus's routine, that name is erased. Those who can see him are those the erasure never reached or does not bind: small children, cats, the very old (Kaka), people at a beginning or an ending (a new fresher, Zainab on the night she decides, Nusaiba on the night she learns the truth), and keeper-candidates (you; Wednesday);
- he can only do small things (a lift, a pen, a hand), because small kindnesses are all that the Maker had left over;
- he knows what he should not: he has walked every corridor for forty years with nothing to do but pay attention;
- he and the Maker never speak, except once (M72): the "old argument" is whether to hand the letter back;
- he wrote "Five more" on your note. He was counting coils, and years;
- he is tired in the dream (M105) because the Maker is at his weakest;
- he vanishes when the letter is returned (M116–M119). The Maker looks down the road for him;
- and he arrives at the gate, years later, as a fresher (M120). The life finally begins, late, as its own person. The Maker, an old man now, is feeding birds two hundred metres away. **We never put them in the same shot again, and never say how both can be true.**

The envelopes for **Room 606** are the Maker's: one each semester for forty years, written to the room he was allocated on a sixth floor that was planned and never built (like the tower, like the ring). They are letters to the self who would have lived there. In M120 they are handed to Muhammad. Their contents are never read aloud, in any version of the game.

**How the three public theories relate to this:** Theory A (he is the Maker, somehow) is closest. Theory C (the erased seventh student) is half right: the scratched name under yours on the seventh plaque is the Maker's own, from when the professor first hoped he would be the keeper, before she wrote "Not him. He never will." Theory B (the light's face) is wrong in fact and right in spirit, and it is the one the game's imagery most encourages. **Do not let any script choose between them.**
