#### M049 · Three Hundred
**When:** Y3 · S1 Wk 1–2 · lecture days · 08:00–17:00. **Where:** eng: Theatre Gabas, the electrical lab, the noticeboard; lib: main reading room. **Who:** Shamsu, Bolaji, Nusaiba, Jega (auditing, asleep), Dr. Okonkwo. **Special helpers (if enabled):** none.

**Mystery:** plants `C-SNITCH` (Shamsu starts a sentence about last year and swerves into a formula); calls back `C-SYMBOL` (six dots and a seventh, scratched on your desk in Gabas, a little deeper than last year).

**Story.** Third year starts with a small shock. Outside Theatre Gabas a fresher steps aside for you and says, "Good morning, senior." You look behind you to see who he means. There is nobody behind you.

Dr. Okonkwo opens 300 level with one sentence and a stack of paper. The sentence is "Page forty," because it always is. The paper is the brief for the year's group project: **Light Work**, a solar street lamp for the New Site road, designed in first semester, built in second, planted along the road where people can judge it by walking home under it. Groups of three. She reads the groups out like a sentencing.

Yours is Shamsu, who has finished the design in his head before she finishes your name; Bolaji, several extra years into Mechanical and attached to your group "for his sins and yours"; and you. Bolaji agrees to build it. He does not agree to attend. Jega is also in the room, three rows back, auditing "for the culture", fast asleep with a football under his chin.

The mission is the dividing of work, and the game quietly notes whom you load. There are nine tasks on the brief: the panel sizing, the battery sums, the pole, the footing, the lamp head, the circuit, the costing, the drawings, the report. Give Shamsu everything and he takes it gladly and grows a little greyer in every later scene. Take too much yourself and your evenings shrink. Give Bolaji the pole and the footing and something unexpected happens: he reads the brief twice, alone, with his lips moving.

Then the library, for the costing. Nusaiba is at the next table with a tower of law reports and helps in the way she helps, by asking who is taking minutes. Shamsu draws the circuit on a napkin. "Simple. The panel is a farmer, the battery is a barn, the lamp is the family eating at night. The controller is the mother who says no." He looks at you a moment too long. "{{PLAYER_NAME}}, last year, in the matter of the basement, I should technically…" He stops. "…mention that the charge controller needs a diode." He writes DIODE and underlines it three times. It did not need underlining.

In the lab you run the first test: one panel, one battery, one bulb, in the sun on the window ledge. It lights. It is the least impressive lamp in Nigeria and all three of you stand and look at it. Bolaji is not there; his contribution arrives in the group chat that evening, a single thumbs-up, replying to a message from eleven days ago.

Dr. Okonkwo initials your plan and says, lightly, the way people mention weather, that the staff union is "having meetings". As you leave, a clerk is pinning a notice to the faculty board. You do not stop to read it. Everybody else does.

#### M050 · Indefinite
**When:** Y3 · S1 Wk 4 · Mon (the first Strike day) · 09:00–18:00. **Where:** student: the SUG hall (congress); market: the motor park. **Who:** Comrade Sadiya, Nusaiba, {{ROOMIE}}, Jega, Shamsu, Mubarak, the twins, Emily, Aleeya, Maryam, Muhammad. **Special helpers (if enabled):** Jiya.
**Connects to:** M49 (the notice you did not read); M22 (everybody you asked for help, now leaving, each with a line about it); M46 (Nusaiba learned at the hearing that the room is where things are decided); M48 (the seniors left; now your own year does).
**Mystery:** plants `MS-13` (he sits on the kerb with no bag; a conductor steps round him like a post).

**Story.** The notice is read aloud by forty people at once, at forty speeds, so that the word "indefinite" arrives like rain on a zinc roof: first one drop, then everything.

Congress is called in the SUG hall. It is loud and it solves nothing. Comrade Sadiya bangs a gavel that has no block to land on. One faction wants a protest, one wants a refund, and one man wants it minuted that the fans are not working. In the middle of this Nusaiba stands with a single sheet: a motion to form a **student welfare committee** for those who cannot go home, with a register, a food rota and a list of jobs. You carry copies along the rows and collect seconders (a short crowd game: catch an eye, hand a sheet, move before the next argument starts). It passes on a voice vote while nobody is listening. She writes down the time anyway. "It counts. Minutes are minutes."

Then the day turns into goodbyes. Six friends, six buses, and each farewell is built from something you actually did together: Emily presses a punnet of strawberries on you "for bad news, and this is bad news"; Lyzzy films you waving and Lily fixes your collar first; Maryam leaves you a book with a yam recipe as a bookmark; Aleeya hugs you, tells you that you look strong, and adds that it is mostly the bag; Mubarak is not travelling, exactly, he is "relocating inventory", and still owes Hajiya Ladi. Each bus you load is one more seat empty in the map of your week.

Three people stay. {{ROOMIE}}: "My village has no network and three aunties. I choose NEPA." Jega, for a town league he describes as "training". Shamsu, to study, with a timetable already laminated.

At the motor park the conductor prices the world. "Kano is ₦4,000. Kano *with your load* is a conversation." You haggle for a friend's bag, and lose with dignity.

On the kerb by the last bus sits a young man in a plain white shirt, hands on his knees, no bag, no ticket. You have seen him before, at the edges of things. "Not travelling?" He smiles as if you had asked something kind. "I'm not going anywhere. Sorry." A conductor comes down the line swinging a Ghana-must-go, and steps round him without a glance, the way you step round a post you have known for years. When the bus pulls out and the dust settles, the kerb is a kerb.

The last bus goes. The park is suddenly a very large piece of ground. {{ROOMIE}} kicks a sachet. The campus you walk back into has the same buildings and about a tenth of the people, and your footsteps have an echo you have never noticed.

#### M072 · Lights on the Long Road
**When:** Y3 · compressed exam weeks (three), then the last Saturday · 16:00–20:00. **Where:** eng: the exam halls; hostel: the New Site road and gate. **Who:** Shamsu, Bolaji, Nusaiba, Jega, {{ROOMIE}}, Emily, Iya Risi, Dr. Okonkwo, Dr. Zainab, the Maker, Muhammad. **Special helpers (if enabled):** none.

End-of-year cliffhanger.

**Story.** An exam hall clock. An invigilator's cough. Then three weeks go by in a montage of pens.

Two semesters of examinations in three weeks: the compressed timetable is the hardest run of papers in the game, most days two, with no gap to forget one before the next. This is where the year's choices are counted. Shamsu's carton of notes sits on your desk with coloured tabs, and each paper's hint system is his handwriting in the margin ("Simple. See M3 tutorial. You know this."). If you taught well in his study group, you remember what you taught. If you loaded him with the whole project in week one, he is thinner in the next seat, and passes anyway, and you feel it. Nobody who did the work fails.

Then the last Saturday: demo day. Dr. Okonkwo walks the New Site road with a clipboard and forty lamp poles lie in a row on the verge, from every group in the class, yours among them, with Bolaji's joints and Asabe's footings and your solder. Plant them. Pole by pole, down the two kilometres where you once rode in a convoy with your phone torch out: dig, drop, plumb, backfill, connect. And at each pole, somebody you helped this year throws the switch: Mubarak (with a small speech about premium photons), Aminu on Jega's shoulders, Hajiya Ladi, Kaka, who has walked in from the forest edge for it, Nurse Hauwa, Lawal, who greets the pole. Iya Risi from the New Site canteen follows the whole line with a bowl of water, blessing each lamp individually and telling it to behave. "You will shine. You will not embarrass me. You hear?"

Dr. Zainab is at the fortieth pole, the last, by the New Site gate. She has come from the town clinic in the mobile keke. She throws the switch, and while the bulb is still warming she says, to the lamp: "I have an offer. A fellowship. Paediatrics. In Seoul."

You have known since the clinic. You can say you knew, or say it is wonderful, or say *go*, or say *stay*. The right thing and the wrong thing get the same answer, which is very quiet and very sharp: "I haven't decided. Don't decide for me." Then, because she is who she is: "Drink water. You've been digging."

Dusk. Forty lamps come up along the long road, one after another, like a sentence being finished. Dr. Okonkwo initials her clipboard and allows that it is "adequate, which from me is poetry".

You walk the road back alone, to see it. Under the last lamp, the one Zainab lit, two figures are standing together, talking: an old lab coat and a white shirt. They are the same height. One of them is speaking with his hands; the other is listening with his head down. It is the only time you ever see it.

When you reach the lamp the Maker is alone. He is outside the garden again and seems not to have noticed. "Who were you talking to?"

"An old argument." He looks up the lit road. "Forty. Good." He walks away toward the campus without another word.

Taped to the pole, at eye height, is a photograph. Black and white, curled at one corner. This road, forty years ago: the same bend, the same gate. Forty poles, new, unlit, wires not yet strung. And one young man in a white shirt, standing exactly where you are standing, looking at the camera as if he knew you would be along.
