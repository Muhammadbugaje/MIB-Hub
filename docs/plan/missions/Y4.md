#### M73 · IT Letter
**When:** Y4 · S1 Wk 1 · Mon–Tue (lecture days) · 08:00–16:00, office hours. **Where:** admin: Registry, Bursary, Senate basement archive; eng: the HOD's office. **Who:** Mrs. Adebayo, Mr. Clement, Dr. Khadija (now HOD), Malam Garba, Muhammad. **Special helpers (if enabled):** none.

**Mystery:** plants `MS-19` (he holds a door; a file cover with his first name and nothing in it); plants `C-DOOR7` (ajar by a hand's width; Garba shuts it with his foot); calls back `C-PHOTO`.

**Story.** Fourth year begins where first year did: in a queue outside the Registry. The difference is that you are at the front of it, and you have brought a folding chair. Freshers stare at the chair the way pilgrims stare at a relic.

You need an industrial training letter, and the letter needs four stamps: Registry (your file), Bursary (your fees), the Head of Department (your placement), and the Senate archive (a seal nobody can explain, kept in the basement by Malam Garba). Three years ago this was a boss fight. Now your Street sense lets you skip one whole queue by knowing which window opens early, and you greet the gateman by his children's names.

At the Bursary, Mr. Clement looks up and opens his mouth. "Network," you say, with him, in unison, and sit down on your chair. He is quietly impressed. It comes back in eleven minutes; you had budgeted forty.

Dr. Khadija has a new door plate, HEAD OF DEPARTMENT, and the same voice. "My office. Now. Bring yourself." This time it is to choose. Three placements: the Works department (Engr. Aliyu's yard), the teaching hospital's power house, or the Ring Road FM transmitter. The work will be the same grid seen from three doors; the uniform and one running joke differ. She signs before you finish the sentence. "I have been waiting three years to give you paper that is not a query."

Back at the Registry, Mrs. Adebayo hunts for your file among towers of them, and because the photograph from the last lamp pole is still folded in your pocket, you ask, casually, for a friend's. Surname unknown. First name Muhammad. She frowns, goes away for a long time, and comes back with a file cover: old, soft at the corners, the name typed on a label gone the colour of tea. She opens it. Nothing. Not a form, not a staple hole. "Graduated? Withdrew? …It doesn't say. It doesn't say anything. How odd." She says it without any interest at all, slides it back on to a shelf, and a minute later asks what you wanted, apart from your own file.

Someone is holding the door for you on the way out. White shirt. Clean shoes on a laterite path. "Sorry," he says, gently, as if the empty folder were his fault. "They never do find it." A clerk walks between you with a tray of tea, and the door is simply swinging.

The last stamp is underground. Malam Garba inks the seal like a man loading a cannon, and stamps. Behind his shoulder, at the end of the basement corridor, the steel door with the painted 7 stands open by the width of a hand. Warm air comes out of it. Garba follows your eyes, hooks the door with his foot and closes it without turning round.

"Hm."

#### M74 · First Day at Work
**When:** Y4 · S1 Wk 2 · Mon–Fri (lecture days) · 08:00–16:00. **Where:** your placement; admin: the Works yard. **Who:** Engr. Aliyu (your supervisor), Abbas, Foreman Ikenna. **Special helpers (if enabled):** none.

**Story.** Monday, 08:00. The Works yard smells of diesel and wet cement. Engr. Aliyu, "Change-Over", walks toward you holding something across both palms like a ceremonial sword. It is a broom.

"Every engineer I respect started here," he says. He does not say where he restarted.

The week is a real job in five small days. Monday: the broom, and Foreman Ikenna's first errand, delivered with a face of perfect stone: "Go to the store. Bring me the left-handed screwdriver." You look for twenty minutes. Abbas, replacing a contactor nearby, watches the whole search without blinking, then says, "There is also a bucket of earth current. But that one is heavy." Tuesday: a clipboard, and a walk along the 11 kV feeder with Aliyu naming every pole like a headmaster taking a register. Wednesday: meter readings, the same meters you crept past in second year, read aloud now, in daylight, with a signature. Thursday: a fault. A transformer by New Site is running hot; trace it on the switchboard, isolate, re-route. Friday: a spanner, and the first bolt on this campus that is tight because of you.

Each afternoon you fill in your logbook. The game offers two entries every day: the honest one ("Swept the yard. Was sent for a screwdriver that does not exist.") and the padded one ("Conducted inventory audit of specialised hand tools."). Nothing happens when you choose. The book only remembers.

Aliyu is a careful, humbled teacher. He shows you the grid as it really is: where it is strong, where somebody tapped it in 1994 and nobody has dared touch it since, where it leaks. "Light does not disappear," he says. "It is always going somewhere. Find where." The new idea arrives quietly on Thursday: open your map and there is a new layer, the campus drawn in current, feeders glowing by load. You can read the place the way he does.

Friday, after closing, he unrolls the feeder map on the bonnet of the Works van. On it he has drawn, in pencil, a line of his own: six points joined in a ring. Three are inked over, by hand, more recently. In the centre, where a seventh mark might go, the paper is thin from rubbing out.

"I have been thinking about what you people are doing," he says. He does not look up. "I took an envelope once, and the dark got longer for everybody. I would like to help. Properly, this time."

Abbas, passing with the toolbox, slows down. "My name," he tells the new intern from Mechanical, who has just called him John Carter, "is Abbas." Nobody hears him. It is a working yard.
