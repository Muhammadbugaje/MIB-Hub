# Freshers School Life — Story Research

What the best-loved story games do, and what Freshers School Life (FSLG) should take from them.

**How this was put together.** The craft lessons in Part B come from published talks, interviews and
essays, which are linked. The survey in Part A is a writer's survey: one line on what each game's
storytelling is known for and the technique worth borrowing, written from general knowledge of the
games and their critical reception. It is not a claim to have re-researched each title online for this
document, and it makes no claims about sales figures. Nothing in FSLG copies a plot, character, line of
dialogue, mission name or set piece from any of these games; the point is technique.

Contents

- Part A. The survey: 126 games, grouped by the lesson they teach
- Part B. Twelve craft lessons in depth, with sources
- Part C. Nigerian and African storytelling to draw on
- Part D. What not to do
- Part E. Sources

## Part B. Twelve craft lessons

### B1. Make the player want the story

CD Projekt Red's quest director Paweł Sasko gave a talk on what the studio learned from two large
role-playing games. The first lesson: hold back a key piece of information so the player has to reach
for the next part. As the write-up quotes him, "We want the players to want the story." Related lessons
from the same talk: make emotional impact a goal; give big moments room; use small human gestures;
cut repeated information; build dilemmas; set up stakes before a choice; show consequences, sometimes
when the player has forgotten; and do not over-design.
[Game Developer: 10 key takeaways](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/10-key-takeaways-from-the-quest-design-of-cyberpunk-2077-and-the-witcher-3)

For FSLG: every mission ends by showing one new thing and hiding one. The cable is numbered 2. Two of
what? (m45.) The mission style guide in the bible turns each of Sasko's points into a rule.

### B2. No fetch quest without a turn

The same studio is known for refusing plain fetch quests and for treating side content with the same
care as the main line; one of its designers has said they reject most pitches. Reported in
[MCV: declaring war on fetch quests](https://mcvuk.com/the-witcher-3-quest-designers-talk-balancing-story-with-gameplay-and-declaring-war-on-fetch-quests/),
[Shacknews: quest designers on process](https://www.shacknews.com/article/94509/witcher-3-quest-designers-talk-design-process-most-difficult-quest-to-create)
and [Yahoo/PC Gamer: rejecting over 90% of pitches](https://www.yahoo.com/tech/cd-projekts-side-quests-good-004000304.html).

For FSLG: rule 4 in the style guide ("the turn"). A mission in which nothing changes between accepting
it and finishing it is demoted to an ambient encounter, where "buy me bread" is allowed to be just that.

### B3. Balance the serious with the silly, on purpose

The director of the Like a Dragon series has described the main story and side stories as a balance of
emotions: when the main line is heavy, the side stories around it are light, and the reverse. He also
compared side content to the other dishes that earn a restaurant its star. A colleague has said the
balance is less a rule than a shared instinct; both can be true.
[PC Gamer: sidequests and the Michelin star](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/like-a-dragon-series-director-sums-up-why-sidequests-are-essential-to-a-great-rpg-with-a-single-metaphor-a-good-main-dish-alone-will-not-earn-you-a-michelin-star),
[GameSpot interview](https://www.gamespot.com/amp-articles/yakuza-like-a-dragon-dev-explains-creating-a-new-hero-and-whats-next/1100-6489092/),
[Automaton: predetermined slots for kinds of substory](https://automaton-media.com/en/interviews/like-a-dragon-infinite-wealths-sicko-snap-was-created-to-solve-a-very-real-problem-during-development/)

For FSLG: the pacing chart plans light missions at every tenth number and directly after every heavy
beat. Side missions around the strike year are the funniest in the game; side missions around the
fame year are the kindest.

### B4. Let the school year be the spine

Writing about the best-known school sandbox keeps returning to the same points: lessons are mini-games
that give you something, the clock makes attendance a choice, the year gives the story seasons, and
cliques have turf and character.
[PC Gamer: school life](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/school-life-in-rockstars-bully),
[PC Gamer: a game with heart](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/reinstall-bully),
[GamesRadar: the cliques](https://www.gamesradar.com/bully-the-cliques)

For FSLG: five years, ten semesters, each ending in exams. Timetable and promotion already exist; the
outline hangs every act on them. Factions replace cliques and none is tied to tribe or faith.

### B5. Time is the scarce thing, and bonds are the reward

Analysis of the Persona series points to its calendar: days are divided into slots, each friend is free
only at certain times, and so who you spend an afternoon with is a real choice. The friendship system
also says the game's theme out loud: bonds make you stronger. Critics note a tension when the efficient
route and the heartfelt route differ.
[Game Developer: S-Links to Confidants](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/s-links-to-confidants-an-evolution-of-character-interaction-),
[GDC narrative review (Persona 4)](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/GameNarrativeReview/Carpenter_Game%20Narrative%20Review.pdf),
[First Person Scholar: Life Will Change](https://www.firstpersonscholar.com/?p=6899)

For FSLG: five friendship scenes per friend, unlocked by time together, with the fifth revealing a
secret that changes a later mission. To avoid the "optimal route" problem, no ending needs every
friendship maxed, and the single path is complete in itself.

### B6. A world that asks for help

Reviews of open-world westerns credit strangers and road encounters with making the world feel as if it
goes on without you: someone flags you down, a small thing happens, and long rides stop feeling empty.
Critics also warn that weak ones feel like busywork.
[GamesBeat review](https://gamesbeat.com/red-dead-redemption-2-review-goodbye-to-the-american-west/2/),
[GameSpot hands-on (2010)](https://gamespot.com/6260497/),
[PC Gamer: NPCs lead interesting lives](https://pcgamer.com/red-dead-redemption-2-npcs-lead-interesting-lives)

A talk on a later open-world series described chaining side quests to recurring people so they add up
to a relationship rather than a list.
[The Loadout: side quest formula](https://www.theloadout.com/horizon-forbidden-west/side-quest-formula-gdc)

For FSLG: thirty ambient templates (outline, section 9) with rules against busywork: a person not a
marker, one objective, always declinable, a twist one time in five, and people who remember. Fifty side
missions come in short chains per person.

### B7. Reactivity: the game is paying attention

The creative director of Hades has described the approach as asking what the characters would talk
about and what they would notice, then writing those lines, so the player feels the game is paying
attention.
[GameSpot: script philosophy](https://gamespot.com/articles/hades-writer-explains-script-philosophy-after-player-discovers-incredible-detail/1100-6482650/),
[Game Developer: narrative rewards](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death)

For FSLG: cheap reactivity first. A friend comments on your outfit once a day; the twins repeat what
you did yesterday; the tea seller knows where you were. The bible's "Around it" block (two radio lines,
two overheard lines, one phone call) is how every mission leaves a trace in the world.

### B8. Branch a little, remember a lot

Writers of interactive fiction describe a common shape: the story branches and then rejoins at fixed
beats, and what keeps choices from being erased is state: the game remembers and refers back. Heavy
early branching explodes; delayed consequences are cheap and feel deep.
[Sam Kabo Ashwell: standard patterns](https://www.ifwiki.org/Standard_Patterns_in_Choice-Based_Games),
[sub-Q: the branch and the merge](https://sub-q.com/making-interactive-fiction-the-branch-and-the-merge/),
[Emily Short: beyond branching](https://emshort.blog/2016/04/12/beyond-branching-quality-based-and-salience-based-narrative-structures/)

For FSLG: no branching plot. About ten remembered choices, five standing tracks, and lines that read
them back. The sign-out shirt (m197) is the purest case: one scene, assembled from five years of flags.

### B9. Structure for a long game

Long games sag in the middle. The common cures: acts with their own question and villain; a change of
routine partway (new place, new rules); rising and falling intensity; and regular arrivals of new toys.
One open-world studio described building a mission structure specifically to keep players oriented in a
big world.
[Ubisoft Toronto on Far Cry 6](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/telling-stories-and-creating-character-arcs-in-i-far-cry-6-i-s-open-world),
[Game Developer: should we ditch missions?](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/narrative-design-on-open-worlds-should-we-ditch-missions-)

For FSLG: each year changes the rules. Year 2 adds money and the mall. Year 3 takes the timetable
away. Year 4 puts you in a workplace and on camera. Year 5 gives you one long project. Section 11 of the
outline lists what is new in each.

### B10. Guide without a forest of icons

Designers of recent open worlds have talked about giving players meaningful reasons to wander and about
"icon soup": maps so full of markers that nobody explores.
[GDC session: meaningful reasons to wander](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/meaningful-reasons-to-wander-creating-open-world-engagement-in-ghost-of-ytei/914335),
[Final Boss: tired of icon soup](https://finalboss.io/players-dont-explore-anymore-ex-rockstar-devs-gdc)

For FSLG: one main objective on screen. Side missions and encounters start from people calling out.
Streetlamps, music changes and crowds do the pointing.

### B11. Music is a place, and radio is a voice

Two families of technique. *Horizontal*: move between sections of music as the situation changes.
*Vertical*: keep one piece playing and add or remove layers. Games often mix them, and can blend music
that exists in the world (a radio) with score.
[Game Developer: hybrid horizontal and vertical structure](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/hybrid-horizontal-vertical-structure-for-game-music-composers-from-spyder-to-sackboy-gdc-2021-),
[Game Developer: diegetic music](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/hybrid-dynamic-diegetic-music-for-game-music-composers-from-spyder-to-sackboy-gdc-2021-),
[Resident Cryptid: interactive music systems](https://residentcryptid.substack.com/p/creating-interactive-music-systems)

On radio: presenters, adverts and phone-ins make a city feel bigger than the map.
[TheGamer: radio stations](https://www.thegamer.com/grand-theft-auto-radio-stations/),
[Red Bull: a soundtrack revolution](https://www.redbull.com/gb-en/rockstar-games-soundtrack-revolution)

On silence: one celebrated open-world score uses short piano phrases and long quiet so the world's own
sounds lead.
[A Closer Listen](https://acloserlisten.com/2018/04/03/press-a-the-music-of-zelda-breath-of-the-wild/),
[Zelda Dungeon: listening to emptiness](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/musical-musings-listening-closely-to-the-emptiness-of-breath-of-the-wilds-hyrule-field-day/)

For FSLG: `04_MUSIC.md` applies all three. Place-and-time themes already exist; the document adds
layers, leitmotifs, a radio that changes by act, and planned silences.

### B12. Endings are goodbyes

The endings people remember are mostly partings: a last talk with each companion, a final walk through
places that have changed, a quiet scene after the loud one. FSLG's last ten missions are built that way:
results, gown, parents, ceremony, shirts, handing over your room, a last ride, and one line at the gate.

The search found nothing specific on campus-drama conventions in games. The following is the writer's
own summary of conventions familiar from Nigerian screen and stage comedy and campus life, offered as
craft notes, not as cited research:

1. **The comic pair.** Two contrasting friends, one schemer and one worrier. FSLG: you and your roommate;
   the twins; Pastor Sam and Ustaz Idris.
2. **The elder with the proverb.** A moral centre who speaks rarely. FSLG: Malam Garba; Baba Dam; Kaka.
3. **The madam or oga with a heart.** Loud, commanding, secretly generous. FSLG: Mama Nkechi; Hajiya Ladi.
4. **The hustle.** Everyone has a side business. FSLG: Year 3's hustles; Emeka's kekes; Ada's app.
5. **NEPA as a character.** Light going and coming punctuates scenes. FSLG's whole mystery.
6. **The moral, lightly.** Stories end with a lesson, said plainly and then undercut with a joke.
7. **Big occasions.** Weddings, naming ceremonies, matriculation, convocation, sign-out: dress, food,
   dance. FSLG: m115, m177, m196, m197.
8. **Parents by phone.** "Have you eaten?" as a refrain.
9. **Bureaucracy.** Clearance, "come back tomorrow", the network being down.
10. **Respect.** Titles, greetings and kneeling or bowing are not decoration; they are how scenes begin.

What to keep out: campus stories about cults, "runs", exam malpractice rings and lecturers behaving
badly are common in film. They are excluded from FSLG entirely (see the bible, section 3).

## Part D. What not to do

1. **Do not copy.** No plot, character, mission, line or set piece from any game above. Borrow method.
2. **Do not pad to a number.** Two hundred missions only work if each has one new thing. Where an
   outline entry cannot find its "one new thing" during scripting, cut it and promote a side mission.
3. **Do not fill the map with icons.** One objective; people call you.
4. **Do not make romance a vending machine.** Gifts in, affection out, is the failure case. Standings,
   listening and time are the inputs. "No" is final for the week.
5. **Do not punish declining.** A player who walks past an encounter should never feel told off.
6. **Do not let the main quest shout "urgent" while offering fifty distractions.** Each act gives the
   mystery natural pauses (exams, the strike, industrial training) so wandering makes sense.
7. **Do not explain the mystery to death.** Entropy stays a principle with a face.
8. **Do not repeat information.** If the Maker explained it, the tea seller jokes about it instead.
9. **Do not write lines that cannot be voiced.** Short, fixed, speakable.
10. **Do not make failure a wall.** Lost races, failed tests and caught-by-rangers all continue the story.
11. **Do not make any group the joke.** Not a tribe, a faith, an accent, a body or a bank balance.
12. **Do not build systems for one mission.** Every new system in the outline is used at least three
    times; the rhythm mini-game is used nine.
