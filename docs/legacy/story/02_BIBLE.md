## 1. Premise and promise

You arrive at a big Nigerian university as a fresher who knows nobody. Five academic years later you
walk out of the Senate forecourt in a gown, and half the school knows your name.

In between, the light keeps going off. Every time it does, something moves in the dark. The school
calls it NEPA. The Maker, the quiet man who keeps the Tesla coil in the Engineering Garden, calls it
Entropy: the rule that everything runs down unless somebody puts energy in.

The game's promise to the player, in one line: **this school is alive, it remembers you, and it gets
brighter because you were here.**

What a session should feel like:

- Wake in your hostel, check the timetable, and already three people want something from you.
- The main story is always one clear next step, never a list of chores.
- Something funny happens within five minutes.
- Something kind happens within fifteen.
- When the light goes, the mood changes at once, and you know what to do about it.

## 2. Themes

One theme per year, each one a thing real students go through. The year's villain is that theme with
a face on it.

| Year | Level | Theme | The question the year asks | The face of Entropy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | Arriving | Can a stranger belong here? | Count Entropy (darkness) |
| 2 | 200 | Money | What will you do for it, and what won't you? | Iron Mama (the stolen light) |
| 3 | 300 | Drift | Who are you when nobody is making you get up? | The Long Silence (the strike) |
| 4 | 400 | Image | Is the person they clap for actually you? | The Mirror (the Echoes) |
| 5 | 500 | Leaving | Can you finish something and let it go? | Chancellor Null (staying forever) |

The answer is the same every year: **light is something people do together.** No coil is ever lit by
one person. Every finale needs the crew, and the crew gets bigger each year.

Secondary themes writers may lean on:

- Dignity of ordinary work: the porter, the tea seller, the shuttle driver, the nurse.
- Faith as community: the mosque and the chapel are places where people look after each other.
- Being broke with grace. Money is always short and nobody is shamed for it.
- Seniors owe freshers a welcome. The first and last missions are the same walk in opposite directions.

## 3. Tone and humour rules

Warm, quick, a little cheeky. The world laughs with people, not at them.

Do:

- Find comedy in systems: queues, clearance forms, "come back tomorrow", NEPA timing, garri economics.
- Let small people win. The gate man outsmarts the big man.
- Undercut drama with something practical. A boss fight ends and somebody asks who is paying for suya.
- Give every named character one thing they love that has nothing to do with the plot.
- Keep sentences short. A line that needs a second breath is too long for a speech bubble.

Do not:

- No real people, living or dead, and no real lecturers, officials, pastors, imams, politicians or musicians.
- No real brands. Invent them (see section 5).
- No cults, confraternities or secret societies, not even as villains. The dark things are shades, not students.
- No sexual content, no suggestive camera, no innuendo about bodies. Romance is hand-holding, talking and time.
- No alcohol, drugs, gambling for money or cigarettes as things the player does or is rewarded for.
- No tribal, religious or regional jokes at anyone's expense. Jokes about food rivalry (jollof) are fine.
- No death on screen. People get knocked down, get up and complain. Villains are machines, shadows and ideas.
- No exam malpractice shown as clever. Anyone who cheats is caught by their own foolishness.
- No bribery shown as the smart route. Guards can be distracted, persuaded or outrun, never paid off.
- No real political parties, elections outside campus, or security agencies. Campus security only.

Violence level: slapstick. Slippers, shoves, cartoon stars. A fight ends with "don't try that again".

Sadness is allowed and should be earned twice a year: a goodbye, a failure, a friend who drifts. Keep it
short, let it breathe (see `04_MUSIC.md` on silence), then let somebody crack a joke.

## 4. Language guide

Base register is clear Nigerian English. Flavour comes from rhythm and a small set of shared words, so a
player in Kaduna, Lagos or Enugu all feel at home and a player elsewhere still follows.

- Pidgin: light, never a whole paragraph. "How far?", "Abeg", "Wetin happen?", "No wahala", "I dey come",
  "Na so", "E don do", "Belle", "Wahala", "Sharp sharp", "Oga", "Madam", "Chop".
- Hausa: "Sannu", "Yaya dai?", "Na gode", "Toh", "Kai!", "Wallahi" (as exclamation only), "Mai shayi",
  "Malam", "Hajiya", "Ranka ya daɗe" (to elders), "Sai anjima".
- Yoruba: "E kaaro", "E se", "O ti o!", "Abi?", "Sha", "Jare", "Oya".
- Igbo: "Kedu?", "Daalu", "Nna", "Nne", "Chai!", "Biko".
- Campus words: fresher, stalite (returning student), staylite (lives on campus), carry-over, spill-over,
  handout, clearance, course rep, HOD, level adviser, sign-out, IT (industrial training), logbook, CGPA,
  bed space, squatting, up NEPA, they have taken light, TDB (till day break), aluta, congress.

Rules:

1. Any non-English word must be clear from context in the same line.
2. Each character leans on one flavour (listed on their sheet), never three in one sentence.
3. Elders are addressed with titles. Nobody calls Malam Garba by his first name.
4. The player character's lines stay neutral so every cast choice can say them.
5. Maximum 90 characters per spoken line, 3 lines per speaker turn, so lines fit bubbles and can be voiced.
6. Every spoken line is fixed text in a table, because the owner is recording voices line by line.

Rules for writers:

- Factions are never evil. They want reasonable things that collide.
- No faction is tied to an ethnic group or religion. Every hall and department is mixed.
- A mission that raises one faction's standing should, about one time in four, cost a little with another.

## 7. The mystery: Entropy and the six coils

This is the spine. Writers must not contradict it.

### 7.1 What is true

1. Long ago a professor, **Adaeze Nwachukwu**, and her students built a ring of six resonant coils
   around the school. Lit together, they hold a steady field that keeps the grid stable. The six plaques
   in the Engineering Garden name the six sites.
2. The first laboratory was in what is now forest: Old Campus. It failed, the school moved, and the ring
   was never completed. Only the Garden coil was kept running, by one student who stayed behind.
3. That student is **the Maker**. He never graduated. He has kept one coil alive for longer than anyone
   has counted, because he believes that if he leaves, the light goes for good.
4. **Entropy** is not a person. It is what happens in the dark when nobody is putting energy in. It takes
   the shape of whatever is running down. In Year 1 it wore a cape and called itself a Count because the
   freshers were afraid of the dark and gave it a face.
5. **Shades** are pieces of it. They are not ghosts and not dead people. Light scatters them. Noise,
   company and movement weaken them. They never speak. Nobody is ever killed by one; people who are
   caught are found sitting very still, tired and cold, and recover with food, light and friends.
6. Each year Entropy finds a new way in, and each year one more coil is found and lit:

| Year | Coil | Site | Entropy's way in | Its face | How it is beaten |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First | Engineering Garden | Blackouts | Count Entropy | The crew holds the coil; the school dances |
| 2 | Second | Under the Mall | Stolen power | Iron Mama, the basement generator | Unplugged; the feeder reconnected |
| 3 | Third | Old Campus, in the forest | An empty campus | The Long Silence | Noise, football, an open mic |
| 4 | Fourth and fifth | Airfield tower; the Dam spillway | Rumour and image | The Mirror, king of the Echoes | Doing what a copy cannot: helping someone |
| 5 | Sixth | Under the Senate tower | Fear of endings | Chancellor Null | Finishing, and letting the Maker go |

7. The Year 1 story already in the game (missions m1 to m36) stays as written. Its finale, "Convocation
   night", is the seniors' convocation that the fresher saves. Two existing lines need a light edit so the
   long story holds: Malam Garba's "Go and dance, graduate" becomes "Go and dance, fresher no more", and
   "for good" in the step text becomes "for tonight". See `03_OUTLINE.md`, note A1.

### 7.2 What the player learns, and when

| End of | The player knows |
|---|---|
| Year 1 | The dark has a shape. One coil pushes it back. The Maker knows more than he says. |
| Year 2 | Somebody was selling the school's light. There is a second coil. The plaques are a map. |
| Year 3 | The ring was a class project that failed because its makers fell apart. The Maker was one of them. |
| Year 4 | Entropy can wear your own face. Two more coils. The Maker is tired. |
| Year 5 | The ring needs a keeper only until it is complete. Completing it frees him. It needs everyone. |

### 7.3 Shade types

| Type | Year | Looks like | Behaviour | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blackout shade | 1 | A dark smudge with two dim eyes | Drifts toward lone people | Torchlight; hits |
| Hummer | 2 | A shade with a rattling metal chest | Guards cables; charges in straight lines | Sidestep; unplugging things |
| Idler | 3 | A seated grey figure | Does not chase; anyone near it slows down and sits | Noise, music, being pulled up by a friend |
| Echo | 4 | A blurred copy of a person | Copies the last move it saw | Doing something unexpected; kindness nearby |
| Null | 5 | A blank outline in a gown | Erases lamps and signs it touches | Lit coils; crowds; your name being called |

Bosses already in the game stay: Big Sly (human rival, reformed after m9), the Dam Eel (returns in Year 4
as a guardian to be calmed, not beaten), Count Entropy.

### 7.4 What stays mysterious

Never explain where Entropy comes from, never give it a backstory or a voice, and never say what the
Maker's real name is. The plaque for him reads only "The Maker".

## 10. Relationships

### 10.1 Friendship

Every named student has a friendship value from 0 to 100, shown as five steps: Stranger, Hi-hi,
Friend, Close, Family. It rises with time spent (walking together, classes together, missions, food
shared, help given) and falls slowly with neglect and sharply with betrayal (standing them up, fighting
their friends).

Each friend has **five friendship scenes**, one per step, unlocked in order, each a two-to-four minute
scene at a place that suits them. Scene five is always the same shape: they tell you their secret, and
it changes how one later mission plays.

**Best friend.** At any time one Close friend can be named best friend (they ask; you accept). The best
friend gets extra lines in every finale and is the first to notice when your standing drops. You can
have one. Changing it is allowed and noticed.

### 10.2 Romance

Ground rules, which are not negotiable:

1. Romance is optional. A player who never dates loses no story, no missions and no ending. The single
   path has its own scenes (the best friend fills the dinner, the dance and the photo).
2. Revision 3: the full lists are in `CHARACTERS_BACKSTORY.md`, section 7. Earlier text: a male player may court Ada, Fatima, Joy or Maryam. A female player may court Yakubu,
   Kelechi, AB or Basit. (A fifth for women players, **Micheal**, a Drama Club actor, is outlined in
   `03_OUTLINE.md` side missions so both lists have five.)
3. Nobody is won by pestering. An interest who says "not now" means it. Asking again inside a week
   lowers friendship. Following, gifting after a "no", or fighting their friends ends the route.
4. Everything is age-appropriate: walks, food, study dates, the Love Garden bench, holding hands at the
   dinner, a forehead touch at most. The camera never lingers.
5. Each interest has reasons to like you that are about who you are in the world (your standings), not
   about gifts. Gifts help only when they show you listened.

**Money.** The owner asked for partners who leave when you are broke. Handle it truthfully and kindly:
being broke never ends a relationship on its own. What ends it is hiding it, borrowing and not
repaying, or missing every plan because you are hustling. Each interest has a "broke" scene (last
column) that deepens things if you are honest. One interest per sex (Basit for female players, Lyzzy for male players) is written as the one

**Two-timing.** Allowed, never rewarded. Dating two people starts a hidden clock. The twins notice
first. Outcomes, by how it comes out: you confess (one may forgive after a cooling-off; standing drops a

back together is possible once, needs time and a real change (a standing they value has risen), and has
its own scene.

**A partner can leave you** for cause: three missed dates, a public fight with their friend, a lie
discovered, or fame going to your head in Year 4. They say why. It is never random.

## 12. Ambient encounters

Wherever you go, somebody needs something. These are short (30 seconds to 3 minutes), optional, and
generated from templates so they never run out. They are the main way the Community and Street tracks
move, and the main way the school feels alive.

Design rules:

1. An encounter starts with a person, never a marker. They call out, wave, or are visibly stuck.
2. The player can always walk on. Declining costs nothing the first time; the person asks someone else,
   and you may see that happen.
3. One objective. No encounter chains unless the player asks ("anything else?").
4. Every template has at least three variations of who, where and why, a twist that appears one time in
   five, and distinct success, fail and decline outcomes.
5. People remember. The second time you help the same walker they greet you by name; the fifth time they
   offer something back.
6. Nobody is ever helpless for comedy. A blind student asking to be walked to class is competent,
   funny and in charge of the conversation.
7. No more than one new offer every three minutes, none during main missions' timed sections, fights,
   chases or moments.

The thirty templates are listed in `03_OUTLINE.md`, section 9. The families:

- **Errands**: buy me, fetch me, deliver for me, charge my phone, find my lecture hall.
- **Lifts**: drop me at Phase 2, take me to the gate, I am late for a test.
- **Escort**: walk me past the dark stretch, guide me (blind student, new fresher, visiting parent).
- **First aid**: fell off a bike, fainted in the sun, twisted ankle at the court → Sick Bay, on foot
  carefully or by vehicle gently.
- **Teach me**: one question from your courses; ride a bicycle; a dance step; how to use the shuttle.
- **Lost and found**: ID card, slippers, a goat from the staff quarters, a flash drive with a project on it.
- **Matchmaking**: carry a note, find out if she likes him, sit with me while I ask.
- **Disputes**: two people, one bucket; a queue-jumper; a debt of ₦200; whose turn at the socket.
- **Company**: sit with me till my shuttle comes; watch my bag; come and cheer.

The game already has people who fight back by temperament (timid, normal, tough). The story adds a
crowd with opinions.

When a fight starts, each bystander within sight picks a role by temperament and by your standings:

| Role | Share | What they do | Lines (examples) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Watcher | 35% | Stops, stares, steps back | "Ah ah!" "Wetin happen?" |
| Filmer | 15% | Phone up; walks a slow circle | "Wait, wait, I'm recording." |
| Laugher | 10% | Laughs, shakes head, walks on | "Two of you no get work?" |

- High Street: more Hype at the court, fewer Callers.

- No main mission requires the player to hit an ordinary student. Human opponents in the story are
  bullies who started it, or sparring by agreement.

## 14. Mission script style guide

Every main mission is one section in a year file under `scripts/`, in this layout. Keep to 35–60 lines
each. Every spoken line ends with an empty voice id, `[v: ]`, to be filled when it is recorded.

```
### mNN — Title
- Act / semester: 
- Level gate: 
- Shape: (one of: delivery, chase, tail, stealth, race, quiz, escort, prank, investigation, rhythm,
  sports, flight, boss, social, choice, build, defend, collect, routine, convoy, care)
- Giver: 
- Places: 
- Time of day / weather: 
- Needs: (missions done, vehicles, items, systems; mark anything not yet built as NEW)
- Music: (cue ids from 04_MUSIC.md)

**Premise.** One or two sentences. What happens and why the player cares.

**Why now.** What in the story makes this the next thing.

**Beats**
1. [step type] Objective text as shown on the HUD (max 60 characters).
   What happens. Then lines:
   - SPEAKER: Line. (max 90 characters, max 3 lines per turn)
2. …
(4 to 7 beats. One beat is the "turn": the plan changes.)

**Choice.** (if any) The options as the player sees them; what each sets.

**If it goes wrong.** Fail states and what the game says. Failing is never a dead end: say how it retries.

**Rewards.** Cash, XP, items, unlocks, standing changes (+ or − on named tracks), friendship changes.

**Sets / pays.** Flags this mission sets for later, and earlier flags it pays off.

**Around it.** Two radio lines, two overheard lines from walkers, one phone call or message.
```

Rules that keep 120 missions from blurring:

1. **One new thing.** Every mission teaches, shows or changes exactly one new thing: a place, a person, a
   mechanic, or a fact.
2. **No two neighbours alike.** Adjacent missions never share a shape. The outline already alternates.
3. **Start in motion.** The first beat is doable within ten seconds of accepting.
4. **The turn.** One beat where the plan changes: the light goes, the person is not there, the easy job
   is not easy. No turn, no mission; make it a side mission.
5. **Objective text is a verb and a place.** "Take the fuel to the Sick Bay", never "Proceed".
6. **Say it once.** If two characters would explain the same thing, one of them says something else.
7. **Leave room to feel.** After a finale beat, one quiet beat with no objective pressure.
8. **Choices are dilemmas between two goods** (or two costs), set up before they are asked, and they
   come back later at least once, ideally when the player has forgotten.
9. **Fail forward.** A failed race is a loss scene and a rematch, not a reload.
10. **Use existing step types first**: talk, go, eat, class, sleep, ride, fight, find, race, job, buy,
    emote, stat. Mark new ones NEW and describe them in a line (tail, stealth, rhythm, build, choice,
    escort, defend, convoy, care, carry).
11. **Every tenth mission is light.** Numbers ending in 0 are celebrations, games or days off.
12. **Callbacks.** Each mission either sets something or pays something. The best do both.

Side missions use the same layout at 20–30 lines with 2–4 beats. Ambient templates use the table form
in the outline.

## 16. Continuity rules and banned list

Continuity:

1. Time moves one academic year per act. Characters age, change clothes, change rooms.
2. Kelechi and Captain Uche's best cadets are a year ahead of the player and leave after Year 4.
3. Big Sly is never a villain again after m9.
4. Count Entropy does not appear between m36 and m109. The other faces carry Years 2 to 4.
5. The Maker never leaves the Engineering Garden until m116, with one exception: Malam Garba's
   daughter's wedding (m77), where he stands at the very edge for one minute.
6. The eel is an animal, never a monster again after m31; from Year 4 it is a guardian.
7. Mall power is honest after m56. Do not reopen that plot.
8. Lecturers strike once (Year 3). Do not use a second strike.
9. Nobody in the crew fails out. Bolaji's and Emeka's school stories end in them finding their own way to stay.
10. The hidden character's presence changes two lines only (people say he looks like the Maker).

Never write:

- A real person's name, a real brand, a real song title or lyric, a real political body.
- A mission whose only content is "go here, press E, come back".
- A romance reward for persistence after a "no".
- A joke about someone's faith, tribe, accent, weight, disability or poverty.
- A shade that talks, a death, a weapon that is not a slipper, a torch, a book or a bucket.

## 17. Garri: the humour of being broke

Every student is broke sometimes, and the school's answer is garri. This is a running joke the whole
game shares, and it is always affectionate.

- **The meal.** "Garri and groundnut" is the broke-student dinner: garri soaked in cold water, sugar if
  you have it, groundnuts if you are rich. With milk it is "garri deluxe". With ice it is "garri on the
  rocks". Eating it restores a little food and a little morale and costs almost nothing.
- **The economy.** Garri is currency. "Lend me one cup, I'll pay two on Friday." A roommate's garri
  level is checked the way other people check fuel. A full paint-bucket of garri is "generational wealth".
- **The asking.** Friends ask you for garri by phone, by knocking, and by standing in your doorway
  holding an empty cup and saying nothing. Giving it raises friendship. Having none and saying so is
  never a failure: "We are both in it, then."
- **The calendar.** Week one after allowance: rice. Week two: noodles. Week three: bread. Week four:
  garri. Characters name the week by the food. "It is garri week." "Already?"
- **The dignity.** Nobody is mocked for it. The joke is the situation, never the person. Mama Nkechi
  never lets a student pay for garri; she sells it as "water with ambition" for ₦0 and a greeting.
- **In relationships.** A date on garri week is the best date in the game (m54). A partner who laughs
  with you over one cup and two spoons is a keeper; the script should let the player feel that.

Lines any character may say (fixed text, recordable):

1. "How far? You get garri?"
2. "I'm not hungry. I'm just asking who has garri. For research."
3. "Soak it well. Let it swell. That is how one cup becomes two."
4. "Groundnut? In this economy?"
5. "It is not poverty. It is intermittent feasting."
6. "My account balance is a phone number. A short one."
7. "Garri has never asked me for anything. Garri has never left me."
8. "Sugar has finished. We drink it like men of old."
9. "Who put hot water? Who raised you?"
10. "Allowance has landed. Hide the garri; we may need it again."

Mechanics the story assumes: garri is a cheap food item sold at Hajiya Ladi's and the Phase 2 shops;
friends request it as an ambient encounter (e31, "You get garri?"); the hostel room shows a garri
bucket whose level is the household's shared stock.

## 19. Rules of the world the story must respect

These are how the game now behaves. Scripts must not contradict them.

- **Night.** Lecture halls and departments are locked at night, except scheduled evening tutorials, which
  the player can join. Shops, the mall, food places and the Sick Bay stay open. Missions that need a hall
  at night must be a tutorial, must have a key holder present, or must be a blackout emergency.
- **Focus.** The player does one activity at a time. A mission step that starts a job, a race, a lecture
  or a mini-game must end or pause the previous one in the script ("Finish the delivery first").
- **Jobs.** Taking a job gives the job's vehicle and shows an "on a job" banner. Story missions that use
  job mechanics (m45, m60, m80) do the same and say so in the beat.
- **Crowds.** By day, paths are busy and people are chatty. At night there are fewer people, they keep to
  lit paths, and they walk in pairs. Scripts set at night should use that: emptier, quieter, closer.
- **Map.** The player can zoom the map and set a waypoint. Objectives should name a place the map knows.
- **Wilderness.** Outside the fence the land is endless. A patrol turns the player back, then brings them
  back safely if they go on. Nobody is hurt. Old Campus is the one fixed place out there.
- **Interiors.** Buildings with doors can be walked into. Scenes set indoors should use the room: the
  board, the counter, the bed, the bench.
- **Arcade and dance.** The Game Room has five mini-games; The Floor has a rhythm game. Story missions
  reuse them and do not invent new ones.
- **Wardrobe.** Outfits come from the mall and the MIB Store. People comment once a day. Dress codes in
  scripts are met by owning any outfit with the right tag (native, corporate, sport, formal).
- **Friends missions.** Co-op missions for 2–6 players are separate from the story
  (`05_FRIENDS_MISSIONS.md`) and never change story flags.
