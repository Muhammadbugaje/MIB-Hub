# The Maker's quotations and big words

Private to the owner. The Maker quotes Rumi at the turning points of the story, and shows off with
very large words exactly ten times. This file lists every quotation, where it is used, and its source.

## Rules

1. **Public-domain translations only.** Every Rumi line below is copied word for word from one of two
   translations published in 1898, which are in the public domain:
   - **W** = E. H. Whinfield (tr.), *Masnavi i Ma'navi: The Spiritual Couplets of Maulána Jalálu-'d-Dín
     Muhammad i Rúmí*, abridged, London: Kegan Paul, Trench, Trübner, 1898. Checked against the scan
     text at archive.org, item `masnaviimanavisp00jala_0`.
   - **N** = Reynold A. Nicholson (ed. and tr.), *Selected Poems from the Divani Shamsi Tabriz*,
     Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1898. Checked against the scan text at archive.org, item
     `in.ernet.dli.2015.52959`.
   James W. Redhouse's 1881 *Mesnevi* (Book I) is also public domain and may be used later, but no line
   from it is used yet.
2. **Never** use modern translations or "versions" that are still in copyright, including the popular
   ones by Coleman Barks and others. Many famous "Rumi quotes" on the internet come from those, or are
   not by Rumi at all. If a line is not in this file with a source, the Maker does not say it.
3. Spelling and punctuation follow the 1898 text. Lines may be shortened only at a line break, and a
   shortened quotation must not change the meaning.
4. Page numbers are approximate (from the scan) and should be checked against the printed page before
   any public release. Book and story or ode numbers were read from the scan's headings.
5. Every quotation the Maker speaks is introduced in his own words, so it is clearly a quotation:
   "A poet wrote, a long time ago…" He never claims the words as his own.

## The ten big words

Once per major beat the Maker shows off. These are original lines, written for the game. Each is
followed by somebody asking what that means, and his plain answer. Spoken lines, each ≤ 90 characters.

| # | Where | The big line | Who asks | His plain answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | m44, first sight of Iron Mama | "A thermodynamically incontinent leviathan, hoarding our electrons." | Bature: "English, sir?" | "A greedy generator." |
| B2 | m53, the second plaque | "Her methodology was incontrovertibly, perspicaciously communitarian." | Bature: "Say am again slowly." | "She liked people." |
| B3 | m55, after Iron Mama | "Antidisestablishmentarian it is not. But it was splendiferous." | Firdausi: "Is that a real word?" | "Two of them were. It went well." |
| B4 | m67, after tape 5 | "I have been perambulating the perimeter of my own procrastination." | Nusaybah: "You've been stuck." | "Thirty years. Yes." |
| B5 | m75, the third coil | "Triskaidekaphobia I have none; trinities I adore. Three!" | Bature: "He is fine. He is just happy." | "I am just happy." |
| B6 | m87, the beacon | "Sesquipedalian sky-sorcery, executed with immaculate alacrity." | Abdul: "Check?" | "Check. Well flown." |
| B7 | m96, after the Mirror | "Your counterfeit was hopelessly, magnificently two-dimensional." | Tunde: "It couldn't draw." | "It couldn't be drawn." |
| B8 | m101, the notebook | "Pneumonoultramicroscopic patience is required for page two hundred and six." | Fatima: "Actually, that's a lung disease." | "So is waiting. Read." |
| B9 | m108, the twelve sections | "A magnanimous concatenation of synchronicity! Twelve departments, one denominator!" | Muktar: "Point of order: what?" | "Everybody came." |

Words used: thermodynamically, leviathan, methodology, incontrovertibly, perspicaciously,
antidisestablishmentarian, splendiferous, perambulating, triskaidekaphobia, sesquipedalian,
alacrity, pneumonoultramicroscopic (shortened from the lung-disease word), synchronicity, magnanimous, concatenation, floccinaucinihilipilification. The long nonsense word from a
famous film song is deliberately not used: it belongs to that film.
