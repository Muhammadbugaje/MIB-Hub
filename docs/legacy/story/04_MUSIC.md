# Freshers School Life — Music and Sound Direction

How the school should sound across five years. Read with `02_BIBLE.md` and `03_OUTLINE.md`.

**Where things stand.** The game makes its music in the browser from small generated patterns: twelve
zones by four times of day, eleven radio stations, and ambience (birds, crickets, water). Recorded sound
effects are being added. There are no recorded songs. This document says what the music should *do*;
section 9 is honest about how much of that generated music can deliver and what would need real
recordings.

## 1. Principles

1. **Music is a place.** You should know where you are with your eyes shut. This exists; keep it.
2. **Radio is a voice.** Stations are the school talking to itself. The presenter knows what you did.
3. **One tune belongs to the school.** A short melody that turns up in the fountain theme, the radio
   jingle, the exam bell and the finale. People should hum it without knowing why.
4. **Layers, not switches.** When tension rises, add to what is playing. Do not cut to a new track.
5. **The dark is quiet.** When the light goes, the music does not get scary. It gets less.
6. **Wins are shared, losses are private.** Celebration music brings in voices and drums. Loss music is
   one instrument.
7. **Never the same loop for long.** Vary by time of day, weather, year and standing.
8. **Stay out of the way of speech.** Music ducks under any spoken line and under the owner's recorded voices.

Techniques, in plain terms (sources in `01_RESEARCH.md`, lesson B11):

- *Horizontal*: change to a different section of music when the situation changes.
- *Vertical*: keep the piece going and add or remove instruments.
- *Diegetic*: music that exists in the world, like a radio or a band at a wedding.
- *Leitmotif*: a short tune tied to a person or idea, which returns in different moods.
- *Stinger*: a two-to-five-second musical mark for an event.

## 2. The school's theme and the leitmotifs

Each motif is described so a composer, or the generator, can make it. Notes are given in a major key as
scale degrees (1 = home note).

| Motif | For | Shape | Feel | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **School** | The school itself | 5–6–1'–6–5–3 (six notes, rising then settling) | Open, proud, a little wistful | Fountain, radio jingle, exam bell, convocation |
| **Light** | The coils, the Maker | 1–5–1' held, with a soft shimmer above | Still, clean, electric | Engineering Garden; grows by one voice per coil lit |
| **Dark** | Entropy | The Light motif downward and unfinished: 1'–5–(stops) | Not menacing; missing its last note | Blackouts; each "face" plays it on a different sound |
| **Crew** | Friendship | A bouncing four-note call and answer: 3–5–6–5 / 6–5–3–1 | Playful | Walking with friends; added to place themes when a friend is near |
| **Home** | Your room, family calls | 3–2–1, slow, repeated | Warm, small | Hostel at night; phone calls from home |
| **Road** | Travel, vehicles | A steady two-chord vamp, no melody | Forward motion | Under engine sounds when the radio is off |
| **Heart** | Romance, and the best friend on the single path | The Crew motif at half speed, one voice | Tender, never syrupy | Dates; the dinner; the gown photos |
| **Work** | Classes, projects, industrial training | A ticking pattern with a patient bass | Focused | Lecture halls, labs, workshop |
| **Market** | Traders, the mall | A talking-drum-style rhythm phrase | Busy, bargaining | Market; becomes glossier inside the mall |
| **Elder** | Malam Garba, Baba Dam, Kaka | Three long notes on a single-string-fiddle-like tone: 1–♭7–1 | Old, patient | Their scenes; the forest edge |

Rules:

- The **Dark** motif never resolves until m116, when the Maker completes it by playing the missing last
  note on the **Light** motif. That is the whole score's one trick; do not spend it early.
- Every finale plays **School** and **Crew** together.
- Each face of Entropy takes the Dark motif on its own instrument: Count Entropy, a low organ; Iron
  Mama, a diesel-engine rhythm; the Long Silence, a single held note that slowly detunes; the Mirror,
  the player's own last-heard radio tune played backwards; Chancellor Null, a ceremonial brass phrase
  with notes missing.

**Weather and season.** Harmattan: thin, dry, high-pass everything slightly, add wind. Rains: rounder
bass, rain on zinc as rhythm. Exam weeks: every place theme loses its drums.

## 4. Dynamic layers

One base piece per place, with up to six layers the game can fade in and out. This suits generated
music well, because layers are just patterns switched on and off.

| Layer | On when | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| L0 Bed | Always | Pad or bass and the place's chord |
| L1 Pulse | Moving | Light percussion that matches walking or riding speed |
| L2 Tune | Daylight, not in a talk | The place melody |
| L3 Crew | A friend is walking with you | The Crew motif as an answering voice |
| L4 Tension | Heat rising; shades near; a timer running | A ticking high pattern; bass drops an octave |
| L5 Drive | Chase, race, fight | Full drums; the tune drops out |

State changes:

| State | Layers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Explore | L0–L2 (+L3) | Default |
| Talk | L0 only | Tune and pulse fade in half a second |
| Tension | L0, L1, L4 | Tune gone; no new instruments |
| Chase / race | L0, L1, L4, L5 | Tempo locks to the action |
| Fight (people) | L0, L5, lighter | Comic percussion: wood blocks, a slide whistle once |
| Fight (shades) | L0, L4, Dark motif | Never heavy drums; the dark is quiet |
| Romance | L0, Heart motif | One instrument and the bed |
| Win | Stinger, then L0–L3 with claps for 20 s | Then back to explore |
| Loss | Everything out; one instrument; 6 s; then L0 | No "fail" jingle |
| Blackout | Everything fades to L0 at half volume over 3 s; Dark motif once | Returns layer by layer when light comes back |

Vehicles: if the radio is on, the radio replaces place music and is diegetic (it gets quieter when you
get off and walk away, and you can hear other people's radios pass). If the radio is off, the **Road**
motif plays under the engine.

## 5. Stingers

Short, distinct, and few. Each has a name scripts can use.

| Id | For | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| `sting_accept` | Mission accepted | Two rising notes from the School motif |
| `sting_step` | Step complete | One soft marimba note; pitch rises with each step |
| `sting_pass` | Mission complete | First four notes of School, with claps |
| `sting_reveal` | A clue or secret | Light motif's shimmer and one low note |
| `sting_dark` | Light goes | A descending slide and then nothing |
| `sting_upnepa` | Light returns | Rising slide, a cheer from far off, School motif's last three notes |
| `sting_warn` | Security warning | Whistle (sound effect), then two dry drum hits |
| `sting_friend` | Friendship step up | Crew motif once, bright |
| `sting_heart` | Romance stage | Heart motif, three notes |
| `sting_break` | Break-up; falling out | Heart motif stops one note early |
| `sting_test` | Test starts | Bell and a ticking pattern |
| `sting_result_good` / `_bad` | Results | Bright chord / a single low note and a sigh from the crowd |
| `sting_level` | Level up; promotion | School motif, full, four seconds |
| `sting_coil` | A coil lit | Light motif with one more voice than last time |
| `sting_face` | A face of Entropy appears | Dark motif on that face's instrument |

### Presenter scripts by act (samples; writers supply about 40 lines per station per act)

**Act 1 — Fresh FM, DJ Kabiru**

- "You're locked in to Fresh FM. If you're new, welcome. If you're lost, face the tall orange building."
- "Time check: eight o'clock. Lectures have started. Why are you listening to radio?"
- "They've taken light at Akenzua. Again. Charge your phones when you can, my people."
- "Somebody saw something moving near the Sculpture Garden last night. It was probably a goat. Probably."
- "UP NEPA! You heard it here first. Actually you saw it first. But you heard it here second."

**Act 2 — Fresh FM**

- "The market is now a mall. Fifteen shops, two floors, one escalator that only goes up."
- "Shout-out to the mall for having light all night. How do you people do it? No, seriously. How?"
- "Shuttle drivers are on strike. Phase 2, we are thinking of you. From here. Where it is near."
- "Department week! May the best department win. And may the worst one at least bring food."

**Act 3 — Fresh FM (the strike)**

- "This is Fresh FM, broadcasting to… I can see four people from this window."
- "Day twenty-one. I have played every track twice. Send requests. Send anything."
- "If you are sitting alone in your room right now: stand up. Open the door. That's all. That's the show."
- "They've called it off! Come back! Bring your bags! Bring my listeners!"

**Act 4 — Fresh FM**

- "Trending on campus: a certain somebody at The Floor. You know yourself."
- "A reminder that not everything you watch on a phone happened the way it looks."
- "Election season. Posters are covering posters that are covering posters."

**Act 5 — Fresh FM**

- "Finalists, your clearance forms will not sign themselves. I checked."
- "To whoever is building something big in the Engineering workshop: the whole school can hear it. Carry on."
- "The light is gone and I am on battery. I'll keep talking as long as it lasts. Somebody stay with me."
- "This is DJ Kabiru. The sun is coming up, every lamp is on, and I have nothing clever to say. Well done."

**Bugaje Beats — the Maker's one sentence an hour (all acts)**

- "Energy in. Light out."
- "Six were planned. Count what you can see."
- "A machine that needs one keeper is not finished."
- "If you are waiting for someone else to switch it on: so are they."

### Radio rules

- After any main mission, the next Fresh FM link mentions it in one line.
- Radio is forced off in tail and stealth missions, and by choice in quiet missions (m90, m107).
- During blackouts only Fresh FM (battery) and Bugaje Beats stay on air. The others hiss.
- In m116 every station plays the same thing: the School theme, each in its own style.

Planned quiet, so it reads as a choice and not a fault.

| Where | What is heard | Why |
|---|---|---|

| The library | Two notes and paper | A place with its own rule |
| The forest, first visit (m62) | Birds only for a full minute | You have left the school's music behind |
| Empty campus (m59–m69) | Place themes at half their layers | Absence you can hear |
| m67, the Maker's secret | The coil's hum only | Nothing should tell the player how to feel |

| m107, failure | Rain. No HUD, no music | The low point |

## 8. Year by year

| Year | Palette shift | New sounds | The face's instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Arriving | Bright, simple, a little thin: you do not know the tunes yet | Bicycle bell, bucket taps, generator cough | Low organ |
| 2 Money | Glossier; more bass; the mall's polished version of the Market motif | Tills, escalator, arcade chirps | Diesel rhythm |
| 3 Drift | Half the layers gone for the first semester; then everything back, slightly too fast | Wind, a far bell, forest birds, tape hiss | A held note detuning |
| 4 Image | Phone-speaker versions of themes; ringtones quoting motifs | Notification pings (sparingly), camera shutter, crowd squeal | Your own tune reversed |
| 5 Leaving | The Year 1 arrangements return, fuller and slower; every motif gets a second voice | Workshop tools, gown rustle, a brass band at convocation | Brass with notes missing |

The emotional idea: by Year 5 the player hears the same melodies they met as a fresher, and they sound
different because the player has changed.

## 9. How the music could be produced

Be clear about three levels.

**Level A: generated in the browser (what exists).** Good at: grooves by place and time, layers, tempo
following action, stingers, radio beds, endless variation, tiny download. Weak at: real instruments,
voices, anything meant to move people on its own. Everything in sections 2–5 can be done at this level,
because motifs are note patterns and layers are switches. This is the recommended first step and
costs no licences.

**Level B: recorded instrumental loops and stems.** Short recorded parts (guitar, talking drum, flute,
piano, kalimba, shekere) arranged by the same layer system. Options:

- Commission a local producer for a pack of loops and one recorded School theme. The owner would own
  the recordings outright under a written agreement.
- Record students: a guitarist, a drummer, a choir humming the School motif. Written permission from
  each performer.
- Use public-domain (CC0) loops. These exist but are rarely in the right styles; check each licence and
  keep a credits file, as is being done for sound effects.

**Level C: songs.** A recorded theme song with words, sung by a real person, for the trailer and for
m116 and the credits. This must be commissioned or performed by people who agree in writing. No
existing commercial song may be used, in the game or the trailer, without a licence.

What must not be done: copying a known song's melody or lyrics, "sound-alike" tracks of a named artist,
or using songs from the internet without a licence that clearly allows it.

Recommended order: build Level A motifs and layers now; record one School theme and five loops (Level B)
when the story is being built; consider one song (Level C) for launch.
