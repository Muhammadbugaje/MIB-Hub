## 1. What changed from the first version

1. **120 missions, not 200.** Missions 1–36 are Year 1 (rewritten fresh in revision 4). Years 2 to 5 have 21 each. Thin missions
   were merged into their neighbours; nothing in the mystery was lost.
2. **The strike year stays**, as you asked.
3. **The Maker leaves his post** at the end. Afterwards he walks the campus like anyone else, content,
   and says something wise each time you talk to him. There are 44 sayings in the bible, section 18.
4. **Romance** is opposite-sex pairings only, five partners each for male and female players.
5. **Being broke** alone never ends a relationship; neglect and cheating do. A partner can still leave
   if you stay broke *and* neglectful.
6. **Garri.** The broke-student humour is now a running thread: garri and groundnut as the dinner of
   week four, friends in your doorway with an empty cup, twelve garri lines anyone can say, and the
   best date in the game happening on garri week (bible, section 17).
7. **The patrol brings you back.** No shooting.
8. **Names.** Your friends' first names are used for characters and cameos, as names only, with
   invented personalities (bible, section 21). Bola is now Bolaji.
9. **The MIB Store** sells exclusives (custom shirts, special outfits, a phone, rides, passes, tickets,
   rare parts) and appears in eleven missions.
10. **What the game has gained since** is respected in every script: night rules, job vehicles, the
    focus system, crowds by day and night, the zoomable map, five arcade games, the dance floor,
    wardrobe, the wilderness and patrol, and walk-in rooms.
11. **Friends missions.** 25 co-op designs for 2–6 players: racing, flying, hunts, sparring and sport,
    holding back the dark together, with options that make each session different.

## 2. The story on one page

You arrive as a fresher who knows nobody. Five years later you leave in a gown, and half the school
knows your name.

In between, the light keeps going off, and something moves in the dark. Students call it NEPA. The
Maker, the quiet man who keeps the Tesla coil in the Engineering Garden, calls it **Entropy**: the rule
that everything runs down unless somebody puts energy in.

Long ago a professor and her students planned a ring of **six coils** round the school to keep the
light steady. They quarrelled, the ring was never finished, and one student stayed behind to keep the
single working coil alive. He never graduated. He is the Maker.

| Year | Missions | The trouble | What is new to play |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (100 level) | 1–36 | Registration day and a room choice; blackouts, shades and Count Entropy | Campus, vehicles, classes, first fights, first flight |
| 2 (200 level) | 37–57 | The mall never loses power; somebody is selling the school's light | Mall, clothes, arcade, dancing, department week |
| 3 (300 level) | 58–78 | A strike empties the campus; grey shapes settle in the halls | The forest, patrol, old campus, hustles, building lamps |
| 4 (400 level) | 79–99 | You go viral; then something walks around wearing your face | Industrial training, night flying, fishing, cooking |
| 5 (500 level) | 100–120 | The sixth coil is your final-year project; Entropy offers you forever | The longest night, convocation, sign-out |

You refuse the offer, the ring closes, the Maker graduates beside you, and the last mission is a new
fresher called Muhammad asking you the way to the Senate.

The message, never preached: **light is something people do together.**

## 3. How it plays

- **One clear main mission at a time**, rotating between the mystery, your department, your friends,
  romance and the wider world. No two neighbours are the same kind of mission.
- **Every tenth mission is a day off**: the arcade, a skate contest, fishing, a dinner.
- **50 side missions**, each belonging to one person and revealing who they are.
- **30 "someone needs your help" templates**, each with three written variations, so small requests
  never run out: buy me something, drop me at Phase 2, walk me to class, take him to the Sick Bay,
  teach me, help me ask her out, settle our argument, you get garri?, sit with me a while.
- **Friends** have five scenes each; in the last they tell you a secret. You can name a best friend.
- **Five standings** record what the school thinks of you: Books, Street, Vibes, Community, Department.
  They decide your class of degree, your award at the final-year dinner, and what your friends write
  on your shirt on sign-out day.
- **Fights have an audience**: watchers, filmers, laughers, people who separate you, people who call
  security.

## 4. Music

One tune belongs to the school. The dark's tune is the light's tune with the last note missing; that
note is played once in the whole game, when the Maker walks out of the garden. Music changes by adding
and removing layers, gets quieter (not scarier) when the light goes, and the radio presenter follows
the story. The game's generated music can do all of this. Real instruments or a real song would have to
be recorded by people who agree in writing; no existing commercial song can be used.

The scripts are written so every spoken line is short and fixed, with an empty voice id beside it for
when you record.

## 6. Risks and advice

1. **Recording.** There are several thousand spoken lines. Record the main characters' key lines first
   and leave the rest as text with the talk sound.
2. **Build one year, then play it.** Year 2 leans on things that already exist. Build it, play it, and
   only then commit to Years 3 to 5.
3. **The strike, fame and loneliness** are written gently, with no real people or bodies named.
4. **Faith** appears only as community: people feeding, visiting and playing football together.
5. **Left out on purpose:** cults, drugs, alcohol, gambling for money, exam malpractice as a clever
   route, bribing guards, real brands, real songs, real people, and anything sexual.
6. **Checked by reading, not by play.** These are scripts. Nobody has played them. Timings, rewards
   and level gates are first guesses that need tuning once they are in the game.
