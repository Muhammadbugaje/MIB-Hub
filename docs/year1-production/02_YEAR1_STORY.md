## Year 1 at a glance

**Question of the year:** *Who wrote the note — and what moves when the light goes?*

**The promise.** Year 1 is about arrival: the smell of a new room, the queue that teaches you the campus, the friend who eats your biscuits, the first night the power fails and nobody panics because everybody is used to it. Under the comedy, four things are planted and never explained: a folded note with seven dots, a clock stopped at 6:06, a hum under the Engineering Garden, and a quiet young man in a white shirt whom nobody else quite remembers.

**The shape.** Two semesters, twenty-four missions, one academic year. The first act is Freshers' Week. The second is the ordinary machinery of student life, ending in the first whole-campus blackout. The third turns outward: work, sport, radio, the dam, the six sites, a week of asking people for help, exams, and the convocation night when the campus lights the coil together.

**The cast.** The fresher (player), the roommate (Bature or Firdausi), the five leads (Jega, Shamsu, Mubarak, Nusaiba, Dr. Zainab), the friend group (Aleeya, Maryam, Lyzzy, Lily, Abbas, Helen, Bolaji, Muhammad), the staff and traders who make the campus run, the Maker, and the mystery that wears a white shirt.

**The tone.** Warm, fast and very funny. The joke is always the situation — sapa, garri, NEPA, queues, "network" — never a person's tribe, faith, body or bank balance. The dark is quiet, not horror. Nobody dies, nobody is expelled, and a knock-down means sitting down for ten seconds with stars round your head.

**The rules the season obeys.**
- Every mission has a "Connects to" line: an earlier object, consequence or person returning.
- Every mission plants at least one clue and pays at least one small thing.
- The player speaks in short lines. Friends carry the long talk.
- The season answers **no** major mystery. It shows behaviour, not truth.

## ACT I · ARRIVAL (M001–M004)

**Freshers' Week.** The player arrives, registers, eats, chases a goat, is photographed, and receives a note. The act establishes the campus as a comic, crowded, livable place and plants the season's four physical clues: the note, the seven dots, the clock at 6:06, and the hum in the garden. Nothing is explained.

## M001 · Welcome, Fresher

**When:** Y1 · Freshers' Week · Mon (resumption day) · 07:00 to sunset.  
**Where:** gate, hostel, student (the Spot), ring road, agric.  
**Who:** Mum, Dad, Baba Porter, {{ROOMIE}}, Mama Nkechi, Malam Sule, Sarki the goat.  
**Special helpers (conditional):** Taty (a passing bark only).  

**Mystery planted.** Plants C-NOTE (the note), C-SYMBOL (inside the tin, on the note), C-CLOCK (6:06, and one strike), C-HUM (the garden), C-GOAT ("one engineering boy"), MS-01 (a still figure in the film).

**Connects to.** Nothing earlier. This is the first ten minutes, exactly as bible §3.

### Story

Dawn over {{UNI_NAME}}: laterite roads, the ring road, the dam like a dropped coin, a clock tower whose hands say 6:06 and mean nothing by it. A family car reaches the Main Gate. Dad is certain this is not the main gate. The guard is certain it is. Mum, in the back, reads from a list that has been growing since the admission letter came: don't follow bad friends, don't follow good friends too closely either, eat, pray, call me.

Baba Porter takes the Ghana-must-go bag on his head with one hand and climbs four flights without changing the rhythm of his complaints. There is no lift. There has never been a lift. Mum makes a bed that is already made. Dad presses folded notes into your palm as if the room were bugged. "Manage it." Then the hug, the door, the car getting small, and Mum's voice arriving after the car has gone: "CALL ME!"

You wake to the sound of your own biscuits. {{ROOMIE}} is on the other bed, guarding them from the inside. A riddle is offered before a name. You are led to the window, which is how you learn to walk and look, and given your kingdom: twin theatres, a clock that is right twice a day ("more than our lecturers"), a line of water beyond.

Downstairs the campus is doing what it does at eight on a Monday: rivers of students toward the faculties, nobody in the road, a queue at the one good tap. At the Spot, Mama Nkechi looks at you once and knows you have not eaten. You pay for two plates because {{ROOMIE}} agreed that you would, in {{ROOMIE}}'s head.

Then a white-faced goat with a brass bell comes through the food court like a rumour, lands on the suya table, and looks you in the eye. It is not a chase so much as a guided insult: it waits at every corner, eats a matriculation poster while you vault a bench, and lets itself be caught only when it is bored. A bicycle leans on a fence ("Borrow it. Everybody does."). Goat in the basket, you ride the ring road with {{ROOMIE}} jogging beside you, narrating badly: Engineering, who don't sleep; the clock, which doesn't work; a fenced garden where something hums. "Don't ask." You ask. "I said don't ask."

At the farm, Malam Sule takes the goat, whose name is Sarki, with the weariness of a man who has done it before. One engineering boy was here, he says, giving it a lecture about littering. Ten minutes. Left the gate open. "You, you brought him back. People don't, you know." He says that last part oddly, as if repeating it for someone. Behind the barn, where the old kola tree was, a loose stone, a tin, a Kola of Vigour. Scratched inside the lid: six dots in a ring and a seventh in the middle.

Sunset. The hostel corridor flickers once. There is a folded note under your door, in careful handwriting, with the same seven dots. "The Maker is watching. Welcome." The corridor is empty. Far off, a clock that does not work strikes once. {{ROOMIE}} reads over your shoulder and, for the only time today, does not make a joke. "…Don't lose that."

## M002 · Queue Theory

**When:** Y1 · Freshers' Week · Tue · 08:00–16:00.  
**Where:** admin: Senate, Registry, Bursary, bank.  
**Who:** Malam Garba, Mrs. Adebayo, Mr. Clement, Aleeya, Muhammad, Mum (phone).  
**Special helpers (conditional):** Jiya.  

**Mystery planted.** Plants C-DOOR7 (a door with no handle), MS-02 (he knows your name; the clerk talks past him), C-CLOCK (the bank's clock also says 6:06: "We set it by the tower").

**Connects to.** M1 (the note is in your Pockets; Malam Garba sees it).

### Story

Registration is four offices, four queues and one file, and the file must be stamped in an order that nobody has written down. {{ROOMIE}} sends you off with the only advice anyone gives: "Carry water. Carry a pen. Carry yourself."

The Senate queue curls round the building like a question mark. A tall girl with a kind face holds your bag and your place while you fetch a form, and introduces herself as Aleeya. She compliments your patience and then observes that it is the patience of a goat tied to a post; you like her at once.

At the Registry, Mrs. Adebayo requires four passport photographs. She has required four passport photographs of everyone since 1994. Your phone rings in the silence of the queue: Mum, on loudspeaker by mistake, asking forty strangers whether you have eaten. You learn the phone this way: the call, the first text ("Daddy sent something small. Don't finish it."), the bank alert that follows.

At the Bursary, Mr. Clement says the word. "Network." Forty people sit down on the floor as one, like a prayer. Someone produces a mat. Someone produces ludo.

It is here that a soft-spoken young man in a plain white shirt, whom you had not noticed standing beside you, says: "Excuse me. Sorry. Don't take the shortcut behind the Bursary, {{PLAYER_NAME}}. The gate is locked on Tuesdays." You did not tell him your name. You turn to ask, and Mrs. Adebayo, calling the next person, looks straight through the space where he is standing and says "Next" to the person behind him. He steps aside, apologises to nobody in particular, and by the time the queue has shuffled he is not there.

You can take the shortcut anyway. The game lets you: a narrow lane, a locked gate, a goat, an hour gone, and a security man who wants ₦500 for "trespass on a path". Or you can go round.

The last stamp is in the Senate basement. On the way to Malam Garba's desk you pass a steel door painted with a 7. It has no handle and no keyhole on this side. Malam Garba is a small man with a large ledger. He stamps your file. Then he sees the folded note sticking out of your pocket, and his stamp stops in the air for the length of a breath. He looks at you properly, for the first time. "Hm," he says, and it is a whole paragraph. He writes something in the ledger that is not your name.

Outside, the clock says 6:06. It is four in the afternoon. You are, officially, a student.

## M003 · Belle First

**When:** Y1 · Freshers' Week · Wed · 18:00–23:00.  
**Where:** hostel: kitchen, corridor.  
**Who:** {{ROOMIE}}, Mama Nkechi (phone), Baba Porter, a quiet fresher (Idris), corridor freshers.  

**Mystery planted.** Plants C-BROADCAST (three notes; a woman counts down from seven); first shade (what moves in the dark); C-KB (he sold out of candles at noon, at double price).

**Connects to.** M1 (the corridor that flickered); M2 (the money from home buys the ingredients).

### Story

You have ₦4,200, a borrowed hotplate with one working ring, and a roommate who describes themself as "more of a consultant". The plan is jollof. Mama Nkechi, phoned for advice, gives a recipe in the form of threats: fry the tomato until it stops shouting; if you add water early she will know.

The hostel kitchen at six is a parliament. Every ring is contested, every pot has a guard. You learn to cook the way the game means it: chop, fry, time the stir, and do not leave your pot, because an unattended pot in a hostel is public property. {{ROOMIE}} supervises from a stool, tasting "for safety", and tells you the corridor's first piece of lore: KB at the corner shop sold out of candles at noon, and charged double. "Who buys candles at noon? Somebody who knows something."

At 19:40 the rice is half-done and NEPA takes the light. The groan goes down four floors like a wave. The hotplate dies. In the dark, twelve freshers discover that they own, between them, one torch, one phone at nine per cent, and a great deal of garri.

So you learn the other recipe. Cold water. Sugar. Groundnut if you are rich. {{ROOMIE}} is very clear about the rules: "Hot water on garri? You want to become eba by accident?" Somebody finds a candle. A cup goes round.

It is when you go back down the corridor for the sugar that you see it. At the far end, where the last room is, the dark is thicker than dark should be: a smudge, low, the size of a child, with two dim places where eyes might go. It is not doing anything. It is drifting, slowly, toward the one door on the corridor with a line of torchlight under it, where a boy called Idris, who has not spoken to anyone since Monday, is sitting alone with a book.

You do not fight it. There is nothing to fight. You knock. You say, "We have garri." He looks at you as if you have offered him a kingdom. You walk him back to the kitchen, and when you look over your shoulder the smudge has thinned to nothing, like breath on glass.

In the kitchen {{ROOMIE}}'s pocket radio, left on, has lost its station. From the dead end of the dial come three slow notes, then a woman's voice, calm and amused, counting: "Seven. Six. Five. Four. Three. Two. One." Then static. {{ROOMIE}} smacks it. "Old thing. It does that."

Twelve freshers round one candle, one cup, and behind them a corridor that is only a corridor again. Baba Porter climbs up with a lamp, counts heads, and seems satisfied by the number. The light comes back at 22:50. The half-cooked rice is finished off at eleven and is the best thing you have ever eaten.

## M004 · Matric Fit

**When:** Y1 · S1 Wk 2 · Fri (matriculation, event day) · 08:00–14:00.  
**Where:** market (tailor), student (SUG), eng (theatre lawn).  
**Who:** Mubarak, Alhaji Needle, Lyzzy, Lily, Maryam, Aleeya.  
**Special helpers (conditional):** Madison.  

**Mystery planted.** Plants MS-03 (a white shirt in the back row; in the print, an empty space), C-PHOTO.

**Connects to.** M2 (Aleeya waves from the next row; your student ID is checked at the lawn).

### Story

Matriculation is at ten. It is eight. Your gown, ordered from Alhaji Needle on Monday, will be ready "Thursday". You point out that today is Friday. He agrees, warmly, that it is, and returns to a wedding outfit he has been making since before you were admitted.

In the Student Union a young man in sunglasses and a belt that says GUCCY has laid out gowns on a table like a carpet seller. This is Mubarak. He is a first-year like you; he has been on campus eleven days and already has a business card. The gown he rents you is "premium, for you, specially". It would fit two of you and a small child. He holds up a mirror the size of a biscuit so that you can only see your face. "See? Fine."

The game teaches outfits here, and Charm: what you wear changes how people answer you. In a gown like a collapsed tent, a security man laughs before he can stop himself. Fix it. By the gate flowers a very quiet girl is watering plants that are not hers; she has safety pins, of course she has, and pins the hem without a word and then says, softly, "It will be windy at ten." Her twin, who has a camera, a ring light and no volume control, tells you not to move because your face is a moment. That is Lily and Lyzzy. At the bookshop door a tall girl called Maryam lends you a belt off her own tote bag and asks whether you have read anything good; you say the fee notice; she laughs properly.

At ten it is windy. Eight hundred freshers stand on the lawn between the Twin Theatres with their right hands up. On "I solemnly promise" the wind gets under your gown and for one long second you matriculate as a kite, pinned to the earth by Lily's work and Maryam's belt. Lyzzy gets the photograph. It is on the noticeboard by noon with a caption.

Afterwards she shows you the class picture on her camera, zooming along the rows to find you. Back row, far left, between a boy with a flag and the end of the bench: a young man in a plain white shirt, no gown, hands folded, looking not at the camera but slightly to one side, at you. "Who's that?" you ask. "Who?" She zooms. "That's nobody, that's a gap." You look again at the little screen. Between the boy with the flag and the end of the bench there is a gap, exactly one person wide. She has already moved on to her own best angle.

Mubarak finds you to collect the gown and discovers he owes you ₦200 change, which he does not have. He gives you his number instead, as if it were worth more. It will be.

## M005 · First Lecture, Wrong Theatre

**When:** Y1 · S1 Wk 3 · Mon (lecture day) · 07:40–10:00.  
**Where:** eng: Theatre Yamma, Theatre Gabas, the electrical lab.  
**Who:** Dr. Okonkwo, Shamsu, Nusaiba, Abbas, Wednesday, a Law lecturer.  

**Mystery planted.** Plants C-PAGE41 (cut out with a blade), C-SEVENTH (six circles on page forty, and a seventh pencilled then rubbed out), C-SNITCH groundwork (Shamsu "cannot" lie about the time he arrived).

**Connects to.** M1 (the symbol in the tin is on page forty); M4 (you sit near people you matriculated beside).

### Story

The Twin Theatres are identical, face each other across a lawn, and have had their signs swapped by tradition since before your parents met. You choose with confidence. Ten minutes into a lecture on the law of tort you begin to suspect. The lecturer, who has decided your name is "the gentleman in the unfortunate shirt", asks you to define a tort. You say it is a kind of cake. The room is delighted. You leave at a dignified sprint.

Theatre Gabas, eight minutes late. Dr. Okonkwo does not stop talking; she simply looks at the clock, then at you, and the look goes on your file. The only seat is beside a neat boy with a pocket full of pens who has drawn the entire circuit already. "Simple," he whispers, and explains it for four minutes while she explains it for two. This is Shamsu. The lecture game begins: listen, catch the key line, note it, answer when the question comes to your row. He whispers the answer, then the history of the answer, then an analogy involving a bucket and your uncle.

In the third row a girl with a folder raises her hand and says, "With respect, ma, the sign on the second term should be negative. I have three reasons." Dr. Okonkwo checks. It should. "Name?" "Nusaiba." "Sit in front from now on, Nusaiba. I need the supervision."

At the end, a practical: the lab's demonstration board is dead for want of a fuse, and the fresher who came late may fetch one. In the electrical lab a patient boy called Abbas is being addressed as "John Carter" by everyone who passes and answering "My name is Abbas" each time, without heat, like a clock. He finds you the fuse. A girl at the next bench says, "See you Wednesday," although it is Monday, and does not look up.

The fuse drawer is under the lab logbook, a ledger older than the building. It is open at page forty. Someone has drawn six circles in a ring, joined by a single line, each labelled with a place name in small capitals: the same six dots as the tin. In the centre of the ring the paper is rough where a seventh circle was drawn in pencil and rubbed out so hard it has gone shiny. You turn the page to see more. There is no page forty-one. It has been cut out along the gutter with a blade, neatly, by a steady hand, a long time ago; the stub has yellowed with the rest.

Dr. Okonkwo's hand closes the book on your fingers, not unkindly. "Page forty," she says, "is not on the syllabus." She takes the fuse, looks at you for a moment longer than the lateness deserves, and adds, "Neither is asking."

### Story

The bravest header of a ball on campus is hiding behind a neem tree outside the women's hall. He has been there forty minutes. He beckons you over with his whole arm. "{{PLAYER_NAME}}. Kai. Come. I have a tactical problem."

The problem is the Cinema Hall, which shows one film every Saturday at six, and a girl he would like to take to it. The girl who keeps the score. The girl whose name he does not know. You point out that everyone on campus knows her name. "Ban sani ba," he says, with dignity. It has gone on too long now; he said it once as a joke, to you, and then to his team, and now if he says her name he has to admit he always knew it. "It is like a penalty, wallahi. The longer you wait, the bigger the goal becomes. No. The smaller. You understand."

He rehearses on you. "Sannu… no. Hello… no, too English. Kai! …No, she is not a goalkeeper." So you are sent, and the mission teaches you the relationship system by making you do it for somebody else. Find out what she likes. The game's rule is plain and it says it out loud: gifts only work if you listened. Ask her friend Firdausi and you learn it is roasted corn, not flowers. Helen is on the volleyball court. She hears you out with her head on one side. She answers questions with questions. "Who's asking?" "Why is he behind a tree?" "Does he think I can't see a man that tall behind a tree that thin?" If you brought corn, she takes it and almost smiles. If you brought flowers, she takes those too, and says, "Tell him these are for a different girl. Find out which, and bring me her corn."

She says yes. Of course she does. She tells you something on the way, to keep: she started "IDK" herself. She heard Jega say "Ban sani ba" at the trials, thought it was the funniest thing she had ever seen, and told her whole team to call her IDK. "I'm waiting to see how long he'll carry it. I think years." She seems to look forward to it.

Then it is your turn: the same cinema, the same evening. If there is someone you have been talking to, you ask them yourself, with what you have just learned. If not, {{ROOMIE}} comes, as a friend, and talks through the whole film, and it is honestly the better evening. Four rows ahead, two silhouettes: a very tall one sitting bolt upright, and one beside him, not upright at all, entirely at ease.

## M022 · Everybody

**When:** Y1 · S2 Wk 12 · Mon–Fri · any hour; each person is where the timetable puts them.  
**Where:** the whole campus.  
**Who:** everyone you have met; the Maker; Baba Porter.  

**Mystery planted.** Plants C-NOTE (Baba Porter: "thirty years"), C-AWAY (the Maker is counting days); Malam Garba's ledger (your name is already in a column you cannot read the heading of).

**Connects to.** Every mission from M1 to M21: each "yes" is a callback to what you did for that person.

### Story

The Maker sends for you, which he has never done. He does it by leaving the garden gate ajar.

He has been thinking about December, about a cape on some steps and eighty torches. "I have kept one coil lit alone for a long time. I am beginning to suspect I was doing it wrong." The seniors' convocation is the Saturday after exams: every lamp on campus switched on for one night, the heaviest load of the year. The dark will come for it. But on that night the coil could be lit properly, fully, for the first time since the ring failed; and it will only hold if there are people standing round it. "Not soldiers. Witnesses. It was designed for a crowd. I checked the drawings. I had forgotten." He looks at you. "I do not know many people. You seem to."

{{ROOMIE}} makes the list on the back of a lecture handout and writes EVERYBODY at the top, underlined twice.

The mission is a week, and it is the calendar that is the puzzle. Each person is where their life puts them. Mai Shayi only at dusk. Jega at five. Dr. Zainab only in the eleven minutes of shift change, in a corridor, eating. Hajiya Ladi on Thursday, market day, between customers. Mrs. Adebayo in office hours and not one minute after. You plan a week of your own and theirs.

And each yes is the year coming back to you. Jega: "You held the torch for my face. Kai, I'm there, with eleven men." Mubarak: "Free? I am doing a free thing? …Yes. Don't publish it." Shamsu has calculated the optimal spacing of persons round a coil. Nusaiba has already drawn up a roster and resents being asked second. Bolaji will come "as security", and asks whether Big Baby may come, as his. Mama Nkechi: only if she can bring a pot. Mrs. Adebayo agrees, subject to four passport photographs. Lula and Faty will come if they may heckle. Zainab, mouth full: "I'll be on call. So if I'm there, something has gone wrong, and if I'm not, I'm working. Yes."

Last, because he has been watching you go up and down the stairs all week with a list: Baba Porter. He reads the handout top to bottom. He takes out his own pen. He signs slowly, like a man signing for a parcel he ordered long ago. "I've been waiting to be asked," he says. "Thirty years."

You ask what he means. He is already shouting at a fresher about a mattress.

## M023 · Exam Fever

**When:** Y1 · S2 Wk 14–15 · exam weeks · days and nights.  
**Where:** lib; exam halls; eng: the campus substation; med.  
**Who:** Shamsu, Nusaiba, Engr. Aliyu, Dr. Zainab, Nurse Hauwa.  

**Mystery planted.** Plants Year 2 (a mall sticker on a Works van; a feeder labelled "2" that is not on Shamsu's diagram); C-KB (batteries dear again this week).

**Connects to.** M11 (tests; this is the real thing); M9 (the hospital blackout); M12 (exam-week nights); M13 (a resit paper is added if you carried one over).

### Story

Four papers in nine days. The library has reached the stage where somebody has brought a mattress and nobody has objected. Your exam results are real: they are your lecture attendance, your tests, the papers you found in the stacks, and how well you play each exam's short question game. The mission lets you sit them properly, one a day, with nights between.

On the second night, at 01:40, your phone lights up with a text in capitals. It is from "Dr. Z, do NOT call for nonsense". "IS YOUR SCHOOL DOING SOMETHING TO THE LIGHT. THE HOSPITAL LINE DROPS EVERY NIGHT AT THE SAME MINUTE. 01:17. I HAVE BABIES ON MACHINES. THIS IS NOT NONSENSE." Then, a minute later, in lower case: "sorry. i know you have exams. but who else do i ask."

You wake Shamsu, who was not asleep. He has, of course, a hand-drawn diagram of the campus feeders. The hospital shares a line with the reading rooms; both are fed through the campus substation behind Engineering, a brick box with a danger sign and a padlock that Shamsu opens with a number he "noticed once". Inside is a wall of old switchgear with handwritten labels, and at 01:17 exactly, as you watch, one breaker walks itself open with a clack. There is nobody there. Something upstream is pulling the load away.

So for one bad night you hold it. A switchboard game: three feeders, a needle each, and you shed and restore load by hand (the staff club's lights, the Senate's, the car park's) to keep two lines in the green: the wards and the reading rooms, where four hundred people are revising by the light you are keeping on. Shamsu reads the needles aloud and apologises to each circuit he sacrifices. On his diagram there are six feeders. On the wall there are seven. The seventh is labelled, in fresher paint than the rest, with a 2.

Engr. Aliyu of Works arrives at four, in a van, sweating, with the wrong keys and then the right ones. He is a pleasant, tired man and he is frightened of something that is not you. He takes over the board with real skill. "You should not be in here. …Thank you for being in here." He locks up. "This never happened. I owe you. I pay what I owe." On the van's back window is a sticker you do not recognise: a smiling sun over the words KWARI MALL, OPENING SOON.

At eight you sit Circuit Theory on two hours' sleep. On your desk, when you turn over the paper, someone has left a sachet of glucose, a sachet of water, and a note in a doctor's handwriting, which is to say barely a note at all: "SLEEP. This is not advice."

## M024 · Convocation Night

**When:** Y1 · after exams · Sat (event: the seniors' convocation) · 18:00–midnight, then dawn.  
**Where:** admin: Senate steps, the boulevard; eng: garden.  
**Who:** everybody who said yes; Malam Garba; Mai Shayi; the Maker; the Dean of Engineering; Muhammad.  
**Special helpers (conditional):** Jiya, Taty, Madison.  

**Mystery planted.** Pays "what is the Count" (the freshers drew it a face); plants C-NOTE ("Five more."), MS-07 (the Maker sees him and looks away), C-SEALS (the first seal), C-CAPE (still on the vane).

**Connects to.** M22 (every yes stands in its place, by name); M12 (the Count, taller); M20 (the plaques light; one socket answers); M1 (the note); M23 (the load that the feeders cannot carry).

### Story

The seniors graduate in the afternoon: gowns, mothers in gele the width of doorways, a brass band, speeches. You are an usher, which is how first-years attend. At dusk every light on campus is switched on at once for the convocation dinner: the boulevard strung with bulbs, the Senate floodlit, the fountain running for the only time this year. It is the most beautiful the place has ever looked, and Shamsu, beside you, is doing arithmetic with his lips. "That is too much load. That is all seven feeders. That is—"

The dark comes down like a lid.

Four thousand people in their best clothes. One long indrawn breath. And on the Senate steps, where the Vice-Chancellor was standing a moment ago, something rises that is taller than in December, broad as the doorway, the cape spreading. It has had a whole year of blackouts to eat.

What happens next is the year. Down the boulevard, behind you, they come: not eighty torches, hundreds. Phones held up. Mama Nkechi with a pot and a ladle, banging. Jega and eleven men singing. Bolaji with the generator on a wheelbarrow, Big Baby walking in front to clear the way. Mubarak, selling "I SURVIVED YEAR 1" shirts as he walks, because he is who he is, and giving the torches away, because he is also who he is. Lula and Faty, heckling the dark. Nusaiba with a roster. Mrs. Adebayo, with four photographs of herself, in case.

The fight is at the garden, not the steps. Hold the fence while the coil is brought up to full: waves of smudges from each side, and your friends covering the gaps *by name*, each with the line you earned from them. If someone said no, their gap is yours to hold. The Maker works at the plinth with his sleeves rolled, faster than you have seen him move; and you catch him, once, in the middle of it, looking past you at the crowd. A young man in a white shirt is standing among the torches, holding a lantern up, steady. The Maker's hands stop. Then he looks away, like a man refusing to look at the sun, and bends back to the work.

The coil lights. It is not a flash. It is a note you have heard all year, finally in tune, and a colour that goes out across the campus like water finding its level. Paint looks newer. Lamps you did not know existed come on along the ring road. The thing on the steps is not there; nobody saw it go.

Then music, and four thousand people remembering they are at a party.

Mai Shayi finds you at the edge of it with two cups. "You know who gave that thing a cape?" He nods at the freshers dancing. "Us. First-years. Every year. Forty years ago it was only dark. But we were scared, so we drew it a face. A thing with a face you can stand against." He drinks. "It's still up there on the weathervane, the cape. Somebody keeps it tied on. I wonder who."

The Dean of Engineering presses a brass disc into your palm: coin-sized, six dots and a seventh. "It has been my paperweight. And the last Dean's. Somebody once told us to hand it over 'when your people are ready'. I think that was tonight." You try it in the first of the ten sockets round the coil's foot. It clicks home, the socket glows, and then it lets go again, warm, as if it has only been measured. The Maker does not look up. "Keep it."

Malam Garba, at the Senate door, in his one good kaftan: "Go and dance. Fresher no more."

You get back to your room at dawn. The note is in the drawer where you have kept it since September. You unfold it, for no reason. Under "The Maker is watching. Welcome." there is a second line, in the same careful hand, that was not there yesterday.

**Five more.**

## Year-end state

### What the player knows

- The note exists, it has seven dots, and it was under the door on the first night.
- The clock has said 6:06 for as long as anyone remembers; the bank sets its clock by it; it strikes once when the corridor is empty.
- There is a hum in the Engineering Garden, and the same note comes up through the pier at the dam.
- The Maker keeps one coil lit and does not write notes. He leaves for days at a time; the blackouts get worse when he is away.
- There are six plaques round the coil and a seventh space with no plaque.
- Something moves in the dark. It has a shape people gave it: a cape, a face, a Count.
- Muhammad knows the player's name, is seen by children and cats, and is not remembered by most people.
- The first seal is real, fits a socket, glows, and returns to the player's pocket.

### What the player does **not** know

- Who wrote the note. (The answer lands much later; Year 1 only teaches the handwriting and the habit.)
- What the Count is. (Year 1 shows what it does, never what it is.)
- Why the clock is stopped at 6:06. (Shown, never explained.)
- Who the Maker is, where he goes, or who his sister was.
- What the seventh space is for.
- What is behind Door 7.
- Who Muhammad is. The evidence supports several answers; the season confirms none.

### What Year 2 inherits

- A lit Engineering coil (seal 1 in socket 1) and a campus that looks slightly newer.
- A player with a reputation in every district, a circle of named allies, a roomie, possibly a romance, a licence, a job, and a gym membership.
- Six sites on the map, five still question marks.
- A note that says **"Five more."**
- A Maker who has seen Muhammad and looked away.
- A seventh feeder on Shamsu's diagram, labelled **2**.
