# Meet The Hospital

> Calm nurses, patient patients, visitors with flasks.

The nurses, patients and visitors of the teaching hospital. Always kind. Illness is never the joke; the waiting is.

### The voice

Nurses, patients and visitors at the Teaching Hospital ("the TH"): calm nurses, patient patients, visitors with flasks of pap. Kind always; never mocks illness.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **45 lines**, about **431 words**. Read straight through, it's **about 5 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. **More voices are welcome:** it's a crowd, so share the lines out among friends and family.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *The Hospital - Common 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Common lines

These are the short lines The Hospital says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | your normal voice | Sannu. [Hello.] Card number? Sit, they'll call you.
:: 2 | firm | Visiting? Two at a time, please. Two.
:: 3 | warm | Good morning. You're here for a visit or a check? Either way, sit.

::: Common 2 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you, warm | You're welcome. Rest. That's your only assignment today.
:: 2 | sorry, warm | Sannu fa. [Take heart.] You'll be better soon.

::: Common 3 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out, firm | Light gone! Generator, please, now is your moment!
:: 2 | the lights go out | Torches on. Nobody touch the drips. Calm, calm.
:: 3 | the lights come back, warm | Light's back. Everybody breathe.

::: Common 4 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | bored, flat | Number forty-two! Forty-two! …Has anybody seen forty-two?
:: 2 | firm | The queue is for everybody. Even the impatient.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | Bring your card, your folder and your patience. Mostly patience.
:: 4 | warm | His mother brought food for the whole ward. Nobody is complaining.
:: 5 | a whisper | Visitors, lower your voices, please. People are healing.
:: 6 | firm | Sleep under your net, eh? It's not decoration.
:: 7 | firm | Drink water. Eat proper food. Garri alone is not a diet.
:: 8 | warm | He says he's fine. He's here, so sit down, Mr. Fine.
:: 9 | dry, deadpan | The lab results will be ready by four. Hospital four.
:: 10 | your normal voice | Wash your hands. Yes, again. Hands forget.
:: 11 | warm | Who brought the flask of pap? The whole bench is smelling it. Bless you.
:: 12 | warm | She's asleep now. Go and eat something. Then come back.

::: Common 5 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | a whisper, only at night | Night shift. That bench by the ward is warm again. It always is.
:: 2 | a whisper, only at night | Shh. Everybody's sleeping. Even the generator is whispering.
:: 3 | a whisper, only at night | The statue in the garden faces this way at night. Funny, eh?

::: Common 6 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, proud | Blackout again. We stitched a hand by phone torch yesterday.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | Students come in tired, not sick. Eat, sleep, come back.

::: Common 7 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | The mall has three generators. We have one. A brave one.
:: 2 | to yourself, sharp, quick | They cut our line again. Somebody is selling our light.

::: Common 8 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Strike. The students who stayed come here to talk. We listen.
:: 2 | to yourself | Free clinic today. Bring anybody who's been sitting too still.
:: 3 | to yourself, proud | Whatever happens outside, the night nurses are here.

::: Common 9 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | I can't confirm what's in any video. Confidentiality. Sit.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | Medicine Week stall: free blood pressure, free advice, free smiles.

::: Common 10 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | An old patient came back to say thank you. Five years later. Made my week.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | Our house officers are graduating. Who will we shout at now?

::: Common 11 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week | Freshers' medical! Line here, forms there, stop panicking.
:: 2 | exam week, firm | Exam week. No sleep, headaches. Water, food, bed. Not more coffee.
:: 3 | results day, dry, deadpan | Results day. One fainted, one danced. Both are fine. Sit.

::: Common 12 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, proud | Strike or not, this ward stays open.
:: 2 | cup final day, dry, deadpan | Football injury? Sit. Ice. The match is over. Let it go.
:: 3 | election week | Election week. Sore throats from shouting. Honey and warm water.
:: 4 | graduation day, warm | Convocation. Our nurses' children are graduating. The ward is smiling.

::: Common 13 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Fasting and dizzy? Break gently. Water and dates first.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Christmas on the ward. The patients chose the colour of the lights.
:: 3 | Valentine's Day, amused | Visitors with flowers. Patients pretending not to be pleased.
:: 4 | harmattan | Harmattan. Dry eyes, cracked lips, coughs. Drink water. Cover up.
