# Meet KB

> For your face today? Three hundred naira.

He owns the corner shop and sets his prices by face and by mood, announced like a game-show host. Under the jokes he is a man who watches the sky. When his batteries get expensive, buy candles.

### The voice

Corner-shop owner, forties, quick and theatrical; prices "by face and by mood", announced like a game-show host. Under the jokes, a man who watches the sky and listens to the radio. Pidgin and English.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **64 lines**, about **559 words**. That's 6 mission lines and 58 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 7 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how KB talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *KB - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

## Common lines

These are the short lines KB says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | amused | Welcome! For your face today? Everything is negotiable. Except bread.
:: 2 | excited | My customer! Your face is fine today. Prices are fine too.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | Ah. That face. That face pays full price.
:: 4 | amused | How far? [How are you?] Look at the board before you talk.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | amused | Go well! Come back when your face is happier.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Bye. Prices change at sunset. So do faces.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | amused | Thank me? Thank your face. It got the discount.

::: Common 4 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | no, dry, deadpan | No credit. Credit is a different shop. It closed.
:: 2 | refusing, amused | Discount? Your face is not in the mood for discount.
:: 3 | confused, dry, deadpan | Who? There was nobody there fa. You want something or not?

::: Common 5 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | a joke, amused | Price by mood. Today my mood is: rent.
:: 2 | a joke, dry, deadpan | Same item, two prices? One is for you. One is for your friend's face.
:: 3 | a question | You have candles at home? …Just asking. Shopkeeper asking.

::: Common 6 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out, dry, deadpan, during a power cut | Light gone? Ehen. [I see.] You bought the batteries? No? Hm.
:: 2 | the lights go out, amused, during a power cut | Shop is open. Bring your own light. And your own face.
:: 3 | the lights come back, amused | Light! Batteries back to ₦200. Mood restored.
:: 4 | rain, amused, when it's raining | Rain! Umbrella ₦1,500. It's raining, my friend. Supply and demand.
:: 5 | a hot day, dry, deadpan, on a hot day | Cold water ₦100. Warm water ₦50. Choose your suffering.

::: Common 7 · Food and money
:: 1 | money, amused | For your face today? ₦300.
:: 2 | money, dry, deadpan | Yesterday ₦200. Today ₦350. Tomorrow? Ask tomorrow.
:: 3 | money, amused | Smile. …₦250. Smile again. …No, the first one was better.
:: 4 | money, dry, deadpan | Transfer? Network is in a meeting. Cash.
:: 5 | food, amused | Indomie, ₦250. Indomie with my advice, ₦300.

::: Common 8 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, amused | Chalk! Box of it. You understand my business model.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | A calculator? Fixed prices? You want to kill me?
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | A new blackboard. Big. …Now I can be twice as confusing.

::: Common 9 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | the day before a power cut | Batteries, ₦900. …What? Batteries have feelings too.
:: 2 | warm, the day before a bad night | Bread, ₦50. Today only. Don't ask why. Eat.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan, the day before a power cut | Candles, sold out. Since noon. Who knew?
:: 4 | amused | Rewriting the board. Chalk is my only staff.
:: 5 | a whisper | Radio says sunny. My radio says other things.
:: 6 | the day before a power cut | Torch, cheap today. Too cheap. Buy two anyway.
:: 7 | dry, deadpan | Matches, ₦5. Don't ask. I'm in a generous mood. Suddenly.

::: Common 10 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | only at night | Late. Last loaf. Take it. Tonight, take it.

::: Common 11 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, amused | Table today, shop tomorrow. Tell your friend with the 'brand'.
:: 2 | teasing, dry, deadpan | Your friend sells chargers in a basement. I sell faces. Different market.

::: Common 12 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | hello, excited | Grand opening! Prices by mood! Today my mood is: opening.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan, the day before a power cut | For your face? No. For the week. Batteries, triple.
:: 3 | to yourself, amused | The mall has escalators. I have a stool. Same function.
:: 4 | teasing, amused | Free torch with that man's face? I charge less for no face.

::: Common 13 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself | Strike prices. Garri cheap. Patience expensive.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm, the day before a bad night | Bread, ₦50. Saturday is a long night. I hear.
:: 3 | money, warm | Student in strike? For your face today? Half.

::: Common 14 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, amused | Famous face! Famous face pays famous price.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan, the day before a power cut | Kerosene up. Don't look at me like that. Look at the sky.

::: Common 15 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | hello, warm, the day before a bad night | Eat now. Bread is half price. Now, not later.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused, after “The Chancellor's Shadow” | Who would believe a shopkeeper? So I priced it.
:: 3 | to yourself, dry, deadpan, after “The Chancellor's Shadow” | True prices now. They're more boring. Batteries, ₦900.
:: 4 | looking back, a whisper, after “The Chancellor's Shadow” | The other radio. Still listening. For your face: free.

::: Common 16 · After graduation | The story is over. The player has come back to visit.
:: 1 | to yourself, amused | Batteries ₦200 every day now. I'm bored. Buy anyway.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Board is the same all week. Customers think I'm sick.

::: Common 17 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, excited | Freshers! New faces! New prices! Everybody, a fresh face price!
:: 2 | exam week, warm, in exam week | Exam week. Coffee ₦100. Rechargeable lamp, cheap. Read.
:: 3 | allowance day, amused | Allowance day! I can see it in your face. Prices up.

::: Common 18 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, warm, during the strike | Strike. Stay-behind price. Don't tell anybody.

::: Common 19 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Christmas, amused | Christmas! Biscuits by the carton. Go home with a face.
:: 2 | Valentine's Day, amused | Valentine. Chocolate by mood. Single face, discount.
:: 3 | harmattan, dry, deadpan | Harmattan! Vaseline ₦400. Your lips are paying, not you.
