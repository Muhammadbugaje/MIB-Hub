# Meet The Staff

> Formal, tired, and dry as chalk.

Lecturers, registry and bursary staff, and departmental secretaries. They have heard every excuse there is, and they are quietly proud of their students.

### The voice

Generic lecturers, registry and bursary staff, departmental secretaries; formal, tired, dry as chalk, quietly proud of their students.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **50 lines**, about **500 words**. Read straight through, it's **about 6 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. **More voices are welcome:** it's a crowd, so share the lines out among friends and family.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *The Staff - Common 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Common lines

These are the short lines The Staff says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Good morning. Are you in my class? You look like you are not.
:: 2 | bored, flat | Yes? Make it quick. I have a meeting about meetings.
:: 3 | warm | Ah, good morning. You came early. Good. Keep it so.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | firm | See me after class.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Go and read. Actually read.

::: Common 3 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | no, firm | No. Extension is not a human right.
:: 2 | refusing, dry, deadpan | Bring it on Monday. Monday of this week.

::: Common 4 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out | Light gone. Carry on. Read the board with your memory.
:: 2 | the lights go out, dry, deadpan | The generator? Bursary says it's 'in process'. Since 2011.
:: 3 | a hot day, firm | No fan, no light. Nobody faint. Fainting is not on the attendance.

::: Common 5 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | firm | …and I told them, the course outline is not a suggestion.
:: 2 | bored, flat | The portal is down. Network. Come back yesterday.
:: 3 | bored, flat | Bring the original, two photocopies and your patience.
:: 4 | dry, deadpan | Is this handwriting or a map of Kwari Dam?
:: 5 | proud | If the light goes, we continue. Knowledge is not on the grid.
:: 6 | dry, deadpan | Office hours are Tuesday. Today is Tuesday. My office is closed.
:: 7 | firm | Sign the attendance. Only for yourself. I can count.
:: 8 | dry, deadpan | Which of you is 'Group Four'? All of you? Okay.
:: 9 | proud | When I was a student, we read with a lantern. And we were grateful.
:: 10 | sharp, quick | Where is the class rep? Class rep! Come and rep the class!
:: 11 | dry, deadpan | The memo says 'urgent'. It was written last semester.

::: Common 6 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | bored, flat, only at night | Marking at midnight. Somebody wrote a poem in question two.
:: 2 | a whisper, only at night | The night porter says the clock chimed once. That clock does not chime.

::: Common 7 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, proud | Every week the light goes. Every week I bring chalk and courage.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | Freshers in the wrong theatre. Again. Every year, the same theatre.

::: Common 8 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | The mall has three generators. My department has one extension box.
:: 2 | to yourself, sharp, quick | Somebody is tapping power from the faculty line. My kettle knows.
:: 3 | to yourself, bored, flat | I'm on the committee for the new mall. I don't know why.

::: Common 9 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself, sad, low | I support the strike. I also miss my students. Both.
:: 2 | to yourself | Strike. My office is open if anyone wants to talk. Not teach. Talk.
:: 3 | to yourself, proud | Students holding free classes under the tree. That is a university.

::: Common 10 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | I'm in a video, apparently. I didn't say that. I said it slower.
:: 2 | to yourself, firm | Elections. Please. Not in my lecture. Campaign outside.
:: 3 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Everybody is famous this semester. Nobody submitted the assignment.

::: Common 11 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Project defence on Monday. Chapter three is still a rumour.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | Final years. I'll miss failing you. Joking. Mostly.
:: 3 | to yourself, warm | I watched you go from 'which theatre?' to 'which job?'

::: Common 12 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, firm | Welcome, freshers. Where you sit today is where you sit forever.
:: 2 | exam week, firm | Pens down at the bell. Not after. At.
:: 3 | exam week, firm | No phones. No notes. No sighing loudly.
:: 4 | results day, dry, deadpan | Results are out. My office is not a complaints department.
:: 5 | allowance day, dry, deadpan | Allowance week. Suddenly the textbook is 'on its way'.

::: Common 13 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, sad, low | Strike day. The theatre is empty. I came anyway. Habit.
:: 2 | cup final day, proud | I won't say who I support. Engineering. I said nothing.
:: 3 | election week, firm | Vote with your head. Not your stomach. Not the free biscuits.
:: 4 | graduation day, proud | Convocation. I'll be in the gown. Hot, proud, slightly late.

::: Common 14 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan | Ramadan. Lectures end early so people can break fast. Be on time.
:: 2 | Christmas, dry, deadpan | Merry Christmas. The assignment is still due on the fifth.
:: 3 | Valentine's Day, dry, deadpan | Valentine's Day is not on the timetable. The quiz is.
:: 4 | harmattan, dry, deadpan | Close the windows. The dust is attending without registering.
