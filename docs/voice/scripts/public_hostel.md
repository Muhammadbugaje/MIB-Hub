# Meet The Hostel

> Nosy, generous, and always borrowing salt.

The neighbours: corridor voices, the kitchen crowd, the water queue. Everybody knows everybody's business and shares everybody's food.

### The voice

Neighbours in Giginya, Kadanya and New Site: corridor voices, the kitchen crowd, the water queue; nosy, generous, always borrowing salt.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **51 lines**, about **491 words**. Read straight through, it's **about 6 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. **More voices are welcome:** it's a crowd, so share the lines out among friends and family.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *The Hostel - Common 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Common lines

These are the short lines The Hostel says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | Neighbour! You get salt? [Do you have salt?]
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Who's knocking? If it's for garri, I'm sleeping.

::: Common 2 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out, sad, low | NEPA! I was ironing! Half my shirt is smart!
:: 2 | the lights go out, amused | Who has a candle? I'll trade two cubes of Maggi.
:: 3 | the lights come back, excited | UP NEPA! Everybody charge! Charge! Charge!
:: 4 | the lights come back, excited | Light don come! [Power's back!] Plug in before it changes its mind!
:: 5 | rain, scared, when it's raining | Rain! Pack the clothes! Whose clothes? EVERYBODY'S clothes!
:: 6 | a hot day, sad, low | The room is an oven. I'm sleeping in the corridor. Don't step on me.

::: Common 3 · Food and money
:: 1 | warm | Garri for the room! Who has sugar? Groundnut? Anything?

::: Common 4 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | sharp, quick | Whose bucket is this? It's been in the queue since Monday.
:: 2 | excited | Water came! Bring every bucket! Bring the kettle! Bring the cup!
:: 3 | sharp, quick | Who used my stove and left the pot to marinate?
:: 4 | bored, flat | Somebody's alarm has been ringing since five. For nobody.
:: 5 | amused | Room twelve is cooking egusi. My door is open for no reason.
:: 6 | proud | I'm not snoring. I'm breathing with confidence.
:: 7 | dry, deadpan | Two rings on one stove, ten people waiting. Hostel economics.
:: 8 | amused | Somebody took my slippers. I'm wearing one of yours. We're even.
:: 9 | your normal voice | Pass the iron when you're done. When the light is done too.
:: 10 | amused | This corridor smells like noodles and ambition.

::: Common 5 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | sharp, quick, only at night | Quiet hours! It's quiet hours! Why are you singing?
:: 2 | a whisper, only at night | Who's walking on the sixth landing? We only have five floors.
:: 3 | a whisper, only at night | Baba Porter still keeps post for Room 606. Don't ask him.

::: Common 6 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Third blackout today. My laptop is charging on vibes.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | Freshers knocking on every door for 'small water'.
:: 3 | to yourself, warm, during a power cut | A candle on every windowsill. The hall looks like a birthday.

::: Common 7 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Our light is weak, mall light is strong. Somebody connected something.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | The fan is turning so slowly. It's just thinking about air.
:: 3 | to yourself, amused | He bought a rechargeable lamp from the mall. We all live in his room now.

::: Common 8 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Strike. Half the hall went home. The water queue is a dream now.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | Our floor is three people and a lizard. The lizard is the chairman.
:: 3 | to yourself, warm | Shared pot of garri on the corridor. Strike solidarity.
:: 4 | to yourself, bored, flat | I slept fourteen hours. The strike is a sleeping tournament.

::: Common 9 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, a whisper | The video from Room 9? Fake. Probably. Send it again.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | The hall candidate is giving out noodles. Democracy smells nice.
:: 3 | to yourself, amused | Everybody's practising their 'viral face' in the bathroom mirror.

::: Common 10 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, excited | Finalists packing. Who's taking the big bucket? I'm claiming it.
:: 2 | to yourself, sad, low | You're leaving? Who will I borrow salt from now?
:: 3 | to yourself, sad, low | This corridor will be so quiet next year.

::: Common 11 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, warm | New neighbours! Welcome! The tap works on Tuesdays.
:: 2 | exam week, a whisper | Exam week. The corridor is a library. Shhh, even your slippers.
:: 3 | results day, a whisper | Somebody screamed in Room 4. Good scream or bad scream?
:: 4 | allowance day, dry, deadpan | Allowance! Who owes who? Accounting first, celebration after.

::: Common 12 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, bored, flat | Strike. The water queue is just me and my thoughts.
:: 2 | cup final day, excited | Corridor TV! Bring chairs! Bring your faculty voice!
:: 3 | election week, amused | Election posters on the bathroom door. Strategic.
:: 4 | graduation day, bored, flat | Convocation tomorrow. The iron is booked till three a.m.

::: Common 13 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, excited | Sahur [pre-dawn meal] in thirty minutes! Who's cooking? Knock knock!
:: 2 | Ramadan, warm | Iftar plates going round the corridor. Take, take, there's plenty.
:: 3 | Christmas, warm | Merry Christmas, neighbour! Here, jollof. Don't argue.
:: 4 | Valentine's Day, a whisper | Flowers delivered to our corridor. The whole floor is investigating.
:: 5 | harmattan, dry, deadpan | Harmattan. The bucket water is so cold it's basically advice.
