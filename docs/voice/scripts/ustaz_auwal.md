# Meet Ustaz Auwal

> Ask him. (He means the pastor.)

The mosque teacher, and best friend of Pastor Sam since primary school. Gentle and dry, slower than Sam and twice as stubborn about suya. The two of them finish each other's sentences.

### The voice

Mosque teacher in his late forties, northern Hausa accent, gentle and dry, slower than Pastor Sam and twice as stubborn about suya. Best friends since primary school. "Ask him."

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **44 lines**, about **402 words**. That's 3 mission lines and 41 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 5 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Ustaz Auwal talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Ustaz Auwal - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Ustaz Auwal says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | Assalamu alaikum. [Peace be upon you.] Have you eaten?
:: 2 | warm | Ina kwana? [Good morning?] How is the reading? Slowly is fine.
:: 3 | amused | Have you seen Sam? He took my good pot. Again.
:: 4 | your normal voice | Sannu. [Hello.] Walk with me to the gate. Tell me something good.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Allah ya kiyaye. [God protect you.] Go well.
:: 2 | warm | Sai anjima. [See you later.] If you see Sam, tell him: the pot.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you, warm | Na gode. [Thank you.] Thank the one who cooked, not the one who served.
:: 2 | sorry, warm | It's forgiven. Forgiving is lighter than carrying.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | warm | You helped without being asked. That is the best kind.

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | amused | You came for the iftar food and stayed for the washing. Good.

::: Common 6 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes, warm | Yes. In sha Allah. [God willing.]
:: 2 | no, dry, deadpan | No. And if Sam says yes, he is wrong. Lovingly.
:: 3 | maybe | No, ask him.
:: 4 | confused, amused | Hm. When Sam is not here I lose half my sentences.
:: 5 | agreeing, warm | We agree. Sam will be shocked. Don't tell him yet.

::: Common 7 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | a question, amused | Sam says his jollof is better. Is this true? Speak freely.
:: 2 | a question | Did you sleep last night? Books are patient. You can rest.
:: 3 | a joke, amused | My suya system? Thirty-one years. He still won't get it.
:: 4 | a joke, dry, deadpan | Sam played striker once. The ball is still recovering.

::: Common 8 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | a hot day | Hot today. Shade, water, patience. In that order.
:: 2 | the lights go out, warm | No light. We have lanterns and good company. Enough.
:: 3 | rain, warm | Rain. Rahama ce. [It is a mercy.] Also, my shoes are inside.

::: Common 9 · Food and money
:: 1 | excited | Masa! Fresh! Sit. Sit. Eat first, talk after.

::: Common 10 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | Dates! The good ones. You have a teacher's heart.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, amused | Sam's team's scarf. Very funny. He sent you, didn't he?
:: 3 | loves the present, shy | A new kettle. Ah. The old one is older than Sam. Thank you.

::: Common 11 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | your normal voice | Thirty mats. Thirty-one. Who brought a mat? …Sam.
:: 2 | amused | If the ball is round, why does Sam's team always run sideways?

::: Common 12 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | New faces. If you are far from home, our door is open.

::: Common 13 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, amused | Sam bet a goat on the Cup. I will name it after him.

::: Common 14 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike, warm | Strike. Come for evening class. Free. Tea included.
:: 2 | looking back, amused, after “The Sit-Down” | That match. Sam cried 'substitute me!' I ran for two.

::: Common 15 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself | A rumour is a guest you didn't invite. Don't feed it.

::: Common 16 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Final year. Sam and I are praying for you. He's louder.

::: Common 17 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | exam week, warm | Exams. Do your part, then rest your heart.

::: Common 18 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | cup final day, amused | Cup day. Sam brought a drum. I brought earplugs.
:: 2 | graduation day, proud | Convocation. I cry. Sam cries more. Write that down.

::: Common 19 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Ramadan Kareem. Iftar at sunset. Sam's pots are already here.
:: 2 | Ramadan, amused | Sam is not fasting, but he hides his lunch from me. Good friend.
:: 3 | Christmas, warm | Christmas. I bring kunu to the carols. Every year. Sam sings flat.
:: 4 | harmattan | Harmattan. Cold mornings, early prayers, hot tea. Alhamdulillah. [Praise be to God.]
