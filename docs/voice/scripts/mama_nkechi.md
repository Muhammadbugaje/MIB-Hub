# Meet Mama Nkechi

> You have eaten? Don't lie to her.

She owns the buka, feeds half the campus on credit, and remembers every naira of it. Loud, loving, and able to hear a lie from the other side of a pot of stew.

### The voice

Buka owner, fifties, Igbo, loud and loving; Pidgin and a little Igbo. Feeds you on credit, remembers every naira, and can hear a lie from across the pot.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **64 lines**, about **574 words**. That's 14 mission lines and 50 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 7 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Mama Nkechi talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Mama Nkechi - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

::: Mission 1 · Belle First | Year 1, the first week. Cooking jollof on a borrowed hotplate. Then the lights go out for the first time.
:: 1 | firm | Fry the tomato till it stops shouting.
:: 2 | firm | Add water early and I will know.

::: Mission 2 · Love in the Time of NEPA | Year 2, second semester. Valentine's Day, with 300 naira in the bank.
:: 1 | warm | Sapa stew. It is mostly confidence.
:: 2 | firm | One onion. One tomato. One cube. And don't be proud.
:: 3 | firm | You have eaten today? Don't lie to me.

::: Mission 3 · Those Who Stayed | Year 3, during the strike. Two weeks into the strike. Most people have gone home. These are the ones who stayed.

::: Mission 4 · Garri Economics | Year 3, during the strike. Mubarak is selling garri from a polished wheelbarrow. Strike economics.
:: 1 | firm | So you are feeding my customers sand and sugar.
:: 2 | sharp, quick | ((Mubarak: “Mama, it is a lifestyle product—”)) Light your stove. Let them taste and judge.

::: Mission 5 · Cancelled | Year 4, first semester. A fake clip of the Fresher goes round a lecture hall, phone by phone.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | ((Mum: “Go and eat. Then call your father. He is pacing.”)) I have seen you eat.
:: 2 | warm | Person wey chop like that no fit keep secret. Sit.

:: 1 | firm | You have eaten? Don't lie to me.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | ((The Fresher: “Yes, ma.”)) Good. You will need it. Three hundred plates.

:: 4 | dry, deadpan | Everybody was a student once. Carry the pot.

# Common lines

These are the short lines Mama Nkechi says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | firm | You have eaten? Don't lie to me.
:: 2 | warm | Nwa m! [My child!] Sit. The rice is just ready.
:: 3 | warm | Bia! [Come!] Why your face be like Monday morning?
:: 4 | amused | Ehen. Hunger brought you. Not love. I know.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Go well. Come back before you faint, not after.
:: 2 | firm | Take this nylon of meat. Don't argue. Go.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you, warm | Thank God, not me. Then pay me.
:: 2 | sorry, warm | Ndo. [Sorry.] Food first. Then cry. In that order.

::: Common 4 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | amused | You are getting thin. Is it love or sapa? Both?

::: Common 5 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | refusing, firm | You will not leave half. Children are hungry somewhere. You.
:: 2 | no, dry, deadpan | No. Spaghetti is not soup.
:: 3 | confused, dry, deadpan | Who? There was nobody there fa. Only you and your hunger.

::: Common 6 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out, warm, during a power cut | Light don go? [Power's gone?] Firewood never fails. Sit.
:: 2 | rain, warm, when it's raining | Rain! Pepper soup weather. God is good.
:: 3 | a hot day, dry, deadpan, on a hot day | Heat? You think the pot is enjoying itself?
:: 4 | bumped into, firm | Mind the pot! Hot soup does not apologise!

::: Common 7 · Food and money
:: 1 | money, dry, deadpan | Credit? Your name is in the book. Page six. It is a long page.
:: 2 | money, warm | No money? Eat. The book is patient. I am less.
:: 3 | money, dry, deadpan | Meat is extra. Fish is extra. Your attitude is extra.
:: 4 | food, firm | Fry the tomato till it stops shouting.
:: 5 | food, amused | Small chop? This is a buka. Everything here is big chop.
:: 6 | food, firm | Add water early and I will know. I always know.

::: Common 8 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | Crayfish! Who taught you sense?
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | Cube from the mall? With English on it? Hm. Thank you.
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | A new pot. Chineke! [My God!] I will name it after you.

::: Common 9 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | firm | Who carried my ladle? Ladle no get leg!
:: 2 | amused | Thirty years of students. Their stomachs never graduate.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | Onions have gone up again. I am crying for two reasons.

::: Common 10 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | warm, only at night | Last pot. Come and scrape it before the cats do.

::: Common 11 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | hello, warm | Fresher! First meal is a welcome. Second meal is business.
:: 2 | food, firm | Belle first. Then the lecture. The lecture does not feed you.
:: 3 | teasing, amused | Your mother called you? Good. Did you tell her about my rice?

::: Common 12 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | food, warm | Sapa stew. It is mostly confidence.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | The mall food court. Plates like coins. Come back when hungry.
:: 3 | money, warm | Allowance late? I know. I can see it in your neck.

::: Common 13 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Strike or no strike, the pot is on. Who stayed, eats.
:: 2 | to yourself, firm | Garri and sugar again? Bia. Come and eat food.
:: 3 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “Garri Economics” | Your friend's garri. Sand and sugar. He is forgiven. Barely.

::: Common 14 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, amused | You are famous? Famous people also chop. Sit.
:: 2 | looking back, proud, after “Cook-Off” | Three hundred plates. Not one came back. Not one.

::: Common 15 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, sad, low | Final year. I am cooking slower. Maybe you will stay longer.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | Your page in the book. I tore it out. Don't tell anybody.

::: Common 16 · After graduation | The story is over. The player has come back to visit.
:: 1 | hello, warm | Senior! You came back. You have eaten? Don't lie to me.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | The new ones eat like you did. Like a hungry tax collector.

::: Common 17 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, warm | New faces, new stomachs. Let them come.
:: 2 | exam week, firm, in exam week | Exam week. You eat before you read. Brain needs rice.
:: 3 | allowance day, dry, deadpan | Allowance day. Now you remember the book. Page six.

::: Common 18 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, warm, during the strike | They closed the school. They did not close my pot.

::: Common 19 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm, in Ramadan | Ramadan Kareem. Pap and akara at sunset. I saved you some.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Jollof for Christmas! Going home? Take some for the road.
