# Meet The Traders

> Never once lost an argument about price.

Market women, food sellers, kiosk men and mall attendants. Loud, quick and generous.

### The voice

Market women, food sellers, kiosk men and mall attendants; loud, quick, generous, and never once lose an argument about price.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **53 lines**, about **513 words**. Read straight through, it's **about 6 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. **More voices are welcome:** it's a crowd, so share the lines out among friends and family.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *The Traders - Common 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Common lines

These are the short lines The Traders says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | excited | Customer! Come, come! I'll do you good price!
:: 2 | warm | Fine fresher, come! First buy is for luck!
:: 3 | warm | Sannu da zuwa! [Welcome!] What do you need?

::: Common 2 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | warm | Thank you, customer! Come back. Don't go to that other one.

::: Common 3 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | No. My price has children. They need to eat.
:: 2 | sharp, quick | Ah! You want to buy it at 1990 price?

::: Common 4 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | rain, scared, when it's raining | Rain! Cover the tomatoes! Customers can swim!
:: 2 | the lights go out, excited | Light don go! [Power's gone!] Candle! Torch! Rechargeable fan!
:: 3 | the lights go out, dry, deadpan | Batteries went up this morning. Don't ask me why. I just feel things.

::: Common 5 · Food and money
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | No change. Bring change, I'll give you change.
:: 2 | bored, flat | Transfer? Network says 'pending'. Me, I say cash.

::: Common 6 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | excited | Tomatoes! Pepper! Onions that will make you cry with joy!
:: 2 | amused | Pure water, cold! Ice-cold! Okay, cool. Reasonably cool.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | Last price? This one is the last price's last price.
:: 4 | excited | Garri white, garri yellow, garri for exam, garri for strike!
:: 5 | amused | You want discount? Go and ask NEPA for discount first.
:: 6 | bored, flat | Recharge card! Data! Network is not my department.
:: 7 | excited | Hot akara! Hot! Okay, warm. Come before it's cool.
:: 8 | amused | Suya don ready! [Suya is ready!] Yaji is free. Regret is extra.
:: 9 | warm | Indomie and egg! Two eggs if you greet me well.

::: Common 7 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | bored, flat, only at night | I'm closing! I'm closing! …Okay, what do you want?

::: Common 8 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, amused | Candles are moving faster than bread this year.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Light goes, candle money comes. I'm not happy. I'm honest.

::: Common 9 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | The mall sells the same thing at double. With air-conditioning.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | They go to the mall to look, then come here to buy.
:: 3 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | They say the mall never blinks. My stall blinks every evening.
:: 4 | to yourself, amused | Escalator this, escalator that. Can escalator sell you ugu?
:: 5 | to yourself, sharp, quick | Mall security asked me for a permit. To walk past.

::: Common 10 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself, amused | Strike? Market is fuller than ever. Everybody is a trader now.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Students selling to students. Who is buying? Me, I'm watching.
:: 3 | to yourself, warm | Sapa combo: garri, sugar, groundnut. Strike price.
:: 4 | to yourself, amused | One boy asked for credit till the strike ends. I said, which year?

::: Common 11 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, a whisper | Did you hear? They said the Plug Store is closing. They said. Who is 'they'?
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Every customer wants to film the bargain. Oya, film my price too.
:: 3 | to yourself, sharp, quick | Campaign people took my pure water. Free water, paid by me? No.

::: Common 12 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Five years you've been buying from me. Go and graduate. Come back rich.
:: 2 | to yourself, sad, low | My first customer is finishing. I'll cry. After you pay.
:: 3 | to yourself, warm | The market has more lights now. Somebody fixed something. God bless them.

::: Common 13 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, excited | Fresher! Bucket, mattress, padlock, I have everything. And patience.
:: 2 | freshers' week, warm | Welcome to campus! Your first bucket is from me. At a price.
:: 3 | exam week, excited | Brain Booster Tea! You'll remember everything. Even your debts.
:: 4 | allowance day, amused | Allowance day! Everybody remembers my name today.
:: 5 | market day, excited | It's Thursday! Everything is moving! Hold your bag!
:: 6 | market day, dry, deadpan | Market day price is today price. Tomorrow price is a different story.
:: 7 | market day, sharp, quick | Pickpockets are also shopping today. Keep your phone in front.

::: Common 14 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, bored, flat | Strike again. My customers are home. My tomatoes are not.
:: 2 | cup final day, amused | Groundnut for the match! It's how they place bets here.
:: 3 | election week, sharp, quick | Posters on my stall again. Who will pay for my paint?
:: 4 | graduation day, excited | Gown photos! Flowers! Small chops! Convocation is our Christmas.

::: Common 15 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, excited | Dates! Kunu! Kosai! [Bean cakes!] Before sunset, please!
:: 2 | Christmas, excited | Christmas chicken! Christmas lights! Who is travelling?
:: 3 | Valentine's Day, amused | Red balloon, red rose, red face. Valentine is good business.
:: 4 | harmattan, excited | Vaseline! Lip balm! Harmattan is here and so am I!
