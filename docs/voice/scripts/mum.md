# Meet Mum

> Have you eaten? No, properly. What did you eat?

The player's mother, heard mostly on the phone, usually on loudspeaker by mistake, in front of forty strangers. Love arrives as instructions, quickly, slightly too loud, one question ahead of the answer.

### The voice

The player's mother, fifties, mostly heard on the phone. Love delivered as instructions, fast, slightly too loud, always one question ahead. Calls the player "my child" or by name, never "my son" or "my daughter". "Have you eaten?"

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **91 lines**, about **950 words**. That's 17 mission lines and 74 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 10 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Mum talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Mum - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

# Common lines

These are the short lines Mum says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | Hello? Hello? Can you hear me? Have you eaten?
:: 2 | excited | (name)! Finally! Did your phone die or did you die?
:: 3 | warm | My child! I was just thinking of you. Have you eaten?
:: 4 | firm | Ehn, so you remember you have a mother.
:: 5 | warm | It's me. Don't panic, nothing has happened. Have you eaten?

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Eat. Pray. Call me.
:: 2 | firm | Don't follow bad friends. Don't follow good friends too closely either.
:: 3 | warm | Okay, go and read. Call me tonight. Not midnight. Tonight.
:: 4 | warm | Your father says hello. He's pretending he's not listening.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you, warm | Thank you, my child. Now say thank you to your father. He's sulking.
:: 2 | sorry, firm | Sorry? Sorry is not food. Go and eat, then say sorry.

::: Common 4 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | You took a bus. In the rain. With no umbrella. I raised you.
:: 2 | amused | You sound tired. Or grown. I can't tell on this network.

::: Common 5 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | no, firm | No. Ask your father. Then don't, because I already said no.
:: 2 | confused | Network is bad. Say it again. Shout it. Shout politely.

::: Common 6 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | firm | Have you eaten? Don't say 'garri'. Garri is not an answer.
:: 2 | warm | Are you sleeping well? Is your roommate clean? Is the roof leaking?
:: 3 | firm | Who is that laughing behind you? Are you reading or laughing?
:: 4 | your normal voice | Did you get the provisions? All of them? The Milo?

::: Common 7 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out, firm | No light there? Buy candles. Two. Don't read with your phone torch.
:: 2 | rain, firm | It's raining there? Where is your sweater? The blue one I packed?
:: 3 | a hot day | Drink water. Wear cotton. Don't sit in the sun like a lizard.

::: Common 8 · Food and money
:: 1 | money, a whisper | I added a little. Don't tell your father. He added a little too.
:: 2 | money, firm | That money is for food. Food. Not that phone screen.
:: 3 | food, warm | I've packed you soup in the cooler. Freeze it. Don't share it. Share some.

::: Common 9 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | loves the present, sad, low | You sent me a wrapper? With your own money? …I'm not crying. Network.
:: 2 | likes the present, warm | You sent a photo of your notes? Neat. Very neat. I'm showing everybody.

::: Common 10 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | firm | Mama Chidi has shown me another video. Is this what we sent you for?
:: 2 | warm | Your little one is asking for you. Talk to them. Here.

::: Common 11 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | hello, excited | First week! Have you found your class? Have you found food?
:: 2 | a question, firm | Did you greet the porter? Greet everybody. Greeting is free.
:: 3 | a question, warm | Is it big, the school? Are you lost? You're lost. I can hear it.
:: 4 | to yourself, sad, low | Your room at home is too quiet. I go in to dust it. It's not dusty.
:: 5 | to yourself, warm | Matric photo. Your aunt has framed it. Twice.
:: 6 | teasing, firm | And stand up straight in photos. You were bending.
:: 7 | results day, excited, after good results | Your results! Eat! Then tell me again! Slowly!
:: 8 | results day, warm, after bad results | It's the first one. Breathe. Eat. We'll talk to your father together.
:: 9 | allowance day, a whisper | Your father has sent it. Spend it like you have a mother.

::: Common 12 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | A shop? In a basement? Is there light down there? Ventilation?
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | Your friend Mubarak called me 'Mummy'. I like him. Don't follow him.
:: 3 | a question | This mall. Is it safe? Who owns it? Why is it so bright?
:: 4 | teasing, amused | Second year and you call less. I've noticed. I've written it down.
:: 5 | results day, proud, after good results | Better than last time! I told Mama Chidi. She went quiet.
:: 6 | results day, firm, after bad results | Two papers in one day, and this is what happened? Next time: sleep.
:: 7 | allowance day, firm | Allowance is in. Pay your debt at the buka first. I know you have one.

::: Common 13 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike, firm | Strike? Then come home. …You're staying? Why are you staying?
:: 2 | the strike, warm | You stayed. Fine. Then eat with those people who stayed with you.
:: 3 | money, a whisper | Things are tight here. Not bad. Tight. I'm sending what I can.
:: 4 | to yourself, warm | Your father has started reading your textbooks. To 'keep up'.
:: 5 | to yourself, sad, low | Your little one asked if strike means you're never coming back.
:: 6 | allowance day, warm | It's small this month. Make it stretch. Like I make soup stretch.

::: Common 14 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | You're on the internet. Mama Chidi sent it. Is this what we sent you to do?
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | …Send me the video. Your aunt wants it. And stand up straight.
:: 3 | to yourself | People are talking. Let them. Talking is free. Results are not.
:: 4 | results day, excited, after good results | Fourth year and still climbing! Eat something special. Not garri.
:: 5 | results day, warm, after bad results | One more year. You can fix it. I fixed your father. It took longer.
:: 6 | allowance day, dry, deadpan | You say you're earning now. Good. I'm still sending. Don't argue.

::: Common 15 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Final year. I've started a list for convocation. It has sections.
:: 2 | to yourself, sad, low | Five years. Your room at home doesn't know you anymore. I do.
:: 3 | a question | Your project. Explain it to me. Slowly. Pretend I'm your supervisor.
:: 4 | results day, excited | Final results! Have you eaten? Eat! Then tell me again!
:: 5 | graduation day, warm | Item one: find you. Item two: feed you. Have you eaten?
:: 6 | graduation day, dry, deadpan | Don't lie to me. Somebody taught me how to check.
:: 7 | allowance day, a whisper | Last allowance. I'm sending extra. Don't tell your father. He also did.

::: Common 16 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, firm | New freshers? Be kind to them. You were lost too. You still are, small.
:: 2 | exam week, firm | Exam week. Read, sleep, eat. If you skip one, let it be the laughing.
:: 3 | exam week, warm | I'm praying for you. Now go and read. God helps those who read.
:: 4 | results day, firm | Go and eat. Then call your father. He is pacing.
:: 5 | allowance day, warm | Money has entered. Have you eaten? Go and eat. Properly.

::: Common 17 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | cup final day | Football? Don't play in the sun. Don't fall. Did you win?

::: Common 18 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Ramadan has started there? Greet your friends who are fasting. Help them.
:: 2 | Christmas, excited | You're coming home for Christmas? I'm cooking already. Don't be late.
:: 3 | Valentine's Day, dry, deadpan | Valentine's. Focus on your books. Books will love you back.
:: 4 | harmattan, firm | Harmattan! Vaseline on your lips. And your elbows. Everywhere.
