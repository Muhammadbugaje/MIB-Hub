# Meet Hajiya Ladi

> Bring your book. Let her see your profit.

Head of the market traders. Formidable, fair and sparing with words. She reads account books on Thursdays and reads people every day of the week.

### The voice

Head of the market traders, fifties; formidable, fair, few words. Hausa first, English second. Reads account books on Thursdays and people every day.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **65 lines**, about **524 words**. That's 18 mission lines and 47 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 7 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Hajiya Ladi talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Hajiya Ladi - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

::: Mission 1 · The Plug | Year 1, first semester. Mubarak has a business opportunity in the Student Union basement. Come alone. Bring legs.
:: 1 | to Mubarak | You have customers.
:: 2 | firm, to Mubarak | You do not have a business.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan, to Mubarak | ((Mubarak: “Hajiya, I have a brand.”)) A business has a book. Bring it when you have one.

::: Mission 2 · Grand Opening | Year 2, first semester. A shiny three-storey mall has swallowed half the market. Grand opening day.
:: 1 | firm | A stall you hate is still a stall.
:: 2 | firm | Forty traders. One door. Four o'clock. You: carry.

::: Mission 3 · Mubarak's Crash | Year 2, second semester. A power bank from Mubarak's stall starts smoking in a lecture. His business collapses.

:: 3 | warm, to Nusaiba | ((Nusaiba: “It has a back page.”)) Two.

::: Mission 4 · Garri Economics | Year 3, during the strike. Mubarak is selling garri from a polished wheelbarrow. Strike economics.

# Common lines

These are the short lines Hajiya Ladi says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | your normal voice | Sannu. [Hello.] You are carrying or buying?
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Ina kwana. [Good morning.] Your face says you want credit.
:: 3 | warm | Yauwa. [Good.] You greeted first. Your mother trained you.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | your normal voice | Go. Count your change outside, not in my face.
:: 2 | warm | Sai anjima. [See you later.] Greet your people.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Thank me with a full book.

::: Common 4 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | firm | Market closes at four. The sun does not negotiate.

::: Common 5 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | no, firm | No. Ask again when you have a book.
:: 2 | yes | To. [Okay.] Thursday. Bring it Thursday.
:: 3 | refusing, dry, deadpan | Credit? I have daughters. I know the word 'later'.
:: 4 | agreeing | Good. Small. But good.
:: 5 | confused, dry, deadpan | Who? There was nobody there fa. Are you buying or dreaming?

::: Common 6 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | rain, firm, when it's raining | Rain! Tarpaulin! Grains first, gossip after!
:: 2 | a hot day, dry, deadpan, on a hot day | Shade is free. Water is twenty. Choose.
:: 3 | the lights go out, dry, deadpan, during a power cut | Market never had light. Market is fine.
:: 4 | bumped into, firm | Kai! [Hey!] That was tomatoes. Now it is stew.

::: Common 7 · Food and money
:: 1 | firm | Bring your book. Let me see your profit.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Price is price. Discount is for those who carry.
:: 3 | firm | Profit is not what you sell. It is what is left.

::: Common 8 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present | A new exercise book. You understand me.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | Mall perfume? I can smell the rent in it.
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | A calculator. Solar. Allah ya saka. [God reward you.]

::: Common 9 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | firm | Forty traders. One door. Everybody carry.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Pepper is up. Onions are up. Patience is down.
:: 3 | your normal voice | A stall you hate is still a stall.

::: Common 10 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Your friend has customers. He does not have a business.
:: 2 | teasing, dry, deadpan | A brand? A brand is a business with no book.

::: Common 11 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | New stalls. Smaller. Dearer. The escalator only goes up.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | That man gave us torches with his face. In the dark. Hm.
:: 3 | money, firm | A loan. Weekly. I read it on Thursdays.
:: 4 | looking back, warm, after “Mubarak's Crash” | The back page. Two. You saw it. Good.

::: Common 12 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself | No students, no market. Still I open. Somebody must.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | My eldest is marrying. Every uncle is coming. All of them.
:: 3 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “Padlock Receipts” | Two hundred padlocks. I told you. Ask my market.

::: Common 13 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, proud | My Aisha keeps three shops' books now. Better than mine.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Phone shops multiplying. Phones are not.

::: Common 14 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Your friend's book. I read it Thursday. I said nothing. Good sign.
:: 2 | money, dry, deadpan | Final year? Final price. Still no discount.

::: Common 15 · After graduation | The story is over. The player has come back to visit.
:: 1 | hello, warm | Graduate. Hm. You still owe me a Thursday.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | The lights stay on now. Business is boring. I like boring.

::: Common 16 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | allowance day, dry, deadpan | Allowance day. Today everybody is my customer.
:: 2 | market day, firm | Market day! Carry or move. Those are the only two jobs.
:: 3 | market day, dry, deadpan | Everybody wants a price. Nobody wants to carry.

::: Common 17 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, firm, during the strike | Strike. Prices stay. Hunger is not on strike.

::: Common 18 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm, in Ramadan | Ramadan Kareem. Dates are fair price this month. Only this month.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Christmas rush. Rice for everybody. Barka da Kirsimeti. [Merry Christmas.]
:: 3 | harmattan | Harmattan. Cover the grains. Cover your lips. Cover your book.
