# Meet Pastor Sam

> Ask him. No, ask HIM.

The chapel pastor, and best friend of Ustaz Auwal since primary school. Big laugh, Pidgin when he gets excited, and equal faith in food and football. The two of them finish each other's sentences.

### The voice

Chapel pastor in his late forties, big laugh, Pidgin when excited, best friends with Ustaz Auwal since primary school. Talks food and football with equal faith. "Ask him." "No, ask him."

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **45 lines**, about **437 words**. That's 4 mission lines and 41 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 5 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Pastor Sam talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Pastor Sam - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Pastor Sam says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | My child! Good morning! Have you eaten? Auwal says I must ask.
:: 2 | excited | How far? [How are you?] You look like someone who needs rice.
:: 3 | warm | God bless your morning. And your afternoon. I'm generous.
:: 4 | amused | Have you seen Auwal? He owes me a match. And a pot.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Go well. Walk in peace. Walk fast if it rains.
:: 2 | warm | God keep you. Greet your roommate. Greet Auwal if you see him.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you, warm | Thank you! Ah, gratitude. My favourite sermon. Short version.
:: 2 | sorry, warm | Forgiven. Quickly. Life is short and lunch is soon.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | warm | You helped carry the chairs. Heaven saw. So did the chairs.

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | amused | You missed service? Fine. You didn't miss the rice after. I saw.

::: Common 6 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes, excited | Yes! Amen! I mean, yes!
:: 2 | no, amused | No. And Auwal also says no. We agree. Write it down.
:: 3 | maybe | Ask him.
:: 4 | confused, amused | I'm lost. Auwal usually finishes my sentences. Where is he?
:: 5 | agreeing, warm | Correct! Auwal will agree too. Eventually. After arguing.

::: Common 7 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | a question, amused | Who makes better jollof, me or Auwal? Careful. Think.
:: 2 | a question, warm | How are your studies? And your sleep? And your mother?
:: 3 | a joke, amused | Auwal's team scored. I was praying. For the goalkeeper.
:: 4 | a joke, amused | Thirty-one years and he still won't tell me his suya spice.

::: Common 8 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | rain, warm | Rain. A blessing. Also a leak in the vestry. Both.
:: 2 | the lights go out, warm | Light's gone. We sing louder. The generator is in God's hands.
:: 3 | a hot day, amused | This heat! Even the choir robes are sweating.

::: Common 9 · Food and money
:: 1 | excited | Pepper soup! Who made it? Don't say Auwal. …It's Auwal.

::: Common 10 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | Puff-puff! You know my weakness. Don't tell Auwal.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, amused | A jersey of Auwal's team? …I will use it as a dishcloth. Lovingly.
:: 3 | loves the present, excited | A photo of me and Auwal, primary school! Look at our heads!

::: Common 11 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | amused | Two hundred chairs. Two hundred and one. Who brought a chair?
:: 2 | warm | Choir practice at six. Pray it doesn't rain. Pray it doesn't flat.

::: Common 12 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Freshers! Lost? Chapel is that way. Mosque is the other way. Both feed.

::: Common 13 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, amused | Auwal bet me a goat on the Cup. I'm praying for a draw.

::: Common 14 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike, warm | Strike? Then come and help. We are cooking for the ones who stayed.
:: 2 | looking back, amused, after “The Sit-Down” | Substitute me! I could not breathe! Auwal ran for both of us.

::: Common 15 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Rumours? Don't carry them. Carry chairs. More useful.

::: Common 16 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Final year. We are praying for you. Both of us. Different times.

::: Common 17 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | exam week, warm | Exams. Read, then rest, then pray. In whatever order works.

::: Common 18 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | cup final day, excited | Cup day! I support Engineering. Auwal supports whoever is losing.
:: 2 | graduation day, proud | Convocation! Thanksgiving! Jollof! My three favourite words.

::: Common 19 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Iftar tonight! Our pots go across the road, as every year.
:: 2 | Ramadan, amused | I'm not fasting. But I won't eat in front of Auwal. I'm not a monster.
:: 3 | Christmas, excited | Carols at seven! Auwal is bringing the kunu. He always does.
:: 4 | harmattan, amused | Harmattan. Everyone in the choir has catarrh. Very soulful.
