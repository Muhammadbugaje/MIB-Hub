# Meet Coach Danladi

> Shouts in proverbs. Loves in laps.

The football coach. A whistle round his neck, a proverb for every mistake, and a heart he keeps well hidden behind the touchline. "Pass! Is the ball your inheritance?"

### The voice

Football coach, fifties, northern accent, a whistle round his neck and a proverb for everything. Shouts with love. "Pass! Is the ball your inheritance?"

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **48 lines**, about **432 words**. That's 5 mission lines and 43 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 5 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Coach Danladi talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Coach Danladi - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

::: Mission 1 · Left Boot First | Year 1, first semester. Football trials at five o'clock. Jega picks the Fresher because of the goat.
:: 1 | firm | ((Jega: “Always. Don't argue with success.”)) Pass! Is the ball your inheritance?

::: Mission 2 · The Vice-Chancellor's Cup | Year 1, second semester. The football final. Education are a player short, and somebody has read the rule book.
:: 1 | firm | ((Jega: “…You can laugh small.”)) Out! Out! The grass is not coming to you!

::: Mission 3 · Comeback | Year 4, second semester. Jega's comeback, in the cup final.
:: 1 | firm | ((Taty: “Left, right, who cares? Bora! Run!”)) Boots are boots! Outside! The grass is waiting!

::: Mission 4 · Contract | Year 5, second semester. A football academy wants Jega now. A pen, a contract and a bus ticket.
:: 1 | firm | ((The football scout: “Sign today. Report Monday. Football does not wait.”)) Is the degree your inheritance? Is the contract?
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Me, I have neither. I have a whistle.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Coach Danladi says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | excited | You! Good! Legs warm? No? Why not?
:: 2 | firm | Sannu. [Hello.] The early bird gets the ball.
:: 3 | warm | Ah, my striker of the future! Or the past. We'll see.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | firm | Go. Eat. Sleep. Come back with legs.
:: 2 | warm | Sai gobe. [See you tomorrow.] Bring your boots, not your excuses.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | warm | Thank you. A goat that greets gets grass.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | proud | Good touch! The calabash is full!

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | firm | Pass! Is the ball your inheritance?
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | You dribbled yourself. Twice. Impressive. Terrible.
:: 3 | firm | The chicken that won't run becomes soup!

::: Common 6 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | walking along, excited | Run with me! A river that stops is a pond!
:: 2 | waiting for the player, firm | Faster! The grass is not coming to you!
:: 3 | waiting for the player, firm | Are you walking? Is this a wedding?

::: Common 7 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes, excited | Yes! Like that! Again!
:: 2 | no, firm | No! Shoot with the foot, not the face!
:: 3 | agreeing, warm | Correct. The elder sees from sitting what the youth sees from the tree.
:: 4 | confused, dry, deadpan | What is this formation? A queue?

::: Common 8 · Winning and losing | After a game, a match or a bet.
:: 1 | after winning, excited | We won! Don't celebrate too long. The ball is round. It comes back.
:: 2 | after losing | We lost. Good. Now we know where the holes are.

::: Common 9 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | bumped into, firm | Up! Up! The ground is not your bed!
:: 2 | after a fall, dry, deadpan | Dive later. You're not in a film.
:: 3 | rain, excited | Rain? Perfect! The ball slides, the brave slide with it!
:: 4 | a hot day, firm | Hot? Drink water. The sun did not ask to play.

::: Common 10 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | A new whistle! Ah! Now they will hear me in the next state.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | A chair? A coach does not sit. …Leave it there.
:: 3 | loves the present, proud | Old Cup photo. Look at me. Look at my legs then. Ah!

::: Common 11 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | firm | Left boot first! Jega is right about one thing.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Cones. Who took my cones? A cone is not a hat!

::: Common 12 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, excited | The Cup is coming. The lazy will watch from the stands.
:: 2 | looking back, proud, after “Left Boot First” | That boy Jega. Left boot first. The goat knows its path.

::: Common 13 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself | A scout in the stands. Play as if nobody is watching. Somebody is.

::: Common 14 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike, firm | Strike? Football does not strike. Pitch at five.
:: 2 | the strike, dry, deadpan | Half the team went home. The other half ate garri. Both are slow.
:: 3 | to yourself | An empty pitch is a sad drum. Come and play.

::: Common 15 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | Boots are boots! Outside! The grass is waiting!

::: Common 16 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself | Is the degree your inheritance? Then finish it.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | Me, I have no degree, no contract. I have a whistle. Enough.

::: Common 17 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, warm | Trials Saturday. Bring boots. Borrowed boots count.
:: 2 | exam week | Exams. Go and read. A footballer who fails is benched at home.

::: Common 18 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | cup final day, excited | Cup day! Drums! Who brought my lucky towel?
:: 2 | cup final day, firm | One match at a time. You cannot eat the whole goat in one bite.

::: Common 19 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan | Ramadan: training after iftar. Light drills. Water first.
:: 2 | harmattan, excited | Cold morning jog! The dust is free training!
