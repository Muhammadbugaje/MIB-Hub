# Meet Big Baby

> Tiny. Terrifying. Adored.

She owns the salon in the market and the heart of the biggest man on campus. She has never raised her voice, because one word from her does the work of a speech. That word is usually "Bolaji."

### The voice

Twenties, salon owner in the market, Bolaji's girlfriend. Tiny, crisp, utterly in charge; never raises her voice because she never needs to. One word does the work of a speech. Warm underneath, and adores him.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **54 lines**, about **523 words**. That's 3 mission lines and 51 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 6 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Big Baby talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Big Baby - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Big Baby says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | firm | You. Sit. Your edges are crying.
:: 2 | warm | Ah, my dear. Welcome. Shoes off the mat.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | Hello. Bolaji's not here. That's why it's quiet.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Go well. Don't touch your hair for two days.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | warm | Thank you. Now go and tell the market who did your braids.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | warm | Your hair is behaving today. Finally. Who did it? …Me. Good.

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | You came in with that hair? After harmattan? Brave.
:: 2 | amused | You walk like Bolaji when he's in trouble. Fast. Looking back.

::: Common 6 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | waiting for the player, firm | Hurry. My clients don't wait. You're not a client yet.
:: 2 | walking along | Walk me to the salon. Carry this. It's heavy. You're fine.

::: Common 7 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | confused, dry, deadpan | What? Say it again. Slowly. I braid while I listen.
:: 2 | confused, dry, deadpan | Who? There was nobody there fa. Sit down.
:: 3 | no, firm | No.
:: 4 | yes, warm | Yes, my dear.
:: 5 | refusing, firm | No credit. Not even for you. Not even for him.

::: Common 8 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | your normal voice | Have you seen Bolaji? Tell him: outside.

::: Common 9 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | bumped into, firm | You bumped me. I didn't move. Think about that.
:: 2 | a hot day, firm | This sun. Hat. Now. Your scalp is not a road.
:: 3 | the lights go out, dry, deadpan | NEPA. Generator. Bolaji. Pull the cord. …Bolaji.
:: 4 | rain | Rain. Everybody runs into the salon. Pay or wait outside.

::: Common 10 · Food and money
:: 1 | money, dry, deadpan | Price is price. Bargain with Bolaji. He says yes to everyone.
:: 2 | food, warm | Suya. One for me, three for Bolaji. He thinks I don't count.

::: Common 11 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | For me? You're a good one. Sit. Free wash.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | Hm. I'll give it to Bolaji. He keeps everything.
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | Hair oil from Kano. The real one. You can come back any time.

::: Common 12 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | firm | Bolaji.
:: 2 | warm | Look at him. Gentle hands. I taught him that.

::: Common 13 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Salon's closed. Even I sleep. Sometimes.

::: Common 14 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | Bolaji. Outside. …No, not you. You're fine.
:: 2 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “Fresher Tax” | Two hundred naira from freshers? I fixed that. One word.
:: 3 | being nice, warm, after “Sly's Last Stand” | You stood up to him. He liked that. He won't say. I'm saying.

::: Common 15 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, proud | Sly's Cuts. I taught him. He still holds the clippers like a baby bird.
:: 2 | looking back, warm, after “Sly Goes Straight” | Malam Garba sat in the chair. I cried next door. Don't tell Bolaji.
:: 3 | teasing, amused | You want a cut from him or me? Me. Obviously. He's still learning.

::: Common 16 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself | Strike. Half my customers gone. The other half are here all day. Gossiping.
:: 2 | looking back, amused, after “Workshop Nights” | He built a tower. He came home with sawdust in his ears. I was proud.
:: 3 | the strike, dry, deadpan | Strike. Bolaji's cutting hair for free. I'm charging for both of us.

::: Common 17 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | He's at the gym at six. For Jega. He came home and slept standing up.
:: 2 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “The Collab” | Lucky wanted him in a video. I said no. He said no. Then I said no again.
:: 3 | being nice, warm | You're a good friend to him. He doesn't have many. He has the right ones.

::: Common 18 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, proud | He ended the tax. Himself. I just watched. That's love.
:: 2 | looking back, firm, after “Fresher Tax, Reversed” | Bolaji.
:: 3 | looking back, warm, after “Fresher Tax, Reversed” | He said he cried in a salon. It was my salon. I gave him tissues.
:: 4 | goodbye, warm | Convocation. Come for braids first. I'm booked from five. You're at four-thirty.

::: Common 19 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, firm | Freshers. If anybody asks for money on the court, come to me.
:: 2 | market day, dry, deadpan | Market day. Thirty heads before noon. Bolaji's sweeping. Good.

::: Common 20 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | graduation day, warm | He's practising Abbas's name. All week. In my mirror. I'm so proud.

::: Common 21 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Ramadan. I close early for iftar. Bolaji carries the pots for the mosque grounds.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Christmas. Everybody wants braids for the village. I'll sleep in January.
:: 3 | Valentine's Day, warm | Valentine's. He brought one rose. I said 'Bolaji.' He knows what it meant.
:: 4 | harmattan, firm | Harmattan. Oil your scalp. I won't say it twice. I'll say it three times.
