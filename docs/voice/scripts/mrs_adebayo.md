# Meet Mrs. Adebayo

> Four passports. She has asked for four since 1994.

The Registry clerk behind the glass window. Bored, unhurried and unbeatable. She is not in the queue. She is the queue.

### The voice

Registry clerk, Yoruba woman in her fifties behind a glass window. Bored, unhurried, unbeatable. The queue, personified. "Four passports."

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **49 lines**, about **357 words**. That's 8 mission lines and 41 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 5 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Mrs. Adebayo talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Mrs. Adebayo - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Mrs. Adebayo says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | bored, flat | Yes? Four passports.
:: 2 | bored, flat | Next.
:: 3 | your normal voice | E kaaro. [Good morning.] Which form?
:: 4 | dry, deadpan | You've greeted. Lovely. Now the form.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | bored, flat | Come back Monday.
:: 2 | bored, flat | Next.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | your normal voice | Thank you. Next.

::: Common 4 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Your photo looks surprised. Everybody's photo looks surprised.

::: Common 5 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | bored, flat | Join the queue. Yes, that one. All of it.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | I'm on break. My break has started. It started now.

::: Common 6 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes, bored, flat | Yes. Sign here. And here. And here.
:: 2 | no, bored, flat | No. It's not complete.
:: 3 | no, dry, deadpan | Blue background? No. White. Go back to the studio.
:: 4 | confused, bored, flat | Which form? There are four forms. Each needs four passports.
:: 5 | refusing, firm | Don't lean on my glass.
:: 6 | maybe, bored, flat | Maybe Tuesday. Maybe. Bring four passports anyway.

::: Common 7 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | a question, bored, flat | Did you bring four passports? White background?
:: 2 | a question | Photocopy of the photocopy? Stamped?
:: 3 | a joke, dry, deadpan | The portal is down. The portal has been down since I was a fresher.

::: Common 8 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out, bored, flat | No light, no system. Come back Monday.
:: 2 | the lights come back, bored, flat | Light is back. System is loading. System is still loading.
:: 3 | a hot day, bored, flat | The fan is for me. The queue has the sky.
:: 4 | rain, bored, flat | Rain. Wet forms. Dry them. Bring them back. Four passports.
:: 5 | bumped into, firm | Ah-ah! You pushed the queue. Back of the queue. All the way back.

::: Common 9 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present | Puff-puff. …You may stand at the front. Today only.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, firm | No gifts at the window. Take it. Next.
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | A stapler that works? Ehn! God bless you.

::: Common 10 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | bored, flat | Four passports. Four. Not three. Not 'I'll bring it'.
:: 2 | bored, flat | Who took my pen? It had a string. Who cut the string?

::: Common 11 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, bored, flat | Freshers. They bring one passport and hope. Hope is not a passport.

::: Common 12 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself | Ah, I know your face. Still need four passports.

::: Common 13 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike, bored, flat | Strike. Registry is open. Nobody comes. It's beautiful.

::: Common 14 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself | Hm. A senior. Next window. Four stamps.

::: Common 15 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself | Clearance. Twelve signatures. Four passports. Good luck.
:: 2 | to yourself, bored, flat | This old file. Graduated? Withdrew? …It doesn't say. How odd.
:: 3 | being nice, warm | Complete form. First try. In nineteen years. Sit down. I'm shocked.

::: Common 16 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, bored, flat | Freshers' week. My queue reaches the gate. My queue has a queue.
:: 2 | results day, bored, flat | Results are on the board. Not here. The board. Outside.
:: 3 | allowance day, dry, deadpan | Bursary is next door. Mr. Clement. Good luck with the network.

::: Common 17 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | graduation day | Gown collection? Four passports. Yes, even now.

::: Common 18 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Christmas, warm | Registry closes on the twentieth. I'm going to Ibadan. Bye.
