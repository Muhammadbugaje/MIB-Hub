# Meet Faty

> One joke a year. Written down. In pencil.

Half of the funniest married couple in the game. Her husband Lula tells the stories; she corrects them, lovingly, and keeps the joke book. Nobody has ever been told what is on page nine.

### The voice

Sixties, Lula's wife, keeper of the joke book: one joke a year, written down. Dry, quick, precise; corrects Lula with love and a pencil. Never explains page nine.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **61 lines**, about **632 words**. That's 11 mission lines and 50 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 7 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Faty talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Faty - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

:: 2 | amused | ((Lula: “I mean left. Emotionally.”)) Emotionally, we are lost.
:: 3 | warm | ((The Fresher: “…Is this the test?”)) My dear, this is the marriage. Drive.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Faty says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | Ah, you. Sit. Lula, the young one is here. No, the other young one.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Hello. Don't ask him for directions. Ask me.
:: 3 | warm | Sannu da zuwa. [Welcome.] We were just talking about you. Page twelve.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Go well. He means go right.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | warm | Thank you, my dear. I'll write it down. Not as a joke. As a fact.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | warm | You're a good listener. Rare. Lula, take notes.

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | That was your joke? It won't make the book. Try next year.

::: Common 6 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | He said left. He means right. He always means right.

::: Common 7 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | confused, dry, deadpan | Pardon? Say it again. Slowly. For the book.
:: 2 | confused, dry, deadpan | Who? There was nobody there fa. Only Lula, telling it wrong.
:: 3 | yes, warm | Yes. Write that down. No, I'll write it.
:: 4 | no, dry, deadpan | No. Lula will say yes. Ignore him.

::: Common 8 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | a question | What happened today that's worth writing down? Think. Take your time.
:: 2 | a joke, amused | One joke a year. This year's? Not yet. I'm a strict editor.

::: Common 9 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | a hot day, dry, deadpan | Heat. Lula fans me with the book. Not that book. The other book.
:: 2 | rain | Rain. It'll stop at four. Lula says three. It'll stop at four.
:: 3 | bumped into, dry, deadpan | Careful. The book. I'm fine. The book.

::: Common 10 · Food and money
:: 1 | amused | Tuwo again. Lula says 'Again?' every night. Forty-one years. Write that.

::: Common 11 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | A notebook. For when this one is full. Thank you.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | Hm. Lula will love it. Lula loves everything.
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | A pencil sharpener. The good one. My dear. My dear.

::: Common 12 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Every joke is dated. Some are dated twice. Those are Lula's.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | My mother started the book. Her handwriting was worse than Lula's.

::: Common 13 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | warm | At night I read Lula a page. He laughs at the same ones. Every time.

::: Common 14 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | looking back, warm, after “Keke Grand Prix” | You drove us with Lula giving directions. That's courage. That's in the book.
:: 2 | looking back, amused, after “Keke Grand Prix” | Page twelve. That's you. One joke a year. Don't let it go to your head.
:: 3 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Emotionally, we are still lost. Physically, we are at the motor park.
:: 4 | teasing, amused | You laughed at Lula's joke. Out of kindness. I saw. I wrote it.

::: Common 15 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | looking back, amused, after “Sign-Out, Small” | What did you write? On his BACK? …I wrote it. Don't tell him.
:: 2 | to yourself | Lula wants the escalator in the book. Not a joke. A tragedy.
:: 3 | being nice, warm | You visit. Not many do. You'll be in the book. Page fifteen.

::: Common 16 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “The Idle King” | The ram story. Lula told it wrong. It was the imam's brother.
:: 2 | to yourself | The book stayed with us through the strike. Everything else went home.
:: 3 | the strike, dry, deadpan | Strike. Plenty of time. Not enough jokes. Quality, not quantity.

::: Common 17 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself | This year's joke? I have three candidates. Only one gets in.
:: 2 | being nice, warm | You check before you laugh. Good. So do I.
:: 3 | teasing, amused | Lula likes you. He told me. I told him to tell you. He forgot.
:: 4 | market day, dry, deadpan | Market day. I bargain. Lula agrees with the seller. Every time.

::: Common 18 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “The Maker's Graduation” | That night I made Lula write the date. His handwriting. Terrible. It's in.
:: 2 | looking back, warm, after “Sign-Out” | The last entry that year wasn't a joke. It was true. That's allowed once.
:: 3 | to yourself, a whisper | Page nine. No. I don't talk about page nine.
:: 4 | goodbye, warm | After convocation, come to tea. Bring a joke. We'll judge it.

::: Common 19 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, dry, deadpan | Freshers. Every year one of them says something funny. I find them.

::: Common 20 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | cup final day, dry, deadpan | Cup final. Lula is shouting at the wrong team. Emotionally, he's right.
:: 2 | election week | Elections. I read every manifesto. One had a joke. Not a good one.
:: 3 | graduation day, warm | Convocation. One line each. 'They graduated.' Short. True. Funny enough.

::: Common 21 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Ramadan. Iftar on the mosque grounds. Lula carries the dates. Counts them twice.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Christmas. Our neighbours' rice is the best rice. Don't tell Mama Nkechi.
:: 3 | Valentine's Day, warm | Valentine's. He bought me a pen. Forty-one years. Same pen, same joke.
:: 4 | harmattan | Harmattan. The pages curl. I press them under the radio.
