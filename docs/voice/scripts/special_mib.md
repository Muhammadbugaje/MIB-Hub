# Meet M.I. Bugaje

> Three letters at the top of every high-score table.

A calm man in white and gold who is seen rarely and explained never. He is very kind and very quiet. He speaks in short lines that can be read two or three ways, and he is gone before the conversation feels finished.

### The voice

Calm, kind, quiet and faintly mysterious. A poet's short lines, with room around them. Never hurries. Never explains.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **45 lines**, about **325 words**. Read straight through, it's **about 4 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how M.I. Bugaje talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *M.I. Bugaje - Common 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Common lines

These are the short lines M.I. Bugaje says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | You're early. Or I'm late. Both are fine.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Ah. There you are. Again.
:: 3 | a whisper | Hello. Don't look surprised. I'm always somewhere.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Go on. I'll be here. Or near.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Until the next time you don't expect me.
:: 3 | amused | I'll leave first. It's a habit.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you, warm | Don't thank me. Pass it on. That's the receipt.
:: 2 | thank you | You're welcome. You always were.
:: 3 | sorry, warm | No need. You'll do better. You already have.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | warm | Good. Now do it without me.
:: 2 | amused | Not bad. Not finished. Nothing good is.

::: Common 5 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | waiting for the player | Take your time. It's the only thing that's yours.
:: 2 | walking along, warm | Walk ahead. I like to see where people go.

::: Common 6 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes | Yes. You knew before you asked.
:: 2 | no, warm | No. Not yet. 'Yet' is a kind word.
:: 3 | maybe, amused | Perhaps. Most true things start there.

::: Common 7 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | a question | What are you carrying? No. The other thing.
:: 2 | a question, warm | Did you eat? Small things first.
:: 3 | a joke, dry, deadpan | Three letters. People assume they stand for something.

::: Common 8 · Winning and losing | After a game, a match or a bet.
:: 1 | after winning | Again.
:: 2 | after winning, amused | One point. It's always one point.
:: 3 | after losing, warm | You won. Don't spend it all at once.
:: 4 | after losing, amused | Well played. I'll remember. I remember most things.

::: Common 9 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | bumped into, amused | Careful. You'll need those elbows later.
:: 2 | after a fall, warm | Up. The ground is a teacher, not a bed.
:: 3 | the lights go out, a whisper | Dark. Look up. That part is free.
:: 4 | the lights come back, warm | There. Someone was listening.
:: 5 | rain, warm | Rain doesn't hurry. Neither should you.
:: 6 | rain, amused | Wet shoes, dry heart. A fair trade.
:: 7 | a hot day, dry, deadpan | White in the sun. You'd think I'd planned it.

::: Common 10 · Food and money
:: 1 | warm | Share it. It lasts longer that way.

::: Common 11 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | warm | For me? Keep half. Give me the other half later.

::: Common 12 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | your normal voice | Play.
:: 2 | amused | The table is never wrong. Only early.
:: 3 | your normal voice | Thirteen is lucky, for people who count.
:: 4 | a whisper | Every game is two games. The one you play, and the one you learn.
:: 5 | your normal voice | Gold is only white that waited.

::: Common 13 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | a whisper | Late. Good. The quiet hours are honest.
:: 2 | warm | Go home. The night will keep.

::: Common 14 · After graduation | The story is over. The player has come back to visit.
:: 1 | hello, warm | You came back. They usually do.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | New names on the table. Good. It was lonely up there.
:: 3 | to yourself, a whisper | The market has more stalls at night. Count them.
:: 4 | to yourself, warm | A cap fits better the second time.
:: 5 | being nice, warm | You're the original now. Someone will copy you. Let them.
:: 6 | goodbye, warm | Go well. Leave a light on for the next ones.
