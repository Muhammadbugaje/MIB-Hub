# Meet The Faithful

> On the way to prayers, in their best.

Ordinary people going to and from the mosque and the chapel: neighbours, mothers, students. Warm, unhurried and respectful. Faith in this game is ordinary and never a joke.

### The voice

Ordinary people on their way to and from the mosque and the chapel: neighbours, mothers, students in their best; warm, unhurried, respectful.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **49 lines**, about **431 words**. Read straight through, it's **about 5 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. **More voices are welcome:** it's a crowd, so share the lines out among friends and family.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *The Faithful - Common 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Common lines

These are the short lines The Faithful says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | Assalamu alaikum. [Peace be upon you.]
:: 2 | warm | Wa alaikum salam. [And upon you, peace.]
:: 3 | warm | Good morning! Happy Sunday!
:: 4 | warm | Barka da Juma'a! [Happy Friday!]

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Sai anjima. [See you later.]
:: 2 | warm | Go well. See you next Sunday.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | warm | Na gode. [Thank you.] Allah ya saka da alheri. [God reward you.]
:: 2 | warm | Thank you! God bless you, eh.

::: Common 4 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | rain, warm, when it's raining | Rain! Share the umbrella. Two heads, one roof.
:: 2 | rain, when it's raining | Rain on Friday. The mats are inside. Come in, come in.
:: 3 | the lights go out, warm | Light's gone, but the hymn knows its way. Keep singing.
:: 4 | the lights go out | No light for the microphone. His voice carries anyway.

::: Common 5 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | amused | Who took my slippers? …Oh. They're mine. Sorry, sorry.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Hurry, it's almost time. Walk fast, but walk well.
:: 3 | bored, flat | Choir practice ran long. My voice is resting till Sunday.
:: 4 | warm | The tap by the mosque is working today. Alhamdulillah. [Praise God.]
:: 5 | excited | Mama, wait for me! I'm coming! I'm wearing my good shoes!
:: 6 | warm | After prayers, tea at Mai Shayi Audu's. Like every Friday.
:: 7 | amused | Pastor Sam and Ustaz Auwal are sharing one umbrella again.
:: 8 | your normal voice | Juma'a crowd. Park well, leave space for the keke.
:: 9 | your normal voice | Somebody left a bag at the gate. Whose bag? Come and take it.
:: 10 | warm | We prayed about the exams. Now we read for the exams. Both.

::: Common 6 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | only at night | Late prayer done. Walk home together, it's dark.
:: 2 | only at night | Vigil tonight. Bring a sweater and a friend.

::: Common 7 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Candles in the chapel, lamps at the mosque. Everybody's lighting something.

::: Common 8 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself | Collection for the generator fund. Every little helps.
:: 2 | to yourself | The mall played music during Juma'a. Somebody asked. They lowered it.

::: Common 9 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Fewer people at Juma'a this strike, but they're greeting harder.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | Small congregation this Sunday. We sang louder to fill the room.
:: 3 | to yourself, warm | Food after service for the students who stayed. Take a plate. Take two.

::: Common 10 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself | The sermon was about honest talk. Election week. Good timing.
:: 2 | to yourself, firm | Don't believe everything on your phone. Check, then speak.

::: Common 11 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Final years asking for prayers. Of course. Come.
:: 2 | to yourself, proud | My son graduates this year. I'll be grateful in the front row.

::: Common 12 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, warm | Freshers! The mosque is there, the chapel is there. Welcome.
:: 2 | exam week | Exam week. Mosque full, chapel full, library full. Everybody's working.
:: 3 | results day, warm | Results day. Thanksgiving at service. Some loud, some quiet.
:: 4 | allowance day, warm | Allowance came. First, give a little. Then the market.
:: 5 | market day | Thursday. Market first, then evening prayer, then rest.

::: Common 13 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, warm | The chapel is open for anybody who needs a quiet place.
:: 2 | cup final day, excited | After prayers, to the stadium! The match won't wait.
:: 3 | election week | Pray for good leaders. Then go and vote for one.
:: 4 | graduation day, proud | Convocation thanksgiving. Families in their best, grinning.

::: Common 14 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Iftar in ten minutes! Dates are on the table, the water is cold.
:: 2 | Ramadan, warm | Ramadan Kareem! [Blessed Ramadan!] Come and break fast with us.
:: 3 | Ramadan, excited | Sallah tomorrow! New clothes, visits, everybody greeting!
:: 4 | Christmas, warm | Carol night at the chapel! Everybody's welcome. Bring your voice.
:: 5 | Christmas, warm | Merry Christmas! The chapel lights are on. Come and see.
:: 6 | harmattan | Harmattan dawn. Cold water for ablution. Brrr. Bismillah. [In God's name.]
