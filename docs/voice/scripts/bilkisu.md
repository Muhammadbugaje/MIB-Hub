# Meet Sgt. Bilkisu

> A whistle first. Then words. Then you're in trouble.

Head of campus security, known as "Madam Whistle". Fierce and fair, and nothing gets past her gate. She also secretly washes the football captain's socks, and will deny it under oath.

### The voice

Head of campus security, northern woman in her forties, "Madam Whistle". A whistle first, then words. Fierce, fair, secretly soft (she washes Jega's socks). "You. Yes, you."

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **54 lines**, about **489 words**. That's 10 mission lines and 44 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 6 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Sgt. Bilkisu talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Sgt. Bilkisu - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Sgt. Bilkisu says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | firm | (whistle) You. Yes, you. …Good morning.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Ina kwana. [Good morning.] ID card. Thank you. Carry on.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | I saw you coming from the gate. I see everything from the gate.
:: 4 | firm | Walk, don't run. Running makes me curious.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | firm | Go. Straight to your hall. Straight. Not through the garden.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Sai anjima. [See you later.] I'll be watching. Kindly.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you | Thank you. That goes in my good book. It's a small book.
:: 2 | sorry, firm | Sorry? Write it on the incident form. Two copies.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | warm | You returned a lost wallet. I noticed. I notice good things too.

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | You climbed that fence? I saw. The fence saw. We both laughed.
:: 2 | firm | That's my camera you're waving at. It's not waving back.

::: Common 6 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | firm | Stand there. Don't move. I'm counting my keys.

::: Common 7 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes | Yes. Go. Quickly, before I change my mind.
:: 2 | no, firm | No. Gate closes at ten. Ten. Not ten-ish.
:: 3 | refusing, firm | I don't take bribes. I take names.

::: Common 8 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | firm | Where were you at noon? Don't think. Answer.
:: 2 | sharp, quick | Whose bicycle is that? Whose? Exactly.

::: Common 9 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | bumped into, firm | (whistle) Who pushed who? Both of you. Here. Now.
:: 2 | the lights go out, firm | Light gone. Torches out. Everybody walk in pairs.
:: 3 | the lights go out | Dark? I have three torches and one temper. Move along.
:: 4 | rain, dry, deadpan | Rain. My whistle doesn't work wet. I'll just shout.

::: Common 10 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | Kosai. Hot. …I am still watching you. Thank you.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, firm | Is this to make me forget something? Take it back.
:: 3 | loves the present, shy | A new whistle. Silver. I… nobody gives security presents.

::: Common 11 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | firm | Sixty keys. Fifty-nine. Who? …Sixty. Found it.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Somebody's socks again. Jega. Always Jega. I'll wash them.

::: Common 12 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | firm | After ten. What are you doing out? …Reading? Show me the book.
:: 2 | a whisper | The garden's humming again. Don't go in. Just a feeling.

::: Common 13 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | A goat in the food court. On my shift. I'm still writing the report.

::: Common 14 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | looking back, after “The Plan” | I'm a heavy sleeper on Saturdays. Remember that.
:: 2 | to yourself, firm | The mall gave us every key. Every key. I count them.

::: Common 15 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike | Strike. Empty campus. Easy shift. I hate it. Too quiet.
:: 2 | the strike, warm | You stayed? Good. I like faces on my rounds.

::: Common 16 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | A video of you at the court? I was in my other camera. Asleep.
:: 2 | to yourself, firm | Posters on my gate. Again. Elections are a security matter.

::: Common 17 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Five years. Not one incident form. Two. Fine. Two.
:: 2 | to yourself | Who is that you're with? …Ah. Never mind. Carry on.

::: Common 18 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, firm | Freshers! Hostel is that way. That way! The other that way!
:: 2 | exam week, firm | Exam hall. No phones. I can see your pocket glowing.

::: Common 19 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | cup final day, firm | Cup day. Drums yes, pitch invasion no. (whistle)
:: 2 | election week, firm | Sound systems off at ten. Even for the winner.
:: 3 | graduation day, warm | Convocation. Every parent wants to park at the Senate. No.

::: Common 20 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Valentine's Day, dry, deadpan | Valentine's. Gate closes at ten. Love can be home by ten.
:: 2 | harmattan | Harmattan haze. Can't see the gate from the gate. Walk carefully.
