# Meet Mai Shayi

> Sit. Tea first. Gist second.

Audu, the tea seller under the neem tree, has been pouring tea there since the tree was a stick. He is the campus memory. Every mystery in the game ends with somebody saying: ask Mai Shayi.

### The voice

Audu, fifties, the campus tea seller and its memory. Unhurried, amused, a voice for gossip told low. Every visit starts with tea; the gist always comes second.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **62 lines**, about **546 words**. That's 12 mission lines and 50 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 7 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Mai Shayi talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Mai Shayi - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Mai Shayi says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | Sit. Tea first, gist second.
:: 2 | warm | Sannu da zuwa! [Welcome!] The kettle knew you were coming.
:: 3 | amused | Ah, my customer. Sugar three, problems four?
:: 4 | warm | Ina wuni. [Good afternoon.] Sit before your legs decide.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Go well. Come back with gist. Tea is free for good gist.
:: 2 | amused | Sai anjima. [See you later.] Bring back my cup.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | warm | Don't thank me. Pay me. Then thank me.

::: Common 4 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | your normal voice | Who? There was nobody there fa. Drink your tea.
:: 2 | amused | You are talking to the bench? The heat has entered you.

::: Common 5 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | a question, warm | Ehen? [And so?] What happened? Start from the middle.
:: 2 | a question, a whisper | You heard about the corridor that cries? No? Sit.
:: 3 | a joke, amused | Riddle for tea? A riddle costs double.
:: 4 | a joke, dry, deadpan | Lipton is tea. What I make is medicine.

::: Common 6 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out, warm, during a power cut | Light don go? [Power's gone?] Good. Bring your stool closer.
:: 2 | the lights go out, warm, during a power cut | Charcoal does not need NEPA. Sit.
:: 3 | the lights come back, amused | Up NEPA! …Don't clap too loud. They hear.
:: 4 | rain, warm, when it's raining | Rain! Under the canopy, all of you. Tea goes up ten.
:: 5 | a hot day, dry, deadpan, on a hot day | Hot tea in hot weather. It cools you. Don't argue.

::: Common 7 · Food and money
:: 1 | money, dry, deadpan | Tea is fifty. Gist is free. Credit is a long story.
:: 2 | money, amused | Transfer? My kettle does not have network.
:: 3 | food, warm | Bread and tea. Half the degrees in this school ran on it.

::: Common 8 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | Sugar! You understand business.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | Coffee? In my house? Take it back, before the kettle sees.
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | A new kettle? …Hm. The old one will be jealous. Thank you.

::: Common 9 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | your normal voice | Forty first-years. Forty. And every one sat on that stool.
:: 2 | amused | That radio gets one station. One. I stopped fighting it.
:: 3 | a whisper | An okada at three. Nobody can describe the rider. Hm.
:: 4 | amused | That boy's love life. I need more sugar to discuss it.

::: Common 10 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | warm, only at night | Last cup. Then go in. The road is darker than it looks.

::: Common 11 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, a whisper | The cape on the weathervane. Count Entropy. Brr. Drink.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | Wrong theatre? Every fresher. Gabas, Yamma. Twins.
:: 3 | teasing, amused | Fresher, you are holding that note like a lottery ticket.
:: 4 | looking back, warm, after “Convocation Night” | We drew it a face. Now you know. Sugar?

::: Common 12 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | The mall sells tea for nine hundred. In a paper cup. Haba.
:: 2 | to yourself, a whisper | Your friend's shop. The basement. People talk, you know.

::: Common 13 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Those who stayed. Sit. Tea is on the house till they call it off.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Empty campus. I still boil the full pot. Habit.

::: Common 14 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, amused | You are on the phone screens now. Still owe me twenty.
:: 2 | to yourself, when the Maker is away | The cape is down tonight. Hm. It will be back.

::: Common 15 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, sad, low | Finalists. Every year you go, the bench gets lighter.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | Tea first. Light second. That has always been the order.

::: Common 16 · After graduation | The story is over. The player has come back to visit.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | He comes every morning now. Sits down. Drinks it hot.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | The clock strikes now. My customers come on time. Terrible.

::: Common 17 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, amused | New faces! New credit! Allah ya kiyaye. [God protect us.]
:: 2 | exam week, warm, in exam week | Exam tea. Extra strong. No gist, only reading.

::: Common 18 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, dry, deadpan, during the strike | No lectures, no customers. Only gist. Gist doesn't pay.
:: 2 | cup final day, amused | The Cup today. Half my customers are hoarse already.
:: 3 | graduation day, warm | Gown on, cup in hand. Don't spill. Your mother is watching.

::: Common 19 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm, in Ramadan | Kettle sleeps till sunset. Then: tea for everybody.
:: 2 | harmattan, warm | Harmattan! Best season for tea. Worst season for lips.
