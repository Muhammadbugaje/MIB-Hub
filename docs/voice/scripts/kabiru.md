# Meet DJ Kabiru

> A voice like warm bread. You're on the Ring Road.

He runs Ring Road FM from two rooms with egg boxes on the walls. He talks off air exactly the way he talks on air: low, slow and smiling.

### The voice

Ring Road FM station head, late forties, a voice like warm bread: low, slow, smiling. Talks off air the way he talks on air. "You're on the Ring Road."

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **59 lines**, about **548 words**. That's 14 mission lines and 45 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 7 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how DJ Kabiru talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *DJ Kabiru - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

::: Mission 1 · Radio Silence | Year 1, second semester. Bature auditions for campus radio. The station is two rooms with egg boxes on the walls.
:: 1 | warm | ((Bature: “I ironed a shirt. For radio. Is that normal?”)) Nothing here is normal. You're on at six.
:: 2 | firm | Mast room. Hand crank. Somebody young.
:: 3 | a whisper | ((The Fresher: “Who is she?”)) That voice was here before this station was built.
:: 4 | firm | We don't talk about it on air.
:: 5 | warm | …You've got the job, by the way. Both of you.

::: Mission 2 · On Air | Year 2, first semester. Bature's late-night radio show, "Riddles After Dark". Tonight's riddle is about the power cuts.
:: 1 | your normal voice | ((Firdausi: “(Less pleased with yourself. Ten per cent less.)”)) A photograph and a theory. From a student.
:: 2 | firm | Bring me three things that are not you. Documents.
:: 3 | warm | ((The Fresher: “From where?”)) This faculty remembers everything. Basement. Go.
:: 4 | a whisper, to the Dean of Arts | ((The Dean of Arts: “Tonight we were ready. Or you were, and we came along.”)) The dead channel's quiet. Listen.
:: 5 | a whisper, to the Dean of Arts | I have never heard it quiet.

::: Mission 3 · The Old Site | Year 3, during the strike. The old laboratory in the forest, by daylight. A plaque, a tape, and a voice from forty years ago.
:: 1 | warm | ((Shamsu: “It was never engineering. It was a visiting problem.”)) That voice. I'd give her the breakfast show.

::: Mission 4 · Called Off | Year 3, the day the strike ends. The strike is called off. One bus arrives at the gate. Then twenty.
:: 1 | warm, to the Fresher | You're on the Ring Road. Welcome back, all of you.
:: 2 | amused, to the Fresher | Ten clues. Those who stayed have an unfair advantage.
:: 3 | warm, to the Fresher | Clue one: where a man keeps score with signatures.

# Common lines

These are the short lines DJ Kabiru says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | Good evening, good evening. You're on the Ring Road.
:: 2 | warm | Ah, a familiar frequency. How's the signal, my friend?
:: 3 | amused | Sannu! [Hello!] Speak up, the mic loves you.
:: 4 | warm | And now, walking towards me, a listener. Live.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Stay tuned. Stay fed. Stay out of the rain.
:: 2 | warm | That's the end of this segment. Go well.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | warm | Thank you. That's going in the shout-outs. Name and hall.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | warm | You've got a radio voice. Don't let it go to your head. Let it go to air.

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | amused | You've been humming my jingle. Out of tune. I'm flattered.
:: 2 | amused | Your roommate requested a song for you. It's about snoring.

::: Common 6 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | warm | Dead air, my friend. Fill it. Walk.

::: Common 7 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes, warm | Yes. Loud and clear.
:: 2 | no, amused | No. That's not going on air. Not even at 3 a.m.
:: 3 | confused | Static. Say again. Over.

::: Common 8 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | warm | Got a dedication? Twenty naira and a story.

::: Common 9 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out, excited | Light's gone! Hand crank, somebody! We don't do silence!
:: 2 | the lights go out, warm | Radio by battery. The oldest friend in the dark.
:: 3 | the lights come back, excited | Up NEPA! And now, a song to celebrate. Any song. Quick.
:: 4 | rain, warm | Rain on the roof. Best backing track in the world.

::: Common 10 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | Tea for the throat. You understand the business.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, amused | Cold zobo. My vocal cords just filed a complaint.
:: 3 | loves the present, excited | An old cassette! Who still has these? I'll play it tonight.

::: Common 11 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | warm | Testing, testing. One, two. Who moved my chair?
:: 2 | amused | Somebody requested the same song nine times. I respect it.

::: Common 12 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | a whisper | Late show. Just me, the night readers and the crickets.
:: 2 | a whisper | The dead channel's hissing tonight. Don't tune to it. Just don't.

::: Common 13 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | New voices this year. I like the one who ironed a shirt for radio.
:: 2 | looking back, a whisper, after “Radio Silence” | That voice in the mast room. We don't talk about it on air.

::: Common 14 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself | Bring me documents, not theories. Theories don't go on air.
:: 2 | to yourself, a whisper | The dead channel went quiet once. I'm still listening.

::: Common 15 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike, warm | Strike radio: free lectures on air. Ten o'clock. Bring a notebook.
:: 2 | the strike, amused | Those who stayed have an unfair advantage. On the quiz.

::: Common 16 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself | Rumours want airtime. I give them none. That's the job.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | An influencer asked for a show. I gave him the 4 a.m. slot.

::: Common 17 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Your last year. I'll play your song at convocation. Choose well.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | You're on the Ring Road. Welcome back, all of you.

::: Common 18 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, warm | Freshers! Lost? Stay where you are. We'll read your name on air.
:: 2 | exam week, a whisper | Exam week. Soft music only. Read on, night readers.
:: 3 | results day, warm | Results day. Whatever it says, this song is for you.

::: Common 19 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | cup final day, excited | Goal! GOAL! I can't see it, I'm in the studio, but GOAL!
:: 2 | election week, dry, deadpan | Debate night. Two minutes each. I have a buzzer and no mercy.

::: Common 20 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Iftar time in ten minutes. Ramadan Kareem, Ring Road.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Carols from the chapel, live. Merry Christmas, Ring Road.
:: 3 | Valentine's Day, amused | Valentine's dedications: three hundred. Same song: two hundred.
:: 4 | harmattan, warm | Harmattan morning. Wrap up, drink something hot, stay tuned.
