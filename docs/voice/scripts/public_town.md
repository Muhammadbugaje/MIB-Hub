# Meet The Town

> A proverb or a complaint for every kind of weather.

The elders, mothers, artisans and tailors of the town around the campus. Unhurried, nosy and generous.

### The voice

Elders, mothers, artisans and tailors of the town around campus; unhurried, nosy, generous, with a proverb or a complaint for every weather.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **43 lines**, about **401 words**. Read straight through, it's **about 5 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. **More voices are welcome:** it's a crowd, so share the lines out among friends and family.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *The Town - Common 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Common lines

These are the short lines The Town says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | Ina kwana? [Good morning?] You greet well. Good child.
:: 2 | warm | Ẹ káàárọ̀! [Good morning!] How's school?
:: 3 | warm | Sannu da aiki! [Well done with your work!]
:: 4 | warm | Nnọọ! [Welcome!] Come and sit small.

::: Common 2 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out, sharp, quick | Light don go again! Bring the lantern, the real one!
:: 2 | the lights go out, warm | No light, no fan, no TV. Then let's tell stories.
:: 3 | rain, excited, when it's raining | Rain! The clothes! Children, the clothes!
:: 4 | a hot day, dry, deadpan | This heat. Even the goats are queueing for shade.

::: Common 3 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | The tailor said Thursday. Which Thursday? He's a philosopher.
:: 2 | amused | Bread went up again. Even the bread is surprised.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | In my time, that clock worked. In my time, many things worked.
:: 4 | warm | My son, come and help me carry this. Your back is young.
:: 5 | sharp, quick | Generator noise everywhere! Who can hear the radio?
:: 6 | bored, flat | Welder! Welder! He's not here. He is 'coming'.
:: 7 | amused | I took my shoe to the cobbler. Now he's renting it back to me.
:: 8 | dry, deadpan | That pothole has been there since my wedding. It's family now.
:: 9 | firm | Children! Out of the road! Okada is not a toy!
:: 10 | amused | These students, always running. Where is everybody going?
:: 11 | your normal voice | Grinding machine! Pepper, tomatoes! Bring your bowl!

::: Common 4 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan, only at night | Lock your doors. Not for thieves. For mosquitoes.
:: 2 | only at night | The old man in the university garden? He greets well. That's all I know.

::: Common 5 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Every evening NEPA takes light. We've stopped being angry. Mostly.

::: Common 6 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | The new mall: big glass, big lights. Our street is still dark.
:: 2 | to yourself, sharp, quick | Somebody is tapping wires at night. My meter is crying.

::: Common 7 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | The students who stayed for the strike help everybody. Good children.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | My tailor has no customers. He's sewing his own trousers.

::: Common 8 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Everybody's pointing a phone at everybody. Then who is living?
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Election posters on my wall. I didn't agree. My wall did.

::: Common 9 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | These students came small. Now look. Gowns. Ahh, time.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | The town feels brighter. More lamps on the road. Somebody did something.

::: Common 10 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, firm | Freshers in town! Hold your bags well. The market is a teacher.
:: 2 | exam week, amused | Exam season. Students buying Brain Booster Tea like medicine.
:: 3 | results day, warm | My neighbour's son passed! He's dancing in the road. Let him.
:: 4 | allowance day, amused | Allowance day. The market will be loud tonight. Good for business.
:: 5 | market day, proud | Thursday! I've been awake since four, ready to bargain.

::: Common 11 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, dry, deadpan | Strike again? In my day— well, also strike. Same.
:: 2 | cup final day, excited | The whole town is shouting for the university. Who else?
:: 3 | election week, dry, deadpan | Candidates. Promise everything, deliver pamphlets.
:: 4 | graduation day, bored, flat | Convocation. Tailors haven't slept. Everything is 'Thursday'.

::: Common 12 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Iftar at my house today. Come, there's enough.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Merry Christmas! Come and eat rice. Don't say no.
:: 3 | Valentine's Day, dry, deadpan | In my time, Valentine was called 'go and greet her father'.
:: 4 | harmattan, firm | Harmattan. Wear a sweater. Yes, you. I can see you shivering.
