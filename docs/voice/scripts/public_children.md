# Meet The Children

> Every student is a teacher. Every goat is a friend.

School pupils and town kids, five to eleven years old. Loud, honest and fast. If you have children at home who would enjoy shouting into a phone, this is their script.

### The voice

Demonstration School pupils and town kids, five to eleven: loud, honest, fast, convinced every student is a teacher and every goat is a friend.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **43 lines**, about **409 words**. That's 1 mission line and 42 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 5 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. **More voices are welcome:** it's a crowd, so share the lines out among friends and family.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *The Children - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

## Common lines

These are the short lines The Children says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | excited | Good morning, sir! Good morning, ma! Good morning, goat!
:: 2 | excited | Lafiya lau! [Very well!]
:: 3 | excited | Uncle! Aunty! Look at my shoe!

::: Common 2 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | your normal voice | Why is the big clock stopped? Is it tired?
:: 2 | your normal voice | Why does the goat have a name and I don't have a goat?

::: Common 3 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out, excited | NEPA took light! When it comes we'll shout UP NEPA!
:: 2 | the lights go out, amused | Torch under my chin! Look! I'm a teacher!
:: 3 | rain, excited, when it's raining | Rain! Dance! Mummy will shout, but dance!

::: Common 4 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | proud | I can count to a hundred. Listen. One, two… I'll do it later.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Teacher said the sun is a star. I said it's too close.
:: 3 | shy | Are you a teacher? You're big. Are you a teacher?
:: 4 | excited | Race me! Ready, steady— I already started!
:: 5 | proud | I found a snail. His name is Professor.
:: 6 | amused | My big sister says she's 'reading'. Her eyes are closed.
:: 7 | your normal voice | Uncle, is university like school but no uniform?
:: 8 | amused | My brother is at university. He sleeps there.
:: 9 | amused | One sweet and I'll tell you a secret. The secret is I want a sweet.
:: 10 | excited | Tag! You're it! No, you're it! We're both it!
:: 11 | proud | I can almost do a front-flip. Almost is the hard part.

::: Common 5 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | only at night | Mummy said come home before the street lights. There are no street lights.
:: 2 | a whisper, only at night | The cats sit in a circle. I counted them. I forgot the number.

::: Common 6 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, proud | Light went in class. Teacher said 'keep singing'. We sang loud.

::: Common 7 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, excited | We went to the mall! There's a staircase that walks!
:: 2 | to yourself | My daddy says the light is weak because somebody is borrowing it.

::: Common 8 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself, excited | University people are teaching us under the tree! They're funny!
:: 2 | to yourself | Are you on strike? My mummy said everybody is on strike.

::: Common 9 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, proud | Somebody filmed me dancing. I'm famous. My grandma saw it.
:: 2 | to yourself, proud | I want to be president. Of the class. Then the country. Then snacks.

::: Common 10 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, excited | You're finishing school? Do you get the big hat?
:: 2 | to yourself, proud | When I'm big I'll come to this university. Keep my seat.

::: Common 11 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, amused | Look! New big students! They're lost like us on first day!
:: 2 | exam week | My brother is reading. Even when he eats, he's reading.
:: 3 | allowance day, excited | My brother got his allowance! He bought me puff-puff! He's my favourite!
:: 4 | market day | Market day. Mummy said hold her wrapper and don't let go.

::: Common 12 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, excited | The big students are free! Like Saturday every day!
:: 2 | cup final day, excited | GOAL! I didn't see it, but GOAL!
:: 3 | election week, excited | Vote for me for class monitor! I'll give you chalk!
:: 4 | graduation day, excited | Big hats! Big hats everywhere!

::: Common 13 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, proud | I'm fasting till lunch! Half fast! Baby fast!
:: 2 | Christmas, proud | New clothes! Don't touch. They're for Christmas.
:: 3 | Valentine's Day | Valentine is when big people buy red things and smile.
:: 4 | harmattan, amused | Harmattan! My lips are crunchy!
