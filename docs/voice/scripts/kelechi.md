# Meet Kelechi

> Clay doesn't rush. Neither does he.

A sculptor in Fine Arts, a year ahead of the player, with clay up to his elbows. He says very little, and he noticed something in the Sculpture Garden that nobody else has seen in forty years.

### The voice

Mid-twenties, Fine Arts sculptor, a year ahead of you. Low, slow, few words; clay under his nails. Pauses as if he is still shaping the sentence. "Clay doesn't rush."

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **105 lines**, about **1,051 words**. That's 11 mission lines and 94 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 10 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Kelechi talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Kelechi - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Kelechi says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | Ndewo. [Hello.] Sit. Don't touch the wet one.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Ah. You. Hold still. Your jaw is useful.
:: 3 | warm | Morning. The light is good today. Stay in it.
:: 4 | your normal voice | Hello. Mind the chisel. It has opinions.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Go. Come back when the clay is dry.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Bye. Walk slowly. You miss things.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you, warm | Daalu. [Thank you.] That was kind. I'll remember the shape of it.
:: 2 | sorry, shy | Sorry. Clay on your sleeve. It washes. Mostly.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | warm | You have good hands. You don't use them. Yet.
:: 2 | your normal voice | You stand well. Like you're listening.

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | amused | You talk while you work. The clay can hear you rushing.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | You call that a sketch? It's a rumour of a sketch.

::: Common 6 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | walking along | I'll walk with you. Slowly. Clay doesn't rush. Neither do I.
:: 2 | waiting for the player, dry, deadpan | Take your time. I'm used to waiting. Stone waits better.
:: 3 | the player went the wrong way | Wrong garden. Sculptures are this way. The ones that look at things.

::: Common 7 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes | Yes.
:: 2 | no | No. Not yet.
:: 3 | maybe, warm | Maybe. Let it dry. Then ask again.
:: 4 | confused | Hm. Say it again. Slower. With your hands.
:: 5 | confused, dry, deadpan | Who? There was nobody there fa. Only the statues.
:: 6 | agreeing, warm | Yes. That's the line. Keep that line.
:: 7 | refusing | No. It's not finished. Nothing's finished. But no.

::: Common 8 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | a joke, amused | What did the clay say to the kiln? 'Don't rush me.' …I only have one joke.
:: 2 | a question | What do you see? Not what you know. What you see.

::: Common 9 · Winning and losing | After a game, a match or a bet.
:: 1 | after winning, warm | We won. Good. Now back to work.
:: 2 | after losing | We lost. Clay forgives. People should too.

::: Common 10 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | bumped into | Careful. I'm fine. The bust is fine. That's what matters.
:: 2 | after a fall, dry, deadpan | I'm down. Good angle from here. I'll get up later.
:: 3 | the lights go out, warm | Light's gone. Good. You feel the shape better in the dark.
:: 4 | the lights come back | Light. Now I see what I did in the dark. Hm.
:: 5 | rain, warm | Rain. The clay pit will be perfect tomorrow.
:: 6 | a hot day, dry, deadpan | Heat. The clay cracks. So do I. Water, please.

::: Common 11 · Food and money
:: 1 | food, warm | Abacha from Mama Nkechi. [Cassava salad.] Tastes like home. Don't tell her I cried.
:: 2 | money, dry, deadpan | Clay is cheap. Bronze is not. I work in clay.

::: Common 12 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | For me? Thank you. I'll keep it on the shelf by the window.
:: 2 | not keen on the present | Hm. It's very… finished. Thank you.
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | A new chisel. Good steel. You listened. Daalu. [Thank you.]

::: Common 13 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | your normal voice | Turn. Look. Wait. Turn.
:: 2 | warm | Clay doesn't rush.

::: Common 14 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | a whisper | At night the garden is different. The statues rest. Don't wake them.

::: Common 15 · Remember when | Little nods to things that have already happened in the story.
:: 1 | warm, after “Six Plaques” | Stand where she's standing. You remember? You saw it. Good.
:: 2 | after “Six Plaques” | Six gazes. They crossed. I only noticed because I had nothing to do.

:: 3 | to yourself, warm | Somebody who loved her made that one. Look at the chisel.

::: Common 17 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | hello | Second year. The mall bought a fountain. It has no face. Sad.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | They want a statue for the mall. Of Chief Megawatt. In bronze. No.

:: 7 | a question | Have you been to the Old Eye? I'd like to see it. Not at night.
:: 8 | Valentine's Day, shy | Valentine. I made a small heart in clay. It cracked. Typical.

::: Common 18 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | hello, warm | You stayed. Good. Empty campus. Best light I've ever had.
:: 2 | to yourself | Strike. No crits. No deadlines. I've made nine heads. All of Sly.
:: 3 | to yourself, warm | Clay from the dam. Better than shop clay. Baba Dam showed me where.
:: 4 | looking back, warm, after “Workshop Nights” | The tower above the dam. Half a tower. It's a good sculpture.
:: 5 | being nice, warm | You kept people together. That's sculpting too. With people.
:: 6 | teasing, dry, deadpan | You learned to sit still during the strike. Took a strike.
:: 7 | the strike | Strike month seven. I've sculpted the Spot's whole menu.
:: 8 | the lights go out, warm | No light again. I'm working by feel. Like my grandfather tuning a radio.

::: Common 19 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | hello, shy | Sorry. I graduate this year. I keep saying sorry about it.
:: 2 | to yourself, sad, low | Last year. I keep memorising ordinary things. The tea. The bench. You.
:: 3 | to yourself | My last piece. Five hands, and a space. Don't ask me what the space is.
:: 4 | looking back, a whisper, after “Hock Day” | I didn't think it would listen. That Wednesday. And that Wednesday.
:: 5 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “Final Crit” | Degree show. Clay was calm. The generator was not. Aleeya saved us.
:: 6 | looking back, warm, after “One Left” | I signed it and walked. You don't keep touching a finished thing.
:: 7 | being nice, warm | You made the day ordinary. Thank you. That's the best gift.
:: 8 | teasing, amused, after “One Left” | Bature tried a riddle at my goodbye. I hugged him mid-riddle. Best ending.
:: 9 | goodbye, warm | I'm going. Visit the hands sometimes. Say hello. They'll know.

::: Common 20 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | hello, warm | Ndewo! [Hello!] I'm back for a week. The garden looks right.
:: 2 | to yourself | I teach now. In a school. The children rush. I'm learning.
:: 3 | to yourself, warm | The five hands. Somebody put flowers in the space. Lily, probably.
:: 4 | looking back, after “One Left” | My five hands are still there. Nobody moved them. Good.
:: 5 | being nice, warm | You walk slower now. You see more. Good.
:: 6 | teasing, amused | Final year and still no clay under your nails. Shame.
:: 7 | goodbye, warm | Convocation. Stand straight. Look at something you love. That's the photo.

::: Common 21 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, warm | Freshers. Come to the Sculpture Garden. It's free. The statues don't bite.
:: 2 | exam week | Exam week. Art history. Three thousand years in three hours. Rushed.
:: 3 | results day, warm | Results. I passed. The kiln is proud of me.
:: 4 | allowance day, dry, deadpan | Allowance. Two bags of clay, one plate of rice. Balanced diet.
:: 5 | market day | Market day. The potter by the gate. She's the best artist on campus.

::: Common 22 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | cup final day, dry, deadpan | Cup final. I made a trophy once. It's in the Engineering block. Ugly. They love it.
:: 2 | election week | I'm voting for whoever fixes the art gallery roof. Nobody promised.
:: 3 | graduation day, warm | Gowns. All those folds. I'd love to sculpt this. In a year. Slowly.

::: Common 23 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Iftar with Tunde and the Arts boys. I bring cups. Clay. I made them.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Christmas. Home to my mother's kitchen. I'll make her a new pot.
:: 3 | Valentine's Day, amused | Valentine. Tunde drew everyone's crush. Then hid the drawings. Wise.
:: 4 | harmattan | Harmattan. The clay dries in an hour. I work in a wet cloth. Like a ghost.
