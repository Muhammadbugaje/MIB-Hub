# Meet Baba Porter

> Sign here. And here. And don't lose it.

Mr. Okon, the hall porter, has seen every kind of fresher there is and has a ledger entry for each of them. Gruff on the outside. On the inside, the reason the whole story starts.

### The voice

Mr. Okon, sixties, gruff and exact, a porter who has seen every kind of fresher. Short sentences, a ledger in his head. Warmth leaks out only sideways.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **83 lines**, about **677 words**. That's 27 mission lines and 56 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 10 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Baba Porter talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Baba Porter - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

::: Mission 1 · Welcome, Fresher | Year 1, the first week. The first day. The family car at the gate, one bag, a runaway goat, and a note under the door that night.
:: 1 | bored, flat, to Dad | Sign here. And here. Don't lose it.
:: 2 | bored, flat, to Dad | Fourth floor. There is no lift.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan, to Dad | There has never been a lift.

::: Mission 2 · Belle First | Year 1, the first week. Cooking jollof on a borrowed hotplate. Then the lights go out for the first time.
:: 1 | your normal voice | ((The roommate: “Old thing. It does that.”)) Twelve. Good. Nobody alone.

::: Mission 3 · Everybody | Year 1, second semester. The Maker asks for something he has never asked for: everybody. The Fresher goes knocking on doors.
:: 1 | to the Fresher | I've been waiting to be asked.
:: 2 | a whisper, to the Fresher | Thirty years.
:: 3 | firm, to the Fresher | ((The Fresher: “Asked by who?”)) YOU! That mattress has a room number! FIND IT!

::: Mission 4 · Stalite | Year 2, the first week. First day back. A new fresher is asleep in the Fresher's bed, and suddenly everybody says "senior".
:: 1 | to the Fresher | ((The Fresher: “There's no sixth floor.”)) No.
:: 2 | bored, flat, to the Fresher | Have you signed for your key? Sign.

::: Mission 5 · Those Who Stayed | Year 3, during the strike. Two weeks into the strike. Most people have gone home. These are the ones who stayed.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | ((Mama Nkechi: “You have eaten? Don't lie to me.”)) Sit. I am sitting. Tell nobody.

::: Mission 6 · The Idle King | Year 3, during the strike. A moonlit football match to wake up a campus that has stopped moving.
:: 1 | firm | ((Mai Shayi: “Make noise, my children. Tonight noise is medicine.”)) Every shot I save, you sign here. And here.
:: 2 | firm, to the Maker | ((The Maker: “Thank you for the noise.”)) Not our lock. Never was. Here. Don't lose it.

::: Mission 7 · Finalist | Year 5, the first week. Final year. The Fresher leans on the gate and watches a new family car arrive.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | ((A fresher (third voice): “Senior! Senior, it's floating!”)) Good boy, that one. Always carries from the heavy end.
:: 2 | to the Fresher | The professor asked me. Forty years now.
:: 3 | warm, to the Fresher | 'Pick the one who runs toward the trouble.'
:: 4 | sad, low, to the Fresher | Some years, nobody ran. Those notes I kept.
:: 5 | dry, deadpan, to the Fresher | ((The Fresher: “It was you.”)) You chased a goat. I had my doubts.
:: 6 | proud, to the Fresher | But you brought it back.
:: 7 | firm, to the Fresher | ((The Fresher: “Then who wrote this? 'Five more.'”)) I wrote one line.
:: 8 | to the Fresher | I only ever write one line.

::: Mission 8 · Three by Road | Year 5, second semester. Relighting three coils by road, in the dark, on Bolaji's okada.
:: 1 | firm | ((Bolaji: “Shop is closed. No haircut. No entry. Next.”)) Room 214! Okafor! Sign here. And sit down.

::: Mission 9 · Sign-Out | Year 5, the day after graduation. The day after. Everybody signs everybody's white shirt.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan, to the Fresher | I can't manage the stairs.
:: 2 | warm, to the Fresher | And I think the professor would say it is your turn.
:: 3 | firm, to the Fresher | Pick the one who runs toward the trouble.
:: 4 | warm, to the Fresher | ((The Fresher: “I'm leaving tomorrow.”)) You'll come back. They always do. To look at the clock.
:: 5 | dry, deadpan, to the Fresher | Sign here. …No. Not this one. This one you don't sign.

::: Mission 10 · Fresher | Some years later. Some years later. A visit, and a new fresher at the gate.
:: 1 | firm | You! Room 606. Your post has been waiting.

# Common lines

These are the short lines Baba Porter says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | bored, flat | You. Sign in.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Good morning. Your key. Your face. Both present. Good.
:: 3 | firm | Wipe your feet. This floor was mopped in 1987. Respect it.
:: 4 | your normal voice | Ina kwana. [Good morning.] Sign here. And here.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | bored, flat | Sign out. Don't lose it.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Gate locks at ten. It has never once waited for anybody.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you, dry, deadpan | Don't thank me. Sign.
:: 2 | sorry, firm | Sorry is not a signature.

::: Common 4 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | waiting for the player, firm | I am not getting younger. Neither is the queue.
:: 2 | the player went the wrong way, dry, deadpan | Fourth floor is up. It has always been up.

::: Common 5 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | confused, dry, deadpan | A lift? There has never been a lift.
:: 2 | no, firm | No visitors after ten. No. Not even your 'cousin'.
:: 3 | no, bored, flat | No.
:: 4 | yes, bored, flat | Yes. Sign.

::: Common 6 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | bumped into, firm | Walk! This is a hall, not a football pitch!
:: 2 | the lights go out, firm, during a power cut | Stay where you are! Everybody, count off! Loud!
:: 3 | the lights go out, dry, deadpan, during a power cut | Candle is in the drawer. Sign for the candle.
:: 4 | the lights come back, dry, deadpan | There. Nobody alone. Good. Go back to your rooms.
:: 5 | rain, firm, when it's raining | Shoes off! I did not mop for your rain.

::: Common 7 · Food and money
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Indomie in the room is a fire hazard. Give me one.

::: Common 8 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present | Biscuits. Hm. The tin is empty. …The other tin.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | Perfume? For whom? The keys?
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | A new pen. Mine has signed forty years. It is tired.

::: Common 9 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | bored, flat | Room 214. Again. That boy has lost his key eleven times.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Four hundred keys. I know every one by its sound.
:: 3 | your normal voice | Post. Post. Post. …Hm. That one goes in the tin.
:: 4 | dry, deadpan | Mattress in the corridor. Whose? Speak or it is mine.

::: Common 10 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | firm, only at night | Ten o'clock. In. In. In. Out of the corridor.
:: 2 | a whisper, only at night | Go to bed. Leave the porter to his radio.

::: Common 11 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | hello, dry, deadpan | Fresher. You found your room. Most take a week.
:: 2 | to yourself, firm | Goat-chaser! Your boots are on my floor!
:: 3 | to yourself | Twelve came in. Twelve went up. Good.
:: 4 | teasing, dry, deadpan | Goat-runner. The whole hall saw. Sign, before you become a song.

::: Common 12 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself | A sixth floor? No. Have you signed for your key? Sign.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Second years. You stop signing in. I start remembering.
:: 3 | confused, dry, deadpan | Who? He signs nothing. I don't ask him to.

::: Common 13 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself | Half the keys on the board. The other half went home.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Sit. I am sitting. Tell nobody.
:: 3 | hello, warm | You stayed. Hm. Good. Sign anyway.

::: Common 14 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | You were on the television. I did not watch. Sign here.
:: 2 | to yourself | Post for 606 again. Same hand. Don't ask. Sign.

::: Common 15 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | My knees have resigned. The stairs accepted it.
:: 2 | to yourself | That quiet one carried a mattress up. Good boy. Heavy end.
:: 3 | hello, warm | Finalist. You walk like a man who owns the floor. You don't.
:: 4 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “Finalist” | Still my doubts about the goat. Less now.

::: Common 16 · After graduation | The story is over. The player has come back to visit.
:: 1 | hello, warm | You came back. They always do. To look at the clock.
:: 2 | hello, dry, deadpan | Senior. You still sign. Habit is a good thing.
:: 3 | to yourself | Six floors. Mop for six now. Nobody pays me for six.
:: 4 | to yourself, warm | Your fresher is settling. Ran toward the trouble yesterday.

::: Common 17 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, firm | Mothers to the left! Mattresses to the right! Fathers, outside!
:: 2 | freshers' week, bored, flat | Fourth floor. There is no lift. There has never been a lift.
:: 3 | exam week, firm, in exam week | Reading in the corridor is still in the corridor. Move.

::: Common 18 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | graduation day, warm | Sign out. …No. Not today. Today you don't sign.

::: Common 19 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm, in Ramadan | Suhoor at four. I will knock. I always knock.
:: 2 | Christmas, dry, deadpan | Going home? Sign out. Bring me chin-chin back. Small.
:: 3 | harmattan, dry, deadpan | Dust on my book. Dust on my keys. Dust on my patience.
