# Meet Dr. Minus

> Good morning. Minus two.

Dr. Dangana, a Law lecturer who takes marks off the way other men clear their throats. He is not cruel. He was failed unfairly once, a long time ago, and decided that must be how standards work.

### The voice

Dr. Dangana, a Law lecturer in his sixties, precise, dry, deadpan. Deducts marks the way other men clear their throats. Never cruel; just counting. "Minus two."

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **59 lines**, about **521 words**. That's 16 mission lines and 43 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 6 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Dr. Minus talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Dr. Minus - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

::: Mission 1 · Point of Order | Year 2, first semester, test week. Nusaiba's moot court. Morning in the law library, afternoon in front of Dr. Minus.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan, to Jiya | Has our visiting fellow anything to add?
:: 2 | dry, deadpan, to Jiya | ((Jiya: “I had more points. They seemed unkind.”)) …Minus two. For the bench.
:: 3 | firm | ((Muktar: “Point of order: I would like to lose gracefully.”)) Minus two, Miss Nusaiba. For confidence.

::: Mission 2 · The Hearing | Year 2, second semester. A formal hearing on Senate paper. Somebody has told on the Fresher.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | ((Nusaiba: “The rules permit a friend. They do not say 'silent'.”)) They do not. I checked them last night.
:: 2 | your normal voice | The argument is sound.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | It was sound in my court in week seven. I marked it down.
:: 4 | your normal voice | For the student.

::: Mission 3 · Padlock Receipts | Year 3, after the strike. Who padlocked the lecture halls? Mubarak finds his own receipt.
:: 1 | firm | Page two. Minus one comma.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | It is competent. Do not let it go to your head.

::: Mission 4 · Manifesto | Year 4, first semester. Nusaiba runs for student president. Posters, a walkabout and a budget of 4,000 naira.
:: 1 | firm | I trust this hobby will not affect my course.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | ((Nusaiba: “…it is your course. I'm doing the practical.”)) Minus two.

::: Mission 5 · Election Day | Year 4, second semester. The election. A televised debate, polling day, and a recount.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan, to Nusaiba | Congratulations. Your examination is in May.

::: Mission 6 · The Missing Script | Year 5, first semester, test week. Nusaiba's exam script has vanished and she is marked absent.
:: 1 | your normal voice | ((The Dean of Law: “Dr. Dangana. Your account, please.”)) I marked it. It is the best script in nineteen years.
:: 2 | sad, low | I could not record that mark for one who argues with me.
:: 3 | sad, low | I was failed that way once. I called it standards.
:: 4 | firm | Plus two. For conduct.

# Common lines

These are the short lines Dr. Minus says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Good morning. You may proceed. No marks either way.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Ah. A student. Walking on my side of the corridor. Minus one.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | You greeted me first. Plus nothing. Manners are expected.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Go. Promptly. Promptness is unmarked, which is a mercy.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Good day. Do not run. Running is an argument with time.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you, dry, deadpan | Thank you. Plus half. I have rounded it down.
:: 2 | sorry | An apology. Noted. The deduction stands, but noted.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | That was competent. Do not let it go to your head.

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Your shirt is untucked. Not a deduction. An observation.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | You used 'basically'. Basically, minus one.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | Your handwriting has the confidence of a man at a buffet. Minus one.

::: Common 6 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes | Yes. Subject to conditions.
:: 2 | no, dry, deadpan | No. Objection overruled. By me.
:: 3 | confused, dry, deadpan | I do not follow. Neither, I suspect, do you.

::: Common 7 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | firm | A question? Minus two. Very well, ask it. It may be worth it.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Did you cite your source? A friend is not a source.

::: Common 8 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | bumped into, firm | Collision. Minus one. Each.
:: 2 | the lights go out, dry, deadpan | The light is gone. The deductions remain.
:: 3 | the lights come back, dry, deadpan | Light. Nobody cheer. Cheering is minus one.
:: 4 | rain, bored, flat | Rain. Wet scripts. I mark them anyway.

::: Common 9 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present | A red pen. Practical. Plus one. Do not tell anyone.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | Flowers. For me? Irrelevant to the course. Thank you.
:: 3 | loves the present, shy | An old casebook. First edition. I… thank you. Truly.

::: Common 10 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Page two. Minus one comma. Page three. Minus two commas.
:: 2 | bored, flat | Nineteen years. Every script says 'in a nutshell'. No nuts. No shells.
:: 3 | your normal voice | Standards are standards. Someone must keep them in a drawer.

::: Common 11 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Freshers who say 'objection' in the corridor. Minus two for drama.
:: 2 | teasing, dry, deadpan | Not a Law student? Then I cannot deduct. How restful for you.

::: Common 12 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “Point of Order” | Your friend the visiting fellow. The bench remembers.
:: 2 | to yourself, firm | Miss Nusaiba is confident again. I have prepared deductions.

::: Common 13 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike, dry, deadpan | The strike. Nobody to deduct. I deduct the pigeons.
:: 2 | the strike | I mark old scripts again. They still have the same commas.

::: Common 14 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | A video about me. Grainy. Minus two for lighting.
:: 2 | election week, dry, deadpan | A manifesto with no full stops. I have deducted it on principle.

::: Common 15 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, shy | Plus two. I have been practising. It is harder to say.
:: 2 | to yourself | The best script in nineteen years. I keep it in my drawer.
:: 3 | being nice, dry, deadpan | Good. Plus one. Do not make a habit of my generosity.

::: Common 16 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, dry, deadpan | Freshers. So many questions. So few marks to deduct from.
:: 2 | exam week, firm | Write in ink. Pencil is for people who intend to change.
:: 3 | exam week, dry, deadpan | Exam week. My red pen has been sharpened. Spiritually.
:: 4 | results day | The mark is the mark. What you do next is unmarked.

::: Common 17 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | graduation day, sad, low | They graduate. I have nobody to deduct from until September.

::: Common 18 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Valentine's Day, dry, deadpan | Red everywhere. Like my pen. Coincidence.
