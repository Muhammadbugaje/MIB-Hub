# Meet Security

> A torch, a whistle, and a notebook nobody has seen them write in.

Campus guards, gatemen and the night patrol. Tired, dry and secretly kind.

### The voice

Campus guards, gatemen and night patrol; tired, dry, secretly kind; a torch, a whistle and a notebook nobody has seen them write in.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **46 lines**, about **453 words**. Read straight through, it's **about 5 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. **More voices are welcome:** it's a crowd, so share the lines out among friends and family.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Security - Common 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Common lines

These are the short lines Security says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | your normal voice | Good evening. ID card, please. Smile is optional.
:: 2 | firm, only at night | Sannu. [Hello.] Where are you going at this hour?
:: 3 | warm | Ah, it's you. Pass. Greet your mother for me.

::: Common 2 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | firm | No ID, no entry. Not even with charm.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Don't argue with the barrier. It's older than you.

::: Common 3 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out, firm | Light don go! [Power's gone!] Torches up! Nobody runs, nobody shouts!
:: 2 | the lights go out | Blackout patrol. Keep to the lit path.
:: 3 | rain, dry, deadpan, when it's raining | The gatehouse is full of students who 'just stopped to greet'.

::: Common 4 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Who parked this keke here? Is this keke a student?
:: 2 | firm | No loitering. Walk with purpose. Any purpose.
:: 3 | firm | Gate closes at ten. Not ten-oh-five. Ten.
:: 4 | dry, deadpan | Your ID photo is from a different century.
:: 5 | bored, flat | The goat again. No ID. Nobody stops it. It's above the law.
:: 6 | dry, deadpan | Somebody's bucket is outside again. We now guard buckets.
:: 7 | bored, flat | Radio check. …Radio? Hello? Network. Of course.

::: Common 5 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | firm, only at night | After midnight, walk with a friend. Or a torch. Both is better.
:: 2 | a whisper, only at night | Yes, the noticeboard has new slips. No, I didn't see who.
:: 3 | only at night | The old man in the garden? We greet. That's all. Keep walking.
:: 4 | bored, flat, only at night | Cats in a circle again. Not my department.
:: 5 | dry, deadpan, only at night | Ghost okada? I don't know what it is. I know you should go home.

::: Common 6 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself | The light is going more this year. My torch is my best friend.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Freshers keep asking me the way. I'm security, not Google.

::: Common 7 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Mall security has uniforms. We have whistles and wisdom.
:: 2 | to yourself, firm | Somebody is cutting cables at night. If you hear something, tell us.
:: 3 | to yourself, amused | I chased a cable thief. Lost him at the mall. The mall never blinks. I blinked.

::: Common 8 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself, bored, flat | Strike. Empty halls. I'm mostly guarding lizards.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | Who's still on campus? Let me write your name. For company.
:: 3 | to yourself, a whisper, during the strike | Quiet nights. Too quiet. I've started greeting trees.

::: Common 9 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | Election week. Shout all you like. Nobody fights. Keep it so.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Don't believe every video. I was in one. I was eating.
:: 3 | to yourself, bored, flat | Posters on the gate again. Down they come.

::: Common 10 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Five years I check your ID. Today I don't need to look.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | Final year, eh? Don't forget us at the gate.

::: Common 11 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week | Freshers, follow the signs. The signs are correct. Mostly.
:: 2 | exam week, firm | Exam hall: phones off, IDs out, mouths closed.
:: 3 | results day, firm | Results crush! One at a time at the noticeboard! One!
:: 4 | allowance day, firm | ATM queue: no pushing. The machine doesn't care who's first.
:: 5 | market day, sharp, quick | Market day. Phone in your front pocket. Back pocket is a donation.

::: Common 12 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike | Strike or no strike, the gate opens at six.
:: 2 | cup final day, dry, deadpan | Stadium rule: no drums in the trophy room. Why do I have to say this?
:: 3 | election week, firm | Campaigning stops at the gate. The gate is neutral.
:: 4 | graduation day, bored, flat | Convocation. Ten lost aunties. All looking for 'Ibrahim'.

::: Common 13 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Ramadan Kareem! [Blessed Ramadan!] Iftar crowd at six. I'll be at the gate.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Merry Christmas! Travellers, sign out at the gate.
:: 3 | Valentine's Day, dry, deadpan | Valentine's. More bouquets than students. Check the flowers too.
:: 4 | harmattan, dry, deadpan | Harmattan haze. If you can't see me, I can still see you.
