# Meet Dr. Khadija

> My office. Now. Bring yourself.

The level adviser, later head of department. Stern, unhurried and, though she would never say so, entirely on your side. Her sentences land like rubber stamps.

### The voice

A northern woman in her fifties, level adviser and later HOD. Stern, unhurried, on your side. Short sentences that land like stamps. "My office. Now. Bring yourself."

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **62 lines**, about **547 words**. That's 17 mission lines and 45 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 7 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Dr. Khadija talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Dr. Khadija - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

::: Mission 1 · Results Day | Year 1, second semester. Results day. A sheet of paper, a box of pins, and a phone call home.
:: 1 | firm | (name). I know your name now.
:: 2 | your normal voice | I will be watching you. In the good sense.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | For now.

::: Mission 2 · Test Week Twist | Year 2, first semester, test week. Three late tests, and an official warning letter from the department.
:: 1 | firm, to the Fresher | The mall basement. After closing. Week nine.
:: 2 | to the Fresher | I was told by someone who would know.
:: 3 | firm, to the Fresher | A warning. Security will be less patient with you.
:: 4 | to the Fresher | Eleven years. I know stupid. I know necessary.
:: 5 | firm, to the Fresher | Whatever you are doing: do it where I can't be asked.
:: 6 | to the Fresher | Because if I am asked, I will answer.

::: Mission 3 · Log Off | Year 4, second semester, exam time. Defending the work-placement logbook in front of a panel.
:: 1 | firm | 'Tuesday: learned a great deal.'
:: 2 | firm | What did you learn?
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | ((The Fresher: “…A great deal, ma.”)) Minus. I am borrowing that from a colleague.
:: 4 | firm, to Engr. Aliyu | Engineer. Can this student be trusted at a live feeder?
:: 5 | to Engr. Aliyu | ((Engr. Aliyu: “I was not. This one is.”)) I do not believe in suspense. Your grade.
:: 6 | warm, to Engr. Aliyu | My office is open. For once, bring nothing.

::: Mission 4 · Project Defence | Year 5, second semester. The project defence. Forty slides, of which eleven are allowed.
:: 1 | firm | ((The Fresher: “They're lit, sir.”)) Begin. And bring yourself. All of yourself.

# Common lines

These are the short lines Dr. Khadija says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | firm | (name). I know your name. That is not always good news.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Ina wuni. [Good afternoon.] Are your courses registered?
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | You greeted first. Good. Manners are on the transcript.
:: 4 | your normal voice | Walk with me. Briefly. I have eleven files and one tea.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | firm | Go. And bring yourself to my office before week six.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Sai anjima. [See you later.] Read.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you | Thank you. Noted. In the good column.
:: 2 | sorry, firm | Sorry is a start. A timetable is the rest of the sentence.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | proud | Your GP went up. I am not smiling. This is my thinking face.

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Your adviser's form says 'adviser: me'. In capital letters. Bold.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Late again. Is your hostel moving further away each week?

::: Common 6 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes | Yes. Signed. Don't make me regret the ink.
:: 2 | no, firm | No. Bring yourself, not excuses.
:: 3 | no, dry, deadpan | No. And before you ask again: also no.
:: 4 | maybe | Perhaps. Come back with a form, not a feeling.

::: Common 7 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | firm | Your carry-over. Is it registered? Don't say 'I think'.
:: 2 | your normal voice | How many units are you carrying? And how many are carrying you?

::: Common 8 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | bumped into, firm | Look where you walk. I have no form for this.
:: 2 | the lights go out, dry, deadpan | Light gone. The files are still here. Unfortunately.
:: 3 | a hot day, bored, flat | This fan is turning out of courtesy only.

::: Common 9 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | Dates. Thank you. This does not change your grade.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | A keyring. I have keys. Thank you.
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | A good flask. Tea that stays hot through a whole meeting. A miracle.

::: Common 10 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | bored, flat | Seventeen students. Seventeen versions of 'the portal said'.
:: 2 | firm | Who signed my name on this? My signature has never been this happy.

::: Common 11 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself | Freshers. Everyone wants to change course in week two.
:: 2 | a question, firm | You've met your adviser? Good. It's me. Sit.
:: 3 | teasing, dry, deadpan | Results came. You lived. Now let us improve on living.

::: Common 12 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | Week nine. The mall basement. Somebody always ends up in my office.
:: 2 | looking back, firm, after “The Hearing” | After the hearing: you, quiet, for one semester. Can you?
:: 3 | refusing, firm | I will not be asked. So don't make me be asked.

::: Common 13 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike | Strike or no strike, my door is open. The office is just quieter.
:: 2 | the strike, firm | Read anyway. The exam does not join unions.

::: Common 14 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | HOD. Same chair, more forms. They gave me a bigger stamp.
:: 2 | to yourself | Rumours come to my office too. I file them under 'later'.

::: Common 15 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Final year. My office is open. For once, bring nothing.
:: 2 | to yourself, firm | Clearance forms. All of them. Yes, the blue one too.
:: 3 | being nice, warm | You brought yourself. All of yourself. I noticed.

::: Common 16 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, firm | Freshers! Course forms by Friday. Not Friday night. Friday.
:: 2 | exam week, firm | Exam slip. Ink pen. Your own body, present. Go.
:: 3 | results day | Results day. Whatever it says, my office after. Bring yourself.
:: 4 | results day, warm | Good result. Enjoy today. Tomorrow we plan.

::: Common 17 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | election week, dry, deadpan | Campaign posters on my noticeboard. Again. I'm keeping score.
:: 2 | graduation day, proud | My students. In gowns. Nobody look at me for one minute.

::: Common 18 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan | Office closes early this month. Plan accordingly. Ramadan Kareem.
