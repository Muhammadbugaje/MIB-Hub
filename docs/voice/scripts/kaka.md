# Meet Kaka

> Sit. Eat first. She already knows why you came.

The forest ranger's grandmother, very old, shelling groundnuts where the road ends and the trees begin. She sees everything and mentions almost none of it.

### The voice

Ranger Lawal's grandmother, very old, sitting at the forest edge shelling groundnuts. Slow, dry, unshockable; mostly Hausa, short English. Sees everything and remarks on little of it.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **56 lines**, about **479 words**. That's 11 mission lines and 45 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 6 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Kaka talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Kaka - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Kaka says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | Sit. Eat first.
:: 2 | warm | Ina wuni. [Good afternoon.] Sit. Gyada. [Groundnuts.]
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | You again. Your legs brought you or your stomach?
:: 4 | warm | Yauwa. [Good.] You greeted an old woman. Sit.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Go. Take a handful. For the road.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Sai anjima. [See you later.] If I am still here. I will be.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you, dry, deadpan | Thank me by eating.
:: 2 | sorry, dry, deadpan | Sorry for what? Eat. Sorry later.

::: Common 4 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | amused | So thin. Do they feed you in that school, or only teach you?

::: Common 5 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | refusing, firm | You are not hungry? Wawa. [Fool.] Everybody is hungry.
:: 2 | confused, dry, deadpan | You and your wires. I don't understand. Eat.

::: Common 6 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | warm | Your mother. She is well? Call her.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | You young people. Where are you rushing? The forest is not moving.

::: Common 7 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | rain, dry, deadpan, when it's raining | Rain is coming. My knee said so this morning.
:: 2 | a hot day, dry, deadpan, on a hot day | Hot? I was born in hotter. Sit in the shade, child.
:: 3 | the lights go out, amused, during a power cut | Light has gone? Here it never came. We are fine.
:: 4 | bumped into, firm | Kai! [Hey!] My groundnuts! Pick them. Every one.

::: Common 8 · Food and money
:: 1 | warm | Groundnuts. Roasted this morning. Don't count, just take.
:: 2 | firm | I made tuwo. You will eat tuwo.

::: Common 9 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | Kola nut. Ah. You were raised well.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | A phone? For who? The trees?
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | A new mat. Allah ya saka. [God reward you.] Sit on it first.

::: Common 10 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | your normal voice | Shell, shell, shell. Eighty years. Still shelling.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | The forest was a school once. Now it is a forest. Hm.
:: 3 | amused | My grandson is somewhere, being stubborn. As usual.
:: 4 | your normal voice | Somebody already ate half my groundnuts today. Good.

::: Common 11 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | firm, only at night | Dark. Go home. The forest is not for night walking.

::: Common 12 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | hello, warm | The school closed and you came to the forest. Sensible. Eat.
:: 2 | to yourself | That light in the trees. Old. Older than my grandson.
:: 3 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Young people in white coats. Always hungry. Always were.
:: 4 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “Ranger Lawal's Debt” | My grandson owes you now. He hates owing. Good for him.

::: Common 13 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself | Lawal greets the trees. I greet Lawal. The trees answer first.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | The forest is quieter. Whatever you did, do it again.
:: 3 | confused | That one? He hasn't finished yet. Eat.

::: Common 14 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Last year? Then come more. An old woman can count.
:: 2 | to yourself, amused | The postman asks me for everybody now. I am the old woman.
:: 3 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “Three by Road” | It looked at my pan. It went. Sensible.

::: Common 15 · After graduation | The story is over. The player has come back to visit.
:: 1 | hello, warm | You finished. Good. Not everybody does. Eat.
:: 2 | to yourself | Nobody passes in the mornings now. Hm. I keep a handful anyway.
:: 3 | to yourself, amused | Clock bell. All the way out here. Even the goats look up.

::: Common 16 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | market day, amused | Market day. Lawal will bring me sugar. And complaints.

::: Common 17 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | the strike, dry, deadpan, during the strike | Strike? In my day we walked to school. It never went on strike.

::: Common 18 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm, in Ramadan | Ramadan Kareem. Kunu at sunset. Come back then.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Your Christmas people are going home. Take groundnuts for them.
:: 3 | harmattan, warm | Harmattan. Shea butter on your lips. Now. Before you crack.
