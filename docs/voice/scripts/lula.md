# Meet Lula

> Turn left. No. The other left. Emotionally.

Retired, proud, and the storyteller half of the funniest married couple in the game. He gives directions with total confidence and the wrong hand, and tells every story to his wife Faty as much as to you.

### The voice

Sixties, retired, Faty's husband: deep, proud, a storyteller who gives directions with total confidence and the wrong hand. "I mean left. Emotionally." Every story is told to Faty as much as to you.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **58 lines**, about **619 words**. That's 8 mission lines and 50 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 7 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Lula talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Lula - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

::: Mission 1 · Keke Grand Prix | Year 1, second semester. The keke (three-wheeled taxi) driving test, with a married couple arguing in the back seat.
:: 1 | proud | ((The keke examiner: “I will follow. On foot.”)) Driver! Motor park. Turn left.
:: 2 | proud | ((Faty: “He means right.”)) I mean left. Emotionally.
:: 3 | your normal voice | ((The Fresher: “And nine?”)) Only one person ever laughed at page nine.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Lula says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | proud | Ah! The young driver! Turn left to greet me. Emotionally.
:: 2 | warm | Barka da yamma! [Good evening!] Faty, look who it is.
:: 3 | amused | You again. Sit. I have a story. Faty, which story?

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Go well. Turn left at the gate. I mean right. Ask Faty.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | proud | Thank you. Faty, write down that somebody thanked me.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | proud | You drive like I did. Before the ram. After the imam.

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | amused | You call that a joke? Faty, is it a joke? …She says no.

::: Common 6 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | the player went the wrong way, proud | Left. …Not that left. The other one. The emotional one.
:: 2 | waiting for the player, amused | Take your time. I took twenty years to find the market. Faty found it first.
:: 3 | walking along, proud | Follow me. I know a shortcut. …Faty, where is the shortcut?

::: Common 7 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | confused | Pardon? Faty hears for both of us. Faty?
:: 2 | confused, dry, deadpan | Who? There was nobody there fa. Only Faty, and she's always there.
:: 3 | yes, proud | Yes! Faty, I said yes. Write it down before I change.
:: 4 | no, dry, deadpan | No. Faty says no. So, no. Emotionally, maybe.

::: Common 8 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | a question | Have you written anything down today? Write it down. It goes.
:: 2 | a joke, amused | A man asks for directions. His wife gives them. That's marriage.

::: Common 9 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | a hot day | Heat. In my day we had the same heat. Fewer fans. More stories.
:: 2 | rain | Rain. Faty says it will stop. It will stop when Faty says.

::: Common 10 · Food and money
:: 1 | warm | Mama Nkechi's tuwo. Forty years I've eaten it. Forty years she says 'Again?'

::: Common 11 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | For us? Faty, a gift! Write it down. Page… not nine. Somewhere.
:: 2 | loves the present, proud | A pen! A good pen! For the book! Faty, we have a pen now!

::: Common 12 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | amused | Faty, which of us said that? …Me? Good.
:: 2 | proud | Forty-one years married. Forty jokes. One a year. We're behind.

::: Common 13 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | warm | At night we read the book. Out loud. Some pages twice.

::: Common 14 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | looking back, proud, after “Keke Grand Prix” | Turn left. Emotionally. Still the best thing I ever said.
:: 2 | looking back, amused, after “Keke Grand Prix” | You won the Grand Prix. Or you stopped to help. Faty wrote down which.
:: 3 | to yourself | Page nine? Ask Faty. …Don't ask Faty.
:: 4 | teasing, amused | You still drive like the trophy is chasing you.

::: Common 15 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, proud, after “Sign-Out, Small” | 'Left. Emotionally.' Faty put it on my back. In chalk. I wore it to the market.
:: 2 | looking back, amused, after “Sign-Out, Small” | The sign-out party. Somebody wrote on my back. I know it was Faty.
:: 3 | being nice, warm | You still visit old people. Good. We have the best gist.

::: Common 16 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | looking back, amused, after “The Idle King” | At our wedding the ram escaped. The imam caught it. I told it on stage!
:: 2 | to yourself, proud | The open mic. I'm famous now. Faty's mother would be shocked.
:: 3 | the strike | Strike. The young ones left. We stayed. We always stay.

::: Common 17 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself | This year's joke? Not yet. Faty is very strict.
:: 2 | teasing, amused | Still single? Faty and I met at a bus stop. Wrong bus. Right wife.
:: 3 | being nice, warm | You have grown. Faty says so. I say so. Twice is a fact.
:: 4 | market day, amused | Market day. Faty bargains. I carry. I also bargain. Badly.

::: Common 18 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | looking back, a whisper, after “The Maker's Graduation” | That night, Faty made me write the date. My hand shook. It's in.
:: 2 | looking back, proud, after “Sign-Out” | We were there. It's in the book. In ink. Faty doesn't use ink.
:: 3 | to yourself, a whisper | The book is nearly full. We'll start another. On page one. Not nine.
:: 4 | goodbye, warm | Convocation. Turn left at the stadium. Faty, tell them which left.

::: Common 19 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, amused | Freshers! Every year they look younger. Faty says we look older.

::: Common 20 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | cup final day, excited | Goal! Faty, write it! …She says a goal is not a joke. It is today.
:: 2 | election week, proud | Elections. I vote for whoever Faty votes for. Then I pretend I chose.
:: 3 | graduation day, warm | Convocation. Gowns everywhere. We'll write your name in the book. Not page nine.

::: Common 21 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Ramadan. Faty breaks fast with dates. I break fast with tea. Then dates.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Christmas. Our neighbours bring rice. We bring a joke. Fair trade.
:: 3 | Valentine's Day, proud | Valentine's. I bought Faty a pen. Forty-one years. She still uses it.
:: 4 | harmattan | Harmattan. My knees know before the radio does.
