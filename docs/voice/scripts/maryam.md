# Meet Maryam

> Judges you by your bookshelf. Offers yam anyway.

An English student who works at the campus bookshop. Thoughtful, dry and teasing. She reads old things aloud in a soft reading voice, and offers yam in every situation, including emergencies.

### The voice

Early twenties, English, works at the campus bookshop. Thoughtful, dry, teasing; reads old things aloud in a soft "reading voice", then drops back into her own. Judges people by their shelves. Offers yam in every situation, including emergencies.

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **56 lines**, about **612 words**. That's 5 mission lines and 51 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 7 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Maryam talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Maryam - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Maryam says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | warm | Have you read anything good? No? Come in. Sit. Yam?
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Hello. What's on your shelf? Be honest. Textbooks don't count.
:: 3 | warm | Sannu. [Hello.] I saved you a book. And some yam. Separately.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | warm | Go. Read on the shuttle. It makes the road shorter.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | warm | Thank you. That deserves a bookmark. Here.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | warm | You returned it on time. With no coffee rings. Marry a librarian.

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | amused | Your shelf has one novel. It's a phone charger box.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | You read the last page first. I can tell. It's in your face.

::: Common 6 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | confused | Pardon? I was in chapter nine. Say it to chapter one.
:: 2 | confused, dry, deadpan | Who? There was nobody there fa. You've been reading too many novels.
:: 3 | refusing, dry, deadpan | No. I don't lend that one. It came back with jollof on page twelve once.

::: Common 7 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | a question | Have you read… anything? This year? A menu counts. Barely.
:: 2 | a joke, amused | Why did the book join the gym? To work on its spine. Yam?

::: Common 8 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | bumped into, amused | Careful. That's a first edition. You're a later edition. Fine.
:: 2 | the lights go out, a whisper | 'It was a dark and stormy night…' Light's gone. Perfect timing. Yam?
:: 3 | rain, warm | Rain on the bookshop roof. Best sound on campus. Stay.

::: Common 9 · Food and money
:: 1 | warm | Roasted yam. Hot. From the woman by the gate. Have half. Have all.

::: Common 10 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | A bookmark! Thank you. My books thank you. They were lonely.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | An e-reader. Thank you. I'll use it as a coaster.
:: 3 | loves the present, excited | An old book! Look at the binding! Smell it! …Don't tell anyone I sniffed it.

::: Common 11 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | a whisper | '…and the rains came early that year, and nobody was ready.'
:: 2 | your normal voice | Biography. Poetry. Yam recipes. That's a shelf.

::: Common 12 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | a whisper | 'The library at night keeps its own counsel.' Somebody wrote that. I'll find who.

::: Common 13 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | hello, warm | Fresher! What are you reading? Your timetable doesn't count.
:: 2 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | First years buy the textbook, then sell it back unopened. Every year.
:: 3 | looking back, amused, after “Matric Fit” | You matriculated in a gown made for two. I put it in my notebook.
:: 4 | teasing, amused | Your first essay. 'In conclusion' three times. Choose one conclusion.

::: Common 14 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, a whisper, after “On Air” | 'A humming room under the meat hall.' I still read it at night.
:: 2 | looking back, warm, after “On Air” | The archive. Three departments, one story. We found it with dust and patience.
:: 3 | being nice, warm | You read the ledger like a novel. That's a skill. Nobody has it.
:: 4 | teasing, dry, deadpan | You've started a business. And a radio show. And stopped reading. I noticed.

::: Common 15 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | to yourself | Strike. Bookshop open. One customer. Me. I'm buying everything.
:: 2 | looking back, a whisper, after “The Old Site” | The tape machine. Her voice. Have you read anything that brave? I haven't.
:: 3 | looking back, warm, after “The Old Site” | The tape machine was under my bed, behind the yam sack. Glad I keep both.
:: 4 | the strike, dry, deadpan | Strike month six. I've reread every book. Even the bad ones. Especially.

::: Common 16 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself | Cook-off. I entered yam. Only yam. It's a statement.
:: 2 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “Cook-Off” | Nobody judged the yam. I waited. I'm still waiting.
:: 3 | being nice, warm | You checked before you shared. Like a good reader. Like an editor.
:: 4 | teasing, amused, after “Double Booking” | Your column in the twins' chart. I read it. Twice. It needs editing.

::: Common 17 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, proud | My book. Printed in the market. Two hundred copies. Forty sold. All to Mum.
:: 2 | to yourself, a whisper | 'Thank you for the lamp.' Off the night noticeboard. No name. I kept it.
:: 3 | looking back, a whisper, after “The Maker's Notebook” | The notebook. Everyone was so quiet. I offered yam. It helped.
:: 4 | being nice, warm | You'll be in my next book. Kindly. Mostly kindly.
:: 5 | goodbye, warm | Convocation. Keep a book in your bag. Always. Yam is optional.

::: Common 18 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, warm | Freshers! Bookshop sale! Second-hand textbooks with first-hand notes!
:: 2 | exam week, dry, deadpan | Exam week. Everyone wants the book I've had on the shelf for a year.
:: 3 | market day, excited | Market day! The book man by the tailor has poetry. Old poetry. I'm going!

::: Common 19 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | graduation day, warm | Convocation. Gowns everywhere. Like a library came to life.

::: Common 20 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Iftar. I'm bringing yam. I'm always bringing yam.
:: 2 | Christmas, warm | Christmas break. Shop shut. I've taken six books home. And yam.
:: 3 | harmattan | Harmattan. The old pages crack. I wrap them in cloth. Like babies.
