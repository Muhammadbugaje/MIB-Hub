# Meet Ranger Lawal

> Greets every tree by name. Tells every student to go back.

The forest ranger, as stubborn as a baobab. Students call him "Baba Ranger", which he hates, mostly. He starts the story saying "Go back." He ends it saying "Follow my light."

### The voice

Forest ranger, quiet northern man in his thirties, stubborn as a baobab. Greets trees by name. Few words, low voice. Hates "Baba Ranger", mostly. "Go back." Later: "Follow my light."

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **51 lines**, about **417 words**. That's 9 mission lines and 42 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 5 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Ranger Lawal talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Ranger Lawal - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

## Common lines

These are the short lines Ranger Lawal says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | firm | Go back.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Sannu. [Hello.] You greeted the trees? Greet them.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | You again. The forest has noticed you. So have I.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | your normal voice | Go home before dark. The path is shorter in daylight.
:: 2 | warm | Sai wata rana. [Until another day.] Greet Kaka if you pass.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | your normal voice | Don't thank me. Thank the path. It held.

::: Common 4 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | You called me Baba Ranger. I heard. The trees heard.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Loud shoes. Every squirrel in Dajin Kwari knows your name.

::: Common 5 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | walking along | Follow my light.
:: 2 | walking along, a whisper | Step where I step. Not near. Where.
:: 3 | waiting for the player, firm | Slow is not a problem. Lost is a problem. Keep up.
:: 4 | the player went the wrong way, firm | Not that way. That way is the way back to that way.

::: Common 6 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes | Yes.
:: 2 | no, firm | No.
:: 3 | refusing, firm | I did not show you this.
:: 4 | confused | Hm. Say it the way you would say it to a tree.

::: Common 7 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | your normal voice | Did you bring water? The forest doesn't sell any.

::: Common 8 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | bumped into | Sit. Breathe. The root didn't mean it.
:: 2 | rain, warm | Rain. The trees are drinking. Be quiet for them.
:: 3 | a hot day | Sun? Under the neem it's ten degrees kinder.

::: Common 9 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | Groundnuts from Kaka? …She sent you? Then I must eat them.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, firm | Plastic. In the forest. Take it back with you.
:: 3 | loves the present, shy | A seedling. Iroko. …I'll plant it where my father planted his.

::: Common 10 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | warm | Sannu, iroko. Good evening, neem.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Somebody cut a branch. Not wind. A cutlass. I'll find them.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | Baba Ranger. Baba. I'm thirty-four.

::: Common 11 · Late at night | After dark. Quieter.
:: 1 | a whisper | The forest is cold tonight. Colder than it should be. Walk.
:: 2 | a whisper | Cold patch ahead. Not ours to fix tonight. Follow my light.

::: Common 12 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | Students at the dam at midnight. Every year. Go back.
:: 2 | looking back, after “The Dam at Midnight” | That night at the dam. I did not see you. You did not see me.

::: Common 13 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself | The forest is quieter since the mall lights. Birds don't like it.

::: Common 14 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike, warm | Strike. Students come to the forest now. Some even greet the trees.
:: 2 | to yourself, a whisper | The Old Site. You know the way now. Don't make me regret it.
:: 3 | to yourself | Kaka says you ate first. Good. She likes you. Bad for me.

::: Common 15 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | No filming the forest. The trees didn't agree to it.

::: Common 16 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, warm | Follow my light. Last time, maybe. Then follow your own.
:: 2 | to yourself, shy | You can call me Baba Ranger. Once. Today only.

::: Common 17 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, firm | New faces at the forest edge. Go back. All of you. Go back.
:: 2 | market day, dry, deadpan | Market day. Someone is selling 'forest honey'. It's sugar.

::: Common 18 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Ramadan, warm | Iftar at Kaka's. Come. She cooks for twenty and expects thirty.
:: 2 | Christmas | Nobody cuts a pine in my forest. We have no pines. Still: nobody.
:: 3 | harmattan, firm | Harmattan. Dry leaves. No fires near the forest. None.
