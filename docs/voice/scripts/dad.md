# Meet Dad

> Sends money. Attaches a lecture. Means "I'm proud of you."

The player's father, a retired accountant, heard mostly on the phone. Every transfer comes with a speech that begins "When I was your age". He is certain this is not the main gate. It is the main gate.

### The voice

The player's father, late fifties, heard on the phone. Slow, measured, a retired-accountant calm; money always comes with a lecture, pride comes out sideways. Never says "my son" or "my daughter". "When I was your age…"

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **85 lines**, about **842 words**. That's 15 mission lines and 70 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 10 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Dad talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Dad - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Mission lines

In story order. Each coloured bar is one mission: its name, when it happens and what is going on. One recording per bar.

::: Mission 1 · Welcome, Fresher | Year 1, the first week. The first day. The family car at the gate, one bag, a runaway goat, and a note under the door that night.
:: 1 | firm | This is not the main gate.
:: 2 | proud | ((Mum: “Eat. Pray. Call me.”)) When I was your age I trekked to school.
:: 3 | a whisper, to Baba Porter | ((Baba Porter: “There has never been a lift.”)) Manage it.

::: Mission 2 · Results Day | Year 1, second semester. Results day. A sheet of paper, a box of pins, and a phone call home.
:: 1 | your normal voice | I am not angry.
:: 2 | firm | I am only calculating.

::: Mission 3 · Those Who Stayed | Year 3, during the strike. Two weeks into the strike. Most people have gone home. These are the ones who stayed.
:: 1 | your normal voice | We are managing. You manage too.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Greet your roommate. Your mother is fine.

::: Mission 4 · Payday | Year 4, second semester. The first real pay day: sixty thousand naira, and three ways to spend it.

:: 3 | warm, to the Fresher | We managed. You have also managed.
:: 4 | firm, to the Fresher | Have you eaten? Don't spend the rest on shoes.

::: Mission 5 · Gown | Year 5, after exams. Final results, and graduation photographs.

# Common lines

These are the short lines Dad says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | your normal voice | Hello. It's your father. Your mother is next to me. She says eat.
:: 2 | firm | (name). Good evening. I have three questions.
:: 3 | your normal voice | Ah. You picked up. On the first ring. What do you need?
:: 4 | warm | Hello. I'm calling for no reason. Your mother made me.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | your normal voice | Greet your roommate. Your mother is fine.
:: 2 | firm | Read your books. Balance your books. Both kinds.
:: 3 | your normal voice | Okay. This call is costing money. Goodnight.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | thank you, shy | …Hm. Thank you. I'll tell your mother you said it first.
:: 2 | sorry, firm | Sorry is good. A plan is better. What is the plan?

::: Common 4 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | proud | When I was your age I trekked to school. Both ways. Uphill.
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Shoes again? How many feet do you have now?

::: Common 5 · Walking with the player | Keeping up, waiting, and when the player goes the wrong way.
:: 1 | firm | Hello? Hello? Are you there? I'm paying for this silence.

::: Common 6 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes | Yes. Manage it.
:: 2 | no, firm | No. A want is not a need. Write that on your wall.
:: 3 | maybe | Let me think about it. That means no, but politely.
:: 4 | confused, firm | What is 'sapa'? …Ah. I see. We called it 'month end'.
:: 5 | agreeing | Correct. Finally, a sensible sentence. I'll write it down.

::: Common 7 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | a question, firm | How much is left? Don't round. Give me the naira and the kobo.
:: 2 | a question | Have you eaten? Your mother asked me to ask. I'm asking.
:: 3 | a question, firm | What did you buy? Itemise it. I'm holding a pen.
:: 4 | a joke, dry, deadpan | Your mother says I'm too strict. I've calculated. I'm exactly strict.

::: Common 8 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | the lights go out | No light? When I was your age, light was a rumour. Read by day.
:: 2 | rain, firm | Raining? Don't stand under a tree. Or a transformer. Read inside.
:: 3 | a hot day | Hot there? Then read early, before the sun gets ideas.

::: Common 9 · Food and money
:: 1 | money | I am not angry. I am only calculating.
:: 2 | money, firm | Money is a guest. If you don't respect it, it leaves early.
:: 3 | money, a whisper | Manage it.
:: 4 | food, firm | Cook. Don't buy every meal. A pot of rice is a week of peace.

::: Common 10 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, shy | A tie. …It's a good tie. I'll wear it on Monday. Or not. Yes. Monday.
:: 2 | loves the present, shy | From your own work? …I will tell your mother it came from you.

::: Common 11 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | firm | When I was your age… I'm starting again. When I was your age…
:: 2 | your normal voice | Your little one wants your old bicycle. I said ask you. Ask you.

::: Common 12 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | That was not the main gate. I know it was the only gate. Still.
:: 2 | to yourself | Did you register your courses? All of them? Show me the slip.
:: 3 | a question | Your adviser. What's the name? I want to know who is watching you.
:: 4 | results day, after good results | I am not angry. I am only calculating. …Good result. Hm. Good.
:: 5 | results day, firm, after bad results | One carry-over. When I was your age, we called it a warning.
:: 6 | results day, a whisper, after good results | …Well done. Don't tell your mother I said it first.
:: 7 | allowance day, firm | Allowance sent. Food first, books second, nonsense never.
:: 8 | allowance day | That is for the whole month. Not for the first weekend.

::: Common 13 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | Your friend has a shop. Does he keep a ledger? Ask him. Ask him.
:: 2 | to yourself | A mall with its own power. Who is paying for that power? Think.
:: 3 | results day, proud, after good results | Up from last year. I've plotted it. The line is going up.
:: 4 | results day, firm, after bad results | Down. I've plotted it. I don't like this line.
:: 5 | allowance day, firm | Allowance sent. Last month you spent it in nine days. Make it ten.
:: 6 | allowance day, dry, deadpan | You asked for 'small extra'. Define small. Define extra.

::: Common 14 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike | We are managing. You manage too.
:: 2 | the strike, firm | Strike or no strike, read. The exam will come back. Exams always do.
:: 3 | to yourself, shy | I'm reading your textbook. Chapter one. Your field is… brave.
:: 4 | to yourself | You're doing small jobs? Good. Work is not shame. Idleness is.
:: 5 | allowance day, sad, low | It's less this month. Things are tight. I'm sorry. Manage it.

::: Common 15 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, firm | You're on the internet. Is the internet paying your fees?
:: 2 | to yourself | Reputation is like a ledger. Every entry stays.
:: 3 | results day, proud, after good results | Good. I told my colleagues. Twice. They've asked me to stop.
:: 4 | results day, after bad results | One more year to fix it. That's enough time. I've calculated.
:: 5 | allowance day, dry, deadpan | You sent money home? …I'm returning it. With a lecture attached.

::: Common 16 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself | Final year. Your project. Is it finished? Is it on time? Is it safe?
:: 2 | to yourself, shy | We managed. You have also managed.
:: 3 | results day, shy | …Well done. Take your mother. She's crying. I'm not. Network.
:: 4 | graduation day, firm | Five years ago I parked here! Ask him! Ask him!
:: 5 | graduation day, excited | Chin up! Smile! Wait, which one is you? You all look the same!
:: 6 | allowance day, a whisper | Last one. After this, the allowance is you. …Manage it.

::: Common 17 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week | New freshers? Show them the way. Show them the bank queue too.
:: 2 | exam week, firm | Exams. Switch off that phone. After this call. Not during.
:: 3 | exam week | Read the question twice. I always told you. You never listened.
:: 4 | results day, firm | When I was your age, results were pinned with a nail.
:: 5 | allowance day | Check your account. Then check your spending. In that order.

::: Common 18 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | cup final day, proud | Your faculty won? When I was your age I scored once. Just once.

::: Common 19 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | Christmas | Christmas break. The bus fare is sent. The bus fare only.
:: 2 | harmattan, firm | Harmattan. Close your window. Dust is not free cleaning.
