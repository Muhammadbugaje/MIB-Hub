# Meet Dr. Okonkwo

> Page forty. Nobody ever failed for being brief.

The Electrical Engineering lecturer who becomes the player's project supervisor. Brisk, fair and funnier than she lets anyone see. She never raises her voice. She doesn't have to.

### The voice

A woman in her forties, Igbo-accented English, brisk and dry; funnier than she lets on. Never raises her voice. "Page forty."

### What is this game?

It's a game you play in a web browser, about surviving five years at a Nigerian university. Please don't expect GTA. Nobody steals a car. The most dangerous vehicle in it is a three-wheeled keke taxi with a married couple arguing in the back, and the nearest thing to a boss fight is a queue.

You play a brand-new student, the Fresher. You register (four offices, four queues, four passport photographs), go broke by week three, live on garri, make friends for life, sit exams by torchlight because the power has gone again, and one day you graduate. Under the jokes there is a mystery: why the lights keep going off, and why a quiet old man has spent forty years in a garden keeping one of them on. Nobody gets hurt. The cure for the monster is company.

The university and the town are made up. The voices are friends and family, recording on their phones. Thank you for being one of them.

# How to record

**What you've got.** **70 lines**, about **574 words**. That's 18 mission lines and 52 common lines. Read straight through, it's **about 7 minutes** of talking. Nobody should do it in one sitting. A few sections a day is perfect.

**Change any word so it sounds like you.** Honestly. If a line doesn't sound right in your mouth, say it your way. If you think of a funnier one, record that as well. You are the expert on how Dr. Okonkwo talks.

#### Getting set up

- **A phone is fine.** Use its voice recorder app on the best quality setting, in aeroplane mode so nothing pings.
- **Pick a quiet, soft room.** A wardrobe full of clothes is the best studio in any house. Switch off fans and the TV.
- **Hold the phone about a hand-span from your mouth,** slightly to one side, and keep it there.

#### Reading the page

- **The big black words are your line.** Say only those.
- **The small orange words above a line are how to say it,** and sometimes who you are talking to. Don't read them out.
- **Small grey words are what somebody else says just before you,** so you know the moment. Don't read them out.
- **Words in [square brackets] are the English meaning.** Don't say them. If the Hausa, Pidgin or any other language is wrong, fix it. You know better than I do.
- **(name)** is where the game drops in the player's name: leave a short pause. **Bature/Firdausi** is the player's roommate: say the line once with each name.
- **Three dots (...) mean a beat of silence.**

#### Recording

- **One recording per coloured bar.** Start recording, read that bar's lines in order, stop. Then the next bar.
- **Leave about two seconds of silence between lines.** That's where I cut. No need to say the line numbers.
- **If you fluff a line,** pause and say it again. Don't stop the recording.
- **Got the energy?** Say a line twice, two different ways, and I'll pick the best one.
- **Shouting lines last,** and drink water.

#### Sending it

- Name each file after its coloured bar, like this: *Dr. Okonkwo - Mission 1*.
- Put everything in one folder and send me a Google Drive or WeTransfer link, with a photo of the signed last page. There is no deadline.

# Common lines

These are the short lines Dr. Okonkwo says around campus, outside the story: when the player walks up, bumps into you, hands you a present, or just passes by. The game picks one each time, so they never play in a row. Say each as if someone has just walked past.

::: Common 1 · Saying hello | The player has just walked up.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Good morning. You look like someone who read. Prove me right.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Ah. One of mine. Walk faster, you're late for something.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | Kedu? [How are you?] Don't answer. Page forty.
:: 4 | amused | You again. The universe is consistent, at least.

::: Common 2 · Saying goodbye
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Go. And close that door like an engineer. Gently.
:: 2 | your normal voice | Good. Off you go. Read something.

::: Common 3 · Thank-yous and sorries
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Thank you. I'll pretend I'm not surprised.
:: 2 | warm | Thank you. That was… adequate. I mean it kindly.

::: Common 4 · Being nice | Compliments. Mean them.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Clean working. Who are you and what have you done?

::: Common 5 · Teasing | Friendly. The joke is never unkind.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Your diagram has a current going nowhere. Like your weekend.
:: 2 | amused | You wrote 'approximately infinity'. Bold. Wrong, but bold.
:: 3 | dry, deadpan | Theatre Yamma is the other one. You'll learn. Everyone does.

::: Common 6 · Quick answers | The player asks something and gets one of these back.
:: 1 | yes | Yes. Briefly.
:: 2 | no, dry, deadpan | No. But I admire the attempt.
:: 3 | confused, dry, deadpan | Start again. Slowly. Like the question is a resistor.

::: Common 7 · Jokes and questions
:: 1 | firm | Units. Where are your units? Did they travel?
:: 2 | dry, deadpan | Have you read page forty? Don't lie. I can hear lying.

::: Common 8 · When something goes wrong | Bumps, falls, power cuts, rain and heat. NEPA is what everybody calls the power company, and so every power cut.
:: 1 | bumped into, firm | Eyes up. A circuit can't be read from the floor.
:: 2 | the lights go out, dry, deadpan | There goes the light. Note the time. It's data.
:: 3 | the lights go out | Everybody stay seated. Nobody's notes are worth tripping for.
:: 4 | the lights come back, dry, deadpan | Light. Stop cheering, it's not your doing. Yet.
:: 5 | a hot day, dry, deadpan | This room is a thermal experiment and we are the samples.

::: Common 9 · Getting a present | The player hands over a present. Some go down better than others.
:: 1 | likes the present, warm | A good pen. You noticed mine was dying. Observant. Good.
:: 2 | not keen on the present, dry, deadpan | Thank you. It will live in my drawer, with its cousins.
:: 3 | loves the present, warm | A proper multimeter. Don't tell anyone I smiled.

::: Common 10 · Talking to yourself | Said to nobody in particular as the player walks past.
:: 1 | dry, deadpan | Forty scripts. Thirty-nine say 'as shown above'. Above what?
:: 2 | bored, flat | Who keeps borrowing my chalk? I count my chalk.
:: 3 | amused | Someone drew a smiley in Ohm's law. It's quite good.

::: Common 11 · Year 1: arriving | Everything is new. Queues, friends, the first power cuts.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Every September someone sits in Yamma for my Gabas class.
:: 2 | to yourself | Freshers. They write so much. Then they learn to write less.
:: 3 | teasing, dry, deadpan | You found the right theatre. Growth.
:: 4 | exam week, firm | One lamp, four students. Share the light and the formulas.

::: Common 12 · Year 2: the mall | A shiny new mall has opened, and the lights keep going off.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | A phone battery exploded in my class. I'm adding it to the exam.
:: 2 | to yourself | That mall has more light than my lab. Note it. It's data.
:: 3 | being nice, warm | Your tutorial last week. Good. I'm not saying it twice.

::: Common 13 · Year 3: the strike | The lecturers are on strike. No lectures, an empty campus, no money.
:: 1 | the strike, sad, low | Empty theatre. I lectured the chairs. They took notes.
:: 2 | the strike | You're still reading during the strike. Quietly: good.
:: 3 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | Meetings about meetings. Then a meeting to plan the meetings.

::: Common 14 · Year 4: fame | Viral videos, an election, and work placements.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | A video says I'm strict. Only 'strict'? I'm insulted.
:: 2 | to yourself | Pick your project early. Late picks pick you.
:: 3 | a question, firm | Have you a supervisor yet? Don't look at me like that. …Fine.

::: Common 15 · Year 5: leaving | Final year. Projects, job offers, and everyone counting the days.
:: 1 | to yourself, dry, deadpan | My supervisee. Your margins are wide. Your chapters are not.
:: 2 | to yourself, warm | Chapter three is good. I wrote 'good'. In ink. Frame it.
:: 3 | waiting for the player, firm | Defence in four weeks. Walk like it.
:: 4 | looking back, dry, deadpan, after “Project Defence” | I was in Yamma once too. Still not writing that down.

::: Common 16 · Special days: term time | Days on the campus calendar. Allowance day is when money from home arrives.
:: 1 | freshers' week, dry, deadpan | New faces. Same question: 'Ma, is this Gabas?'
:: 2 | exam week, firm | Read the question twice. Answer it once.
:: 3 | exam week, dry, deadpan | No, page forty is not coming out. Probably.
:: 4 | results day | Results are a reading, not a verdict. Adjust and continue.

::: Common 17 · Special days: the big ones | Save any shouting for last.
:: 1 | cup final day, amused | Engineering won? I'll allow one song. One.
:: 2 | graduation day, warm | Gowns. I pretend I don't cry. Watch me pretend.

::: Common 18 · Special days: seasons and holidays | Harmattan is the dry, dusty season.
:: 1 | harmattan, dry, deadpan | Harmattan. Static on every doorknob. Free practical.
